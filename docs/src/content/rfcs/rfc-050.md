## Context

An application and the decision on it can be recorded at one moment. The rest of a zorgtoeslag case cannot. After the application come a voorschot (Awir 16), an installment every month (Awir 22), a voorschot for each following year (Awir 15 lid 5, 16 lid 2), the aanslag of the Belastingdienst, the toekenning after it (Awir 19), and the settlement: a nabetaling (Awir 24) or a terugvordering (Awir 26, 26a, 28). Several of these depend on facts recorded by another body, and the money moves through a bank.

To record that process the cell needs rules for time that RFC-022 does not give: what a moment is, when a decision is due, how an execution that recurs every month arises, which version of the law a decision applies, and what happens to a message between two cells. The design note `packages/cel/ONTWERP-proces-zorgtoeslag.md` (in Dutch) works these out for the zorgtoeslag. This RFC records the generic rules, with the source of each: the law, an RFC, or our own choice.

## Decision

### 1. One clock, forward only

A runtime has one clock. In the demo it is the reference date, and every submission, decision, announcement and gram is recorded on that day; the wall clock gives only the time of day. Before this, the Awb stages used the wall clock and the grams the reference date: two time axes next to each other.

The clock moves forward only.

- **A fact that has yet to happen is not a fact.** The cell refuses to execute a day after the current one. The voorschot records its amount and its moment; it does not record the installments still to come.
- **A chronicle refuses a gram recorded before its last one.** Going back would make the chronicle say something it did not know then. The way back is to start again.
- **A preview is not a fact.** The cell executes the law without recording (`preview_execution`, `preview_decision`) so a runtime can show what the law gives as the next moment. Nothing is recorded until the clock reaches it.

Moving the clock several days at once goes through every moment on the way. An installment of a skipped day holds from that day and reads the case as it held then.

Reading a chronicle "at a moment" means two things that may differ: what legally holds by then (`effective_at` on or before the moment) and in what order. The cell orders by `effective_at`, then by the moment it recorded the gram, then in recording order ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) §7).

### 2. An execution is a rule per moment

Awir 22 says the voorschot is "uitbetaald in 12 termijnen", the first in December before the berekeningsjaar "en elke volgende termijn telkens een maand later" (lid 1), or as many as remain after the month of the dagtekening (lid 2), with the months already passed paid at once (lid 4). It does not speak of a list of payments. It says per month whether an installment falls.

So Awir 22 is modeled as a rule per month: given the voorschot, its dagtekening and a month, it answers whether an installment falls in that month. Each time the clock reaches a month the cell asks the law, and only an installment that is executed becomes a gram. The amount of an installment is not in Awir 22 at all; a fictitious policy of the Dienst Toeslagen gives it, marked as such.

The policy article that records an execution says so in its explicit block ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049) §5):

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `executed_on: {parameter, once_per: month, day}` | the parameter the day goes into; at most once per month per case and period; on that day of the month, or on the first day the execution may arise if that is later |
| `record_when` | the boolean output that says whether a gram arises; the law decides when a gram arises, not the cell |
| `until: {stage}` | no gram arises once the case has a gram of that stage (error kind `ended`); what follows from the end is derived, not recorded |
| `refers_to` (required) | the decision the execution executes, by stage or by article |

`Cell::due_executions` gives the days an execution may arise, from `executed_on` and `until`. Which day of the month is the choice of whoever executes the law (`day: 1` in the fictitious policy), not of the cell and not of the demo.

### 3. When a decision is due

A stream may name, per decision event, an article that gives the day the holder takes the decision (`decided_on`). Its one date output is that day.

- **A day is when the decision is due. No day is not yet due.** A `null`, or a day that rests on a fact that is not there yet, means the decision is not due. The cell refuses the decision before its day and without a day. A preview is not refused: it shows what the law would decide.
- **The decision that answers the application, for the period it asks for, is the exception.** Whoever answers the application (a caseworker, the case lifecycle) takes it when they do. That is the decision at the first decision stage after the application in its procedure: in the Awir, the voorschot, not the toekenning, although Zorgtoeslagwet 2 establishes both.
- **The first period with a day is due.** `Cell::due_decision` looks at every period of the case without that decision, from the one applied for, and gives the first the holder gives a day for. A period without a day (nothing to recover, no inkomensgegeven yet) does not hold up a later one.

The days in the zorgtoeslag case come from fictitious policies, marked as such: the voorschot for a following year on 1 November before it, the toekenning on the day of the aanslag once the inkomensgegeven is there, the terugvordering on the day of the toekenning when it leaves something to recover after Awir 26a.

### 4. A decision concerns one period, and applies that period's law

A decision concerns one period, and the cell gives it: the first decision of an event concerns the period the application asks for (its parameter with `rol: TIJDVAK`, [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)), the next one the period after it. Awir 15 lid 5 makes this law for the toeslagen: "Een aanvraag wordt geacht mede te zijn gedaan voor op het berekeningsjaar volgende berekeningsjaren." The cell refuses a period before the one applied for.

A decision applies the version of the law in force on the first day of its period, whatever the day it is taken: a voorschot for 2026 decided in November 2025 computes with the law of 2026, and its gram names the version (`regulation_valid_from`). That is what `unit: year` means, and not an assumption of the cell: a law that means another day needs another unit. The Awb periods of the decision itself run from the day it is taken.

Everything that hangs off a decision runs per period: references, `once_per`, `until` and the due days. December 2025 then pays the last installment of 2025 next to the first of 2026, and the toekenning over 2025 ends only the installments of 2025. A case is read for a period: the cell passes the period to a policy that reads the chronicle, and a value read for the toekenning over 2025 counts only what was paid on 2025.

### 5. Decisions nobody applies for

A beschikking without `decides_on` is taken ex officio ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049) §2), such as the aanslag the inspecteur sets under AWR 11. It refers to no gram. Two things a decision on an application takes from the application must come from elsewhere.

- **The period.** Schema v0.8.0 allows the role `TIJDVAK` only with `waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE`, a period the applicant chooses. Nobody applies for an aanslag, so the stream names the parameter that gives the period (`period: {parameter: belastingjaar, unit: year}`). This is our own choice and a stopgap. The cell refuses it on a decision whose law names a period, and on a decision taken on a submission.
- **Whom it concerns.** The stream names the parameters that identify the subject (`subject: [bsn]`). The cell takes such a decision once per period per subject: two decisions with the same subject and the same period are the same decision, whatever else they were given.

`Cell::due_ex_officio` gives, per subject, the first period without such a decision that the holder gives a day for. It asks every period from the first decision about that subject (before any, from the period before the current one: our own choice) through the current one, so a period passed over while a later one was set is asked again. A stream may fix the first period (`first_period`). A range of more than 50 periods is an error rather than an empty answer.

### 6. Messages between cells

The law does not say who the bank is or how a message travels, so the transport is the runtime's. In the demo, channels in its configuration say which gram of which cell goes to which article of which cell, which field fills which parameter, and which reference the answer gets. The law and the cell name no channel.

A cell receives a message by executing the receiving article once, with the message as its parameters. An execution with `record_when` and without `executed_on` arises on receipt: the cell records each event the article establishes whose `record_when` is true. A receipt may read a policy of its holder, executed with the message as its parameters, as the bank reads the balance of an account before a debit.

A message may be offered more than once, so a cell must recognize it.

- **One answer per message.** A receipt names a required `refers_to` to the gram it answers, or `identified_by`, a required parameter whose value identifies the message (the betaalkenmerk). The cell refuses a second answer to the same gram, or a second message with the same value, with the error kind `answered`. A receipt with neither is refused when the cell derives its shape, because the cell could not recognize a message delivered twice.
- **Not for this article.** An article that does not answer the gram a message refers to refuses it with the error kind `not_addressed`. The bank's answer to a nabetaling is offered both to the article for installments and to the one for nabetalingen; the first refuses it as not addressed. A sender counts neither `answered` nor `not_addressed` as a failure; a message no article takes stays undelivered.
- **The moment of a message.** A received message may not hold after the moment it is recorded, nor before the gram it refers to.

### 7. A refusal is a decision

A decision that grants nothing is still a decision (Awb 1:3). A following decision whose outcome is a refusal is recorded with that outcome, so its period is decided and the next one follows. Without a gram, the cell would offer the same period again, and later periods and the terugvordering would never come.

The demo treats a first decision that a caseworker refuses differently: it ends the case and is not recorded. That is the demo's case lifecycle, not a rule of the cell.

## Why

### Benefits

- Time is a parameter of the law, not of the screen. What the demo shows as "the next moment" is what the law gives; the demo orders dates the cell returns and computes none.
- Executions and due days follow the text: Awir 22 per month, Awir 15 lid 5 per year. A future installment is not recorded and so cannot be wrong when the voorschot changes.
- Messages can be delivered again without doing anything twice, which a transport that may fail needs.

### Tradeoffs

- A rule per month means the cell asks the law each month. A runtime that runs many cases asks often.
- `decided_on` makes the day of a decision depend on a policy article. Where the holder has none, the article is fictitious, and the demo's timing is ours.
- The period and the subject of a decision ex officio stand in the stream, outside the law, until the schema has a place for them.
- The order of reading (by `effective_at`) means a fact recorded late about an earlier moment can change what an earlier reading would have shown, read at the same moment later. That is intended: the chronicle records what was known when, and both moments are kept.

### Alternatives Considered

**The voorschot records its installments as a list** (the earlier proof of concept, an obligation block with amount and rhythm). Rejected: a future installment is not a fact, Awir 22 speaks of no list, and a revised voorschot would leave a list that is wrong.

**The demo computes the next moment from its own calendar.** Rejected: the dates then come from the demo, not from the law, and a second law (Awir 24, 28) needs a second calendar.

**`decided_on` without a day means now.** Rejected: an unknown day (the aanslag is not there yet) would then make a decision due that the law does not yet allow.

**The law of the day the decision is taken.** Rejected: a voorschot decided in November 2025 for 2026 would compute with the law of 2025.

**The transport in the law or the policy.** Rejected: no text says who the bank is or how a message reaches it.

## Implementation in this pull request

All of it is built, in `packages/cel` (`Cell::decide`, `execute`, `receive`, `due_executions`, `due_decision`, `due_ex_officio`, `preview_decision`, `preview_execution`, the chronicle's order of time) and in `frontend-demo` (the one clock, "To the next moment", the channels and the outbox). The zorgtoeslag case runs through it from the application to the terugvordering, in `packages/cel/tests` and in the demo.

## Open Questions

1. **Announcement and objection of later decisions.** The voorschot for a following year, the toekenning and the terugvordering have no announcement stage of their own in the demo's case lifecycle.
2. **The moment of a late answer.** A message that arrives only when it is offered again holds from its arrival, not from the moment of the order it answers.
3. **The period of a decision ex officio in the law** (§5): a role for a period nobody applies for.
4. **Awir 19 lid 2.** Without an aanslag there is no toekenning in the demo; the latest date of 31 December of the following year and the inkomensgegeven without an aanslag are not modeled.
5. **Fixed dates of a decision still to come.** The demo finds the dates that do not move with the decision day by comparing two previews in different months. That is a heuristic, not a rule of the law; the law should mark such a date itself.
6. **Entitlement per calendar month** (Zorgtoeslagwet 2 lid 5), revision of a voorschot after a change (Awir 16 lid 5, 17), and interest and collection (Awir 27 to 29) are not modeled.
