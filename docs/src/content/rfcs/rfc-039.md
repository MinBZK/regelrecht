## Context

An engine records how it reached a result, as a tree of steps. A step cannot say which article it came from: there is no article, no law, no citation, no link to the statute, and no address by which anything can name a step.

So consumers reconstruct legal meaning out of the line the trace writes for a terminal:

- `frontend-demo/src/data/lineage.js:17` runs `/^Resolving from SOURCE ([^:]+):/` over it to find which organization supplied a value, and builds the citizen-facing "Gebruikte gegevens" tree from that.
- `frontend/src/lib/traceEdges.js` tracks which law a step is in by looking for a `#` in the step's name, and reads an article out of a `"{law_id} ({output})"` string.
- `frontend/src/components/graph/TraceStepDetail.vue:65` carries a comment saying it cannot render units, because nothing on a step declares one.

`packages/tui/src/views/trace.rs` is the fourth signal. It renders the tree itself rather than using the engine's text rendering, and it already addresses a step by its index chain, `path: Vec<usize>`, for want of an address on the step.

The material is in scope at evaluation time and never joined. An article carries its number, its verbatim text and a link to wetten.overheid.nl. The law schema allows a `legal_basis` reference on a field, an action and every operation. An addressing scheme exists in `regelrecht://{law_id}/{output}#{field}`.

No RFC owns any of this. [RFC-013](/rfcs/rfc-013) defines the Execution Receipt and leaves the trace as `"trace": { }` in its envelope example. Four roadmap work packages already treat the trace as a legal document: "Rechtsbescherming en de execution trace", "Informatiepositie burgers/ondernemers", "Lakmoesproef besluiten met execution trace en burgerdossier", and "Werkwijze rechterlijke macht I". None is answerable while a step cannot name its provision.

## Decision

The trace becomes a published document format, `schema/trace/v1/trace-schema.json`. What the Rust engine derives its fields from is its own business; the format is what a second engine has to produce, so that a municipality running its own engine can produce a trace a court recognizes.

The document is `{"trace_version": 1, "root": …}`, and a step carries:

| Field | What it denotes |
|---|---|
| `node_id` | Address of the step within this trace: the index chain from the root, `n`, `n0`, `n0.2`. |
| `anchor` | The innermost article the step was evaluated within, whatever the document cites. |
| `legal_basis` | The citation the document declares for the element at `yaml_path`, as written. |
| `yaml_path` | Address of the element within the law document, as written. |
| `uri` | Convenience address of the value, as a `regelrecht://` URI. |
| `source` | Where a resolved value came from: kind, provider, and the law a scoped source was registered for. |
| `type_spec` | Declared unit and precision of the result, so 209692 can be shown as an amount in euros. |

Six things that decision settles beyond the field list.

**Its own version axis.** `schema/trace/v<trace_version>/`, not a law-schema version directory. A trace shape and a law shape change for different reasons, and the note sidecar of [RFC-005](/rfcs/rfc-005) went under `schema/v0.5.2/` and `v0.5.3/` and then froze there while the law schema moved to v0.5.9. The immutability job in `ci.yml` is extended to cover the new axis, so a published trace version cannot be edited either.

**`yaml_path` uses the path language the corpus already speaks.** Dotted, and for a list item keyed on its `output`, `name` or `number` rather than its index: `articles.2.machine_readable.execution.actions.hoogte_zorgtoeslag.value.values.1`. `YamlNode` stamps that language on every rendered node as `data-path`, and `expanded_paths` in `demo-config.yaml` keys on it to decide which lines of a law open by default. So a step's address is already an `expanded_paths` entry, and it reads: `voldoet_aan_voorwaarden.value.conditions.0` says something, `/execution/actions/0/value/conditions/0` does not. It also addresses one branch of a nested operation, which has no name for a URI to use.

The path names the element **as written**. An action carrying its operation inline is `actions.X.values.1`; one nesting it under `value` is `actions.X.value.values.1`. A path that does not select the element it names is worse than no path, so an engine that normalizes those two forms internally must either keep the distinction or omit the field.

**`uri` is a convenience.** `anchor.law_id` and `yaml_path` are the address; the URI is nicer to read where it can be written, and often it cannot. The scheme puts the law id before a `/`, so it cannot express a law whose id contains one, and 30 of the 79 law ids in `corpus/demo` do. It also has nothing to put in the output segment for a step naming an input or an open term. It is then absent, and absence does not mean the step denotes no value.

**`anchor` and `legal_basis` stay apart.** One is where the engine was, the other is what the document claims. Collapsing them would hide a citation hung on the wrong provision. `anchor` is reported for every step inside an article, including a bare arithmetic operation; `legal_basis` is absent when that element cites nothing, which is why `explanation` occurs on a `legal_basis` and never on an `anchor`.

**`message` is presentational.** It exists to be read by a person, its wording is not a contract, it may be in any language, and parsing it is a defect. Every fact it states is in a field. Three consumers parse it today, and each is a consequence of there being nothing else to read.

**Trace depth stays all or nothing.** The claim in [RFC-013](/rfcs/rfc-013) that it is configurable is withdrawn. Folding a subtree is a presentation decision, and whatever is doing the presenting knows how much room it has and who is reading.

## Why

### Benefits

A step can name its provision, so a trace can be read beside the statute. That is what Awb article 3:46 asks of a motivation and what AERIUS I (ECLI:NL:RVS:2017:1259) means by *inzichtelijk en controleerbaar*. The four roadmap work packages become answerable.

Four consumers get shorter. The regular expression goes; `anchor.law_id` retires both string parses in `traceEdges.js`; units become showable; and the terminal rendering stops being an interface.

A format rather than an engine detail is what [RFC-006](/rfcs/rfc-006) and RFC-013 argue for elsewhere: the schema is the specification and an engine is one implementation.

A recorded trace becomes a fixture, so a renderer can be built and tested against one with no engine in the loop.

### Tradeoffs

**The envelope is a breaking change**, at the WebAssembly boundary as much as on disk: `executeWithTrace` and `executeMultipleWithTrace` hand back the root step directly today, and a dozen call sites across seven files in the demo and the editor read it that way, as do `concepts/traceability` and `components/engine` in these docs. All of them will read `root` out of a document instead. That is deliberate, because a trace that travels needs its version inside it, and the same work converts those consumers anyway. Version 1 is the first version, not a claim about what came before. Both docs pages need rewriting with it.

The document grows, and by how much is not yet known: a full anchor with a wetten.overheid.nl link and a Juriconnect reference runs to about 200 bytes, there are two anchors per step, and a step's current JSON is on the order of 150. So a densely cited law costs several times its present size and a law citing nothing costs almost nothing. Nothing populates an anchor yet, so this is arithmetic rather than measurement. Traces are produced on request and archived by hash, so the cost lands at archiving rather than in the hot path.

Anchors will be sparse. Under `corpus/regulation/` the reference object appears on no element at all: the nine `legal_basis` occurrences there are four law-level authority lists and five plain strings on open terms. The element-level form is used 346 times, all under `corpus/demo/`, where `explanation` also occurs 344 times and in `corpus/regulation/` never. A step whose element cites nothing reports nothing rather than borrowing the enclosing article's citation, which leaves the corpus gap visible.

`node_id` addresses a position, not an identity. Two evaluations whose trees differ in shape address the same work differently: a memoized cross-law result is one childless step where a cache miss is a whole subtree, so asking for one output and asking for two can number the same evaluation differently. Adding a step to the instrumentation renumbers everything below it, which implementing this RFC will itself do. Stability holds within a trace document, which is what a fixture pins and a URL fragment names.

A trace reads back as JSON, not as engine values. `Value` serializes a whole decimal as an integer, so a round trip is faithful as a document and lossy as a value tree. Fixing that would change the shape every consumer already reads and belongs to its own RFC.

### Alternatives Considered

**Keep reading the message.** Rejected: three consumers already do it, one is broken on units, and the string they parse exists to be read by a person, so every rephrasing is a silent break in a citizen-facing view.

**A separate explanation document.** Rejected: two accounts of one evaluation have to be kept in agreement, and the second is derived from the same missing information as the first. The trace has the right shape and is missing its anchors.

**An event stream instead of a tree.** Rejected: every consumer wants the tree, the TUI included, so this moves the same work into four places to save it in none.

**Anchors only in the receipt.** Rejected: the receipt does not reach the browser, which is where a citizen reads an explanation, and per-output provenance is the wrong granularity anyway. It names the article that produced an output, not the provision behind each step.

**Put `legal_basis` on the law model.** Rejected here, because this RFC decides a document format and not what a model parses. The citation belongs to the element at `yaml_path`, so an engine can report it from the document it loaded without modelling it.

**Derive a unit for an intermediate result.** Deferred. `units::infer_operation_unit` already does this for the engine's own type checking, but it works from one article's symbols, so a cross-law operand falls outside it, and it has no notion of precision. Version 1 reports a declared `type_spec` only.

### Implementation Notes

For the Rust engine specifically, the article and the law are in hand wherever a trace step is pushed inside article evaluation, so attaching an anchor is threading context rather than finding information. The operation and resolve steps are the harder half: they are pushed from the operation evaluator and the value resolver, which carry no position and no article today, and it is those steps that `yaml_path` needs most.

The existing box-drawing rendering keeps its current output. It is what `trace_text` carries across the WebAssembly boundary, and `packages/engine/tests/expected_zorgtoeslag_trace.txt` pins it, which is the guard against the text drifting while the structure changes underneath it. Note that neither the CLI nor the TUI renders it: the TUI walks the tree itself, which is why it had to invent an address.

This RFC does not expose the receipt across the WebAssembly boundary, add an HTTP execution endpoint, correlate traces across organizations (the `trace_id` of [RFC-009](/rfcs/rfc-009)), or sign a trace. Staged execution under [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) returns outputs and pending inputs with no trace at all, so a decision that unfolds over several stages cannot yet be explained as a whole. That gap deserves an issue of its own: a staged *beschikking* is where a citizen most needs an explanation.
