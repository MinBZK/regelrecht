## Context

A specific law says that someone may apply for a decision and what the application contains. The Algemene wet bestuursrecht (Awb) adds what every application contains, and what happens once it has been received. Take the zorgtoeslag:

- Awir 15 lid 1: a tegemoetkoming is applied for at the Dienst Toeslagen, for a berekeningsjaar; lid 2 asks the partner to sign too.
- Awb 4:2 lid 1: "De aanvraag wordt ondertekend en bevat ten minste: a. de naam en het adres van de aanvrager; b. de dagtekening; c. een aanduiding van de beschikking die wordt gevraagd."
- Awb 4:13 lid 1: the decision is due within the statutory or a reasonable period "na ontvangst van de aanvraag".

Awir 15 does not mention Awb 4:2, and it does not need to. Awb 1:3 lid 3 defines an application as "een verzoek van een belanghebbende, een besluit te nemen", and afdeling 4.1.1 applies to every application for a *beschikking* (4:1). The Awb applies itself. Together the two laws give the application its shape: its fields, the moment that counts, the obligations that follow.

The application is a legal act of its own. It binds no one and is no decision. Still the Awb attaches consequences to it that exist before and without any decision: the content (4:1 to 4:4), the invitation to complete it (4:5), the decision period that runs from receipt (4:13 to 4:15), the penalty for deciding too late (4:17) and the appeal against not deciding at all (6:2). An application on which nobody ever decides still exists; 4:17 and 6:2 are there for exactly that case.

### What the model did before

[RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) lets an article hook onto the execution of another article, filtered on what that article `produces`: its `legal_character`, its `decision_type` and, per [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008), the stage. RFC-008 models the application as the first stage, `AANVRAAG`, of the lifecycle of a *beschikking*, and places the decision period in "a hook at the AANVRAAG stage".

That puts the application inside the decision, and three things follow.

1. A hook on `AANVRAAG` fires only when the engine executes the decision from that stage. No caller does: a decision is executed at `BESLUIT` and later. Hooks for Awb 4:2 and 4:13 placed there never run.
2. What the application contains depends on the decision article. Derived from a run of Zorgtoeslagwet 2, the application carries only what article 2 reads. The partner's signature that Awir 15 lid 2 asks for falls out, because no rule of article 2 reads it.
3. A request for a regulation of general scope has no `AANVRAAG` stage, which is right, but only by accident of the procedure table.

A process runtime needs the shape of the application to record it. In the chronolex proof of concept (branch `poc/chronolex`) the Awb core of an application first lived in five places (a JSON schema and four stream definitions), and the next build composed it in the cell runtime from blocks with `extends: {stage: AANVRAAG}`. The owner of this work rejected that on 30 September 2026: the combination of the specific law and the Awb has to come from executing the law.

## Decision

The application becomes something an article can produce, and something a hook can apply to. The engine combines the specific law and the Awb by executing the article that establishes the application, with the hooks that apply to it. The shape of the application is what that execution declares.

### 1. An article says that it establishes an application

`produces` gets an optional block `submission`, next to the legal character of the article's own outputs:

```yaml
# Awir 15
- number: '15'
  machine_readable:
    execution:
      produces:
        submission:
          kind: AANVRAAG            # lid 1: a tegemoetkoming is applied for
        legal_character: TOETS
        decision_type: GEEN_BESLUIT
      parameters:
        - name: aangevraagd_berekeningsjaar
          type: number
          origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: 'algemene_wet_inkomensafhankelijke_regelingen#15 lid 1', rol: TIJDVAK}
        - ...
```

`kind` names what the belanghebbende submits. This RFC defines one value, `AANVRAAG` (Awb 1:3 lid 3). The same block would carry `BEZWAARSCHRIFT` (Awb 6:4, 6:5) and `BEROEPSCHRIFT` later, which the Awb treats the same way: a document with a required content and a period that runs from submission. One block with a `kind` keeps one mechanism, with one filter and one index in the engine. The key is English like the rest of the structure; the value is the legal term, as `BESCHIKKING` is.

Awir 15 does not say which decision follows, and the `submission` block does not either. The link sits in the decision article, which names the application it decides on:

```yaml
# Zorgtoeslagwet 2
- number: '2'
  machine_readable:
    execution:
      produces:
        legal_character: BESCHIKKING
        decision_type: TOEKENNING
        procedure_id: tegemoetkoming
        decides_on:
          - algemene_wet_inkomensafhankelijke_regelingen#15
```

`decides_on` names the articles that establish the submission the decision is taken on. The engine keeps a reverse index from each such article to the legal characters of the decisions taken on it. The application of Awir 15 is an application for a *beschikking* because a *beschikking* is taken on it.

A `submission` block does not change the article's own outputs. It says, in addition, that the article is where the application comes into being.

### 2. A hook can apply to an application

Three filter fields join the ones of RFC-007:

| Filter field | Matches against |
|---|---|
| `submission` | `produces.submission.kind` of the executing article |
| `decided_by` | the legal character of every decision that `decides_on` the executing article |
| `established_by` | the executing article itself, `<regulation>#<article>` |

```yaml
# Awb 4:2
hooks:
  - hook_point: pre_actions
    applies_to:
      submission: AANVRAAG
      decided_by: BESCHIKKING
```

A hook with `submission` does not match an article that only produces a decision, and a hook on a decision does not match an article that only establishes an application. A hook names `legal_character` or `submission`, not both, and a hook on a submission takes no `stage` or `decision_type`. The filters are AND-combined, as in RFC-007. A submission on which decisions of more than one legal character are taken matches a hook with `decided_by` only if all of them match; the engine does not guess which Awb regime applies. A submission on which no modeled decision is taken matches no hook with `decided_by`.

`established_by` is for policy that works out the application of one law. The fictitious policy of the Dienst Toeslagen asks the account number the payments go to, on the application of Awir 15 only:

```yaml
hooks:
  - hook_point: pre_actions
    applies_to:
      submission: AANVRAAG
      established_by: algemene_wet_inkomensafhankelijke_regelingen#15
```

The hooks index of RFC-007 is keyed on `(hook_point, legal_character)`. It gets a second key, `(hook_point, submission)`.

### 3. The shape of an application is what its execution declares

Executing an article with a `submission` block fires the hooks that apply to it, as executing a decision fires its hooks. Every parameter the article and the firing hooks declare, with its origin ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)), is part of the application.

`execute_stage` on an article with a `submission` block collects those parameters. While a required one is not supplied, it does not fail on the first; it yields with the missing names and the whole model: the articles in firing order, the decisions taken on it, and every parameter with the article that asks it, as declared, and whether it was supplied. Executed with nothing, an application yields its own shape. Complete, the same call computes, and the result carries the model too. An optional parameter that is not supplied is unknown, as anywhere ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036)). Because the model comes from the same hook resolution the execution uses, the shape and the execution cannot disagree.

A hook runs on every execution of the establishing article, also when another article reads one of its outputs. Those calls go through ordinary evaluation, which has no yield: a hook parameter the caller does not pass must be `required: false` there, or the call fails.

A runtime that records applications takes the fields from this model. The cell in `packages/cel` executes Awir 15 with an empty application and records as fields the parameters with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL` ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049)). Its stream names only the event and the establishing article.

### 4. What the Awb computes on an application

A `post_actions` hook on an application computes what follows from it, the way Awb 6:8 computes the objection period on a notice. Awb 4:13 is such a hook. In this repository it carries only the moment that counts (`produces.moment`, lid 1: receipt; RFC-049). A specific law that sets its own period overrides an output with the lex specialis of RFC-007, as it would today.

### 5. Relation to RFC-008

The lifecycle of RFC-008 keeps its stages, including `AANVRAAG` as the name for the phase in which an application is pending. What changes is where the facts of that phase are declared. A hook that is about the application (its content, its receipt, the decision period) applies to the application with `submission`, not to the decision with `stage: AANVRAAG`. RFC-008 gets a note pointing here; its text stays as written.

RFC-008 also gains `is` on a stage (see its Later additions): a procedure of its own, such as `tegemoetkoming` of the Awir, can say that VOORSCHOT and TOEKENNING are each a `BESLUIT`, so the Awb hooks on the decision fire on both. That addition and this RFC were made for the same case.

### 6. Schema

Law schema v0.8.0 has `produces.submission` (`kind`), `produces.decides_on` and the three filter fields. Laws without a `submission` block are unaffected.

## Why

### Benefits

- The application exists on its own, as the Awb treats it. It does not depend on a decision being modeled, executed or ever taken.
- The Awb applies itself through the hook mechanism that already exists, without the specific law naming it. Awir 15 and any other specific law get the same Awb core from the same articles.
- A change in the Awb reaches every application. If Awb 4:2 lid 1 gains a telephone number, one new version of the Awb adds the parameter, and every application carries it from the date that version applies.
- Each link sits where the text puts it: Awir 15 says an application exists, Zorgtoeslagwet 2 that a decision is taken on it, the Awb what every application for a *beschikking* contains.

### Tradeoffs

- A schema change, with a new filter key in the engine's hooks index. A corpus that pins an older schema does not know the fields until it upgrades.
- An article can now carry two roles: Awir 15 establishes the application and has outputs of its own. A reader has to learn that `submission` and `legal_character` describe different things.
- The Awb applies to an application only once the decision on it is modeled. For an application without a modeled decision article the engine cannot tell whether afdeling 4.1.1 applies.
- More hooks fire on more executions. Each run of Awir 15 now fires Awb 4:2 and 4:13.

### Alternatives Considered

**The application as the first stage of the decision** (RFC-008 as it stands). Rejected for the reasons in the context: the hooks never fire, the application inherits only what the decision reads, and an application without a decision cannot exist.

**The specific law names the Awb** (`includes: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2` on Awir 15). Simple, but the text does not say it, every specific law has to repeat it, and a law that forgets it silently loses the Awb fields.

**Composition in the consumer.** The proof-of-concept runtime composed the application from blocks with `extends: {stage: AANVRAAG}`. It removed five copies of the Awb core, but it was a second resolution of the same hooks outside the engine. Rejected on 30 September 2026.

**The application names the decision it asks for** (`requests:` on the application article). The text of the application article does not say that; the decision article does, from the other side.

**`legal_character: AANVRAAG`.** Smaller, since hooks already filter on the legal character. But an application is not a decision, and the legal character is the vocabulary of decisions (Awb 1:3). It would also leave no room for the article's own outputs.

## Implementation in this pull request

Built in `packages/law-model` and `packages/engine` (PR #1679, carried into PR #1689): `produces.submission`, `produces.decides_on`, the three filter fields, the second index key, and the submission model on `execute_stage`. Schema v0.8.0 declares them. In the corpus, Awir 15 establishes the application, Zorgtoeslagwet 2 decides on it, and Awb 4:2 and 4:13 hook onto it. Not built: a decision period computed by Awb 4:13 (§4), and the other kinds of §1.

## Open Questions

1. **Awb 2:8 and 2:10.** Electronic messages and the notice that one is waiting apply to every message between the body and a person, not only to an application. A broader filter may fit better.
2. **Lex specialis for a field.** A specific law that asks less than Awb 4:2 ("in afwijking van artikel 4:2") needs an override on a parameter; RFC-007 overrides outputs only. A specific law that asks the same datum in its own words uses `specifies` ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049)).
3. **Which version shapes an application.** The Awb version on the day of receipt, or on the day of recording? They differ only for a paper application entered later, across a change of the Awb.
4. **The position paper.** A chronolexogram composed from the establishing article and the Awb articles that apply to it: is that one fact with several legal bases, as this RFC assumes, or should the paper's single source article be the establishing one only?
