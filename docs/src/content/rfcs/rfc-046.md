## Context

A specific law says that someone may apply for a decision and what the application contains. The Algemene wet bestuursrecht (Awb) adds what every application contains, and what happens once it has been received. Take the decentralized party subsidy in the Wet op de politieke partijen (Wpp):

- Wpp 102 lid 1: "Een decentrale politieke partij kan subsidie aanvragen". Lid 2 to 5 list what the application states: the subsidy year, the statutory name, the registered designation, per representative body the seats.
- Awb 4:2 lid 1: "De aanvraag wordt ondertekend en bevat ten minste: a. de naam en het adres van de aanvrager; b. de dagtekening; c. een aanduiding van de beschikking die wordt gevraagd."
- Awb 4:13 lid 1: the decision is due within the statutory or a reasonable period "na ontvangst van de aanvraag".

Wpp 102 does not mention the Awb, and it does not need to. Awb 1:3 lid 3 defines an application as "een verzoek van een belanghebbende, een besluit te nemen", and afdeling 4.1.1 applies to every application for a *beschikking* (4:1). The Awb applies itself. Together the two laws give the application its shape: its fields, the moment that counts, the obligations that follow.

The application is a legal act of its own. It binds no one and is no decision. Still the Awb attaches consequences to it that exist before and without any decision: the content (4:1 to 4:4), the invitation to complete it (4:5), forwarding to the competent body (2:3), the decision period that runs from receipt (4:13 to 4:15), the penalty for deciding too late (4:17) and the appeal against not deciding at all (6:2). An application on which nobody ever decides still exists; 4:17 and 6:2 are there for exactly that case.

### What the model does now

[RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) lets an article hook onto the execution of another article, filtered on what that article `produces` (its `legal_character`, `decision_type` and, per [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008), the stage). RFC-008 models the application as the first stage, `AANVRAAG`, of the lifecycle of a *beschikking*, and places the decision period in "a hook at the AANVRAAG stage".

That puts the application inside the decision. Three things follow.

1. A hook on `AANVRAAG` fires only when the engine executes the decision from that stage. No caller does: a decision is executed at `BESLUIT` and later. The hooks of Awb 4:1, 4:2 and 4:13 never run.
2. What the application contains depends on the decision article. If the application is derived from a run of Wpp 107 (the decision on the subsidy), only the fields of Wpp 102 that 107 reads come along. The registration and the list of representative bodies that 102 lid 2 and 4 ask for fall out, because no rule of 107 reads them.
3. A request for a regulation of general scope has no `AANVRAAG` stage, which is right, but only by accident of the procedure table.

The chronolex proof of concept ([RFC-044](/rfcs/rfc-044), branch `poc/chronolex`, note "het gram uit de wet" of 29 September 2026) needs the shape of the application to record it as a gram. Before that note the Awb core of an application lived in five places: a JSON schema (`core_application`), the stream definitions of the NAPP case and of three test fixtures, and the extension of Awb 4:2. The first build after the note composed the application in the cell runtime from `produces.extensions.chronolex` blocks, with `extends: {stage: AANVRAAG}` on the Awb articles. That removed the five copies, but the composition happened in the runtime, outside the execution of the law. Tim de Jager rejected that on 30 September: the combination of Wpp 102 and the Awb has to come from executing the law.

## Decision

The application becomes something an article can produce, and something a hook can apply to. The engine combines the specific law and the Awb by executing the article that establishes the application, with the hooks that apply to it. The shape of the application is what that execution declares.

### 1. An article says that it establishes an application

`produces` gets an optional block `submission`, next to the legal character of the article's own outputs:

```yaml
# Wpp 102
- number: '102'
  machine_readable:
    execution:
      produces:
        legal_character: TOETS          # its outputs: complete, on time
        decision_type: GEEN_BESLUIT
        submission:
          kind: AANVRAAG                # lid 1: "kan subsidie aanvragen"
      parameters:
        - {name: subsidiejaar, ..., origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: ..., rol: TIJDVAK}}
        - {name: statutaire_naam, ..., origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: 'wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102 lid 3'}}
        - ...
```

`kind` names what the belanghebbende submits. This RFC defines one value, `AANVRAAG` (Awb 1:3 lid 3). The same block would carry `BEZWAARSCHRIFT` (Awb 6:4, 6:5) and `BEROEPSCHRIFT` later, which the Awb treats the same way: a document with a required content (6:5 asks name and address, date and a description of the decision, as 4:2 does for an application) and a period that runs from submission. One block with a `kind` keeps that one mechanism, with one filter and one index in the engine. The key is English like the rest of the structure; the value is the legal term, as `BESCHIKKING` is.

Wpp 102 does not say which decision follows, and the `submission` block does not either. The text that links the application to a decision is in the decision article. Wpp 107 lid 1 reads "De Autoriteit besluit op de aanvraag", so 107 refers to the application:

```yaml
# Wpp 107
- number: '107'
  machine_readable:
    execution:
      produces:
        legal_character: BESCHIKKING
        decision_type: TOEKENNING
        decides_on:
          - wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102   # lid 1: "besluit op de aanvraag"
```

`decides_on` names the articles that establish the submission the decision is taken on. The engine keeps a reverse index from each such article to the legal characters of the decisions taken on it. The application of Wpp 102 is an application for a *beschikking* because a *beschikking* is taken on it, which is what the law says. Awb 4:21 and following, which make the granting and the determination of a subsidy *beschikkingen*, point the same way, but the direct textual link is 107.

A `submission` block does not change the article's own outputs. Wpp 102 still computes whether the application is complete and on time. It says, in addition, that it is the article where the application comes into being.

### 2. A hook can apply to an application

Three filter fields join the ones of RFC-007:

| Filter field | Matches against |
|---|---|
| `submission` | `produces.submission.kind` of the executing article |
| `decided_by` | the legal character of every decision that `decides_on` the executing article |
| `established_by` | the executing article itself, `<regulation>#<article>` |

```yaml
# Awb 4:2
- number: '4:2'
  machine_readable:
    hooks:
      - hook_point: pre_actions
        applies_to:
          submission: AANVRAAG
          decided_by: BESCHIKKING
    execution:
      parameters:
        - {name: naam_aanvrager, type: string, required: false,
           origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: 'algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2 lid 1'}}
        - {name: adres_aanvrager, ...}
        - {name: dagtekening, type: date, ...}
        - {name: gevraagde_beschikking, ...,
           origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: ..., rol: GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT}}
        - {name: ondertekening, ...}
      output:
        - {name: aanvraag_bevat_kern_awb4_2, type: boolean}
      actions: ...
```

A hook with `submission` does not match an article that only produces a decision, and a hook on a decision does not match an article that only establishes an application. The filters are AND-combined, as in RFC-007. A submission on which decisions of more than one legal character are taken matches a hook with `decided_by` only if all of them match; the engine does not guess which Awb regime applies. A submission on which no modeled decision is taken matches no hook with `decided_by`: the corpus does not yet say what it is a submission for.

`established_by` is for policy that works out the application of one law, as the general law works out every application. The implementing policy of the NAPP authority says what the application of Wpp 102 also carries (the KvK number from the login means, the bank account, the receipt on paper):

```yaml
# uitvoering_napp art. 4
hooks:
  - hook_point: pre_actions
    applies_to:
      submission: AANVRAAG
      established_by: wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102
```

Before this filter the proof of concept joined such policy to the application by the name of the chronicle event (`extends: aanvraag_ontvangen`), which put a runtime name in the law and left the engine unaware that the policy takes part.

The hooks index of RFC-007 is keyed on `(hook_point, legal_character)`. It gets a second key, `(hook_point, submission)`, filled from hooks that name `submission`. A hook names one or the other.

### 3. The shape of an application is what its execution declares

Executing an article with a `submission` block fires the hooks that apply to it, exactly like executing a decision fires its hooks. Every parameter the article and the firing hooks declare, with its origin, is part of the application. The engine reports that as the declaration of the application:

```text
application  wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102 (AANVRAAG, decided by wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#107: BESCHIKKING)
  subsidiejaar               BELANGHEBBENDE  TIJDVAK           wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102
  statutaire_naam            BELANGHEBBENDE                    wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102 lid 3
  ...
  naam_aanvrager             BELANGHEBBENDE                    algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2 lid 1   (hook)
  dagtekening                BELANGHEBBENDE                    algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2 lid 1   (hook)
  gevraagde_beschikking      BELANGHEBBENDE  GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT  algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2 lid 1   (hook)
  ondertekening              BELANGHEBBENDE                    algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2 lid 1   (hook)
```

The declaration comes from the same hook resolution the execution uses, so the shape and the execution cannot disagree. In the proof of concept (branch `poc/chronolex`) it is the execution itself: `execute_stage` on an article with a `submission` block collects the parameters of the article and of the hooks that fire, and while a required one is not supplied it does not fail on the first, but yields with `pending_inputs` (the missing required names) and the whole model (`Submission`: the articles in firing order, the decisions taken on it, every parameter with the article that asks it, as declared, and whether it was supplied). Executed with nothing, an application yields its own shape; complete, the same call computes, and the result carries the model too. An optional parameter that is not supplied is unknown, as anywhere (RFC-036).

A hook runs on every execution of the establishing article, also when another article reads one of its outputs (the completeness test, the decision). Those calls go through the ordinary evaluation, which has no yield: a hook parameter the caller does not pass must be `required: false` there, or the call fails. A consumer that records applications (a chronolex cell, an intake form) takes the fields from it. The chronolex stream of the NAPP case then names only the establishing article and the intake:

```yaml
events:
  - name: aanvraag_ontvangen
    establishes: wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102
    intake: portaal
```

Parameters without an origin, and parameters with origin `DOSSIER`, `OORDEEL` or `REGISTER`, belong to the rules, not to what the belanghebbende submits. They are not part of the declaration. Where an implementing policy says who supplies a parameter ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) `origins`), the declaration carries the origin in force, with the policy article.

### 4. What the Awb computes on an application

A `post_actions` hook on an application computes what follows from it, the way Awb 6:8 computes the objection period on a notice. Awb 4:13 becomes such a hook:

```yaml
# Awb 4:13
- number: '4:13'
  machine_readable:
    hooks:
      - hook_point: post_actions
        applies_to: {submission: AANVRAAG, decided_by: BESCHIKKING}
    execution:
      parameters:
        - {name: datum_ontvangst_aanvraag, type: date, ...}
      output:
        - {name: uiterste_beslisdatum_awb4_13, type: date}
      actions: ...   # lid 2: eight weeks, unless a statutory period applies
```

The moment of receipt is a parameter of 4:13, not a field the specific law invents. A specific law that sets its own period (Wpp 107: before 1 July of the subsidy year) overrides the output with the lex specialis of RFC-007, as it would today.

### 5. Relation to RFC-008

The lifecycle of RFC-008 keeps its stages, including `AANVRAAG` as the name for the phase in which an application is pending. What changes is where the facts of that phase are declared. A hook that is about the application (its content, its receipt, the decision period) applies to the application with `submission`, not to the decision with `stage: AANVRAAG`. RFC-008's resolution of the decision-period question ("calculated by a hook at the AANVRAAG stage") moves to section 4 of this RFC. If this RFC is accepted, RFC-008 gets a note pointing here; its text stays as written.

### 6. Schema

The law schema gets a new minor version with `produces.submission` (`kind`), `produces.decides_on` and the three filter fields. The Rust law-model follows, and `just conformance` covers both. Laws without a `submission` block are unaffected.

## Why

### Benefits

- The application exists on its own, as the Awb treats it. It does not depend on a decision being modeled, executed, or ever taken.
- The Awb applies itself through the hook mechanism that already exists, without the specific law naming it. Wpp 102 and a fictitious allowance regulation get the same Awb core from the same articles.
- A change in the Awb reaches every application. If Awb 4:2 lid 1 gains a telephone number, one new version of the Awb adds the parameter and every application carries it from the date that version applies, without touching a stream, a form or a specific law.
- A request for a regulation of general scope gets no Awb 4:2 fields because the decision taken on it has another legal character, not because of a gap in a procedure table.
- Each link sits where the text puts it: Wpp 102 says an application exists, Wpp 107 says a decision is taken on it, the Awb says what every application for a *beschikking* contains.
- The decision period hangs on the application, where 4:13 puts it, so 4:17 and 6:2 can build on it when there is no decision.

### Tradeoffs

- A schema change, with a new filter key in the engine's hooks index. Corpus repositories that pin an older schema (the NAPP corpus uses v0.5.9) and their editor engines do not know the fields until they upgrade.
- An article can now carry two roles: Wpp 102 establishes the application and holds the completeness test. That is what the text does, but a reader has to learn that `submission` and `legal_character` describe different things.
- A hook on an application is triggered by executing the establishing article. A caller that wants the shape of an application without values asks for the declaration (section 3), which is new API surface on the engine.
- The Awb applies to an application only once the decision on it is modeled. For an application without a modeled decision article the engine cannot tell whether afdeling 4.1.1 applies, and says so.
- More hooks fire on more executions. Wpp 102 is executed in every completeness test, and each such run now fires Awb 4:1, 4:2 and 4:13.

### Alternatives considered

**The application as the first stage of the decision** (RFC-008 as it stands). Rejected for the reasons in the context: the hooks never fire, the application inherits only what the decision reads, and an application without a decision cannot exist.

**The specific law names the Awb** (`includes: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2` on Wpp 102). Simple, but the text does not say it, every specific law has to repeat it, and a law that forgets it silently loses the Awb fields.

**Composition in the consumer.** The chronolex runtime composed the application from `produces.extensions.chronolex` blocks with `extends: {stage: AANVRAAG}` (branch `poc/chronolex`, commit `27a567f3`). It works, and it removed five copies of the Awb core. But it is a second resolution of the same hooks, outside the engine, and it made the shape of an application a runtime concern instead of an outcome of the law. Rejected by Tim de Jager on 30 September 2026.

**The application names the decision it asks for** (`requests: wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#107` on Wpp 102, an earlier draft of this RFC). Wpp 102 does not say that; 107 does, from the other side. Rejected on 30 September 2026.

**A key per kind** (`application`, later `objection`). Shorter to read, but the bezwaarschrift of Awb 6:5 follows the same pattern as the application of 4:2, and a key per kind repeats the filter and the index for each.

**`legal_character: AANVRAAG`.** Smaller, since hooks already filter on the legal character. But an application is not a decision, and the legal character is the vocabulary of decisions (Awb 1:3). It would also leave no room for Wpp 102's own outputs, which are a test (`TOETS`), in the same article.

## Open questions

1. **Awb 2:8 and 2:10.** Electronic messages and the notice that one is waiting apply to every message between the body and a person, not only to an application. The proof of concept hooks them onto the application. A broader filter (every exchange with a belanghebbende) may fit better.
2. **Lex specialis for a field.** A specific law that asks less than Awb 4:2 ("in afwijking van artikel 4:2") needs an override on a parameter. RFC-007 overrides outputs only.
3. **Which version shapes an application.** The Awb version on the day of receipt, or on the day of recording? They differ only for a paper application entered later, across a change of the Awb.
4. **Wpp 71b.** The national party subsidy has its own application article. It would get the same `submission` block; the proof of concept has not done that yet.
5. **The position paper.** A chronolexogram composed from the establishing article and the Awb articles that apply to it: is that one fact with several legal bases, as this RFC assumes, or should the paper's single source article be the establishing one only?
