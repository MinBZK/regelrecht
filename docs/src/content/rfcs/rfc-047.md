## Context

In the chronolexography position paper a cell is autonomous over its own facts, and that includes its reduction: the cell decides how it reads its chronicle back into a lexostatus. [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) §4.1 follows that, and lets each cell declare which lexostatussen it exposes and how each is reduced.

The compact cell in `packages/cel` did exactly that. Its configuration held a file `lexostatuses.yaml` with two lexostatussen. `aanvraag` read the application of a case back: which grams, the latest one, and per value a derivation and a legal basis. `uitbetaald` summed what was paid on a voorschot for one berekeningsjaar. A decision event in a stream said which lexostatus it read (`reads: aanvraag`), and the cell passed each value as the parameter of the same name.

Two things were wrong with that, and the owner of this work named both on 9 October 2026.

- **What a lexostatus gives was not traceable to the law.** The names and types of the values of `aanvraag` stood in the configuration, while the law already declares them: the application of Awir 15, with the hooks on it, says which fields it has, and the decision article says which parameters it asks. "If we hide it in our own system, a large part of the working disappears into the inside of the case system."
- **The configuration repeated the law in a language of its own.** The same point as [RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045), now for the shape of the answer rather than the reading.

The nuance came later the same day. Never using cell configuration is the five-star solution, not a hard rule. The rule is to make as much as possible derivable from the articles of the law, to use policy where the law really says nothing, and to use cell configuration only as a last resort, with the reason stated.

The proof of concept had tried a broader version of this, as RFC-047 on branch `poc/chronolex`, which is not merged: the whole process of an actor (portal, worklist, acts) derived from law and policy, with `executes` to link a policy article to the article it works out. This RFC takes over its thesis for the cell, and its origin role `BESLUIT` (§5). `executes` and channel declarations in policy are not part of it.

## Decision

The law sets the interface of a cell: which values it must be able to supply to an execution, with their name, their type and who supplies them. Each cell decides how it arrives at those values. RegelRecht's own fact reduction, in the law format, is one way to do that.

### 1. The interface is what the executing articles declare

What a cell must supply to a decision is what the stage of the decision asks: the parameters of the article and of the hooks that fire at that stage, and the values the stage `requires`. The engine reports them, with each parameter's `origin` ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)) and the article that declares it. What a cell must supply to an execution (an installment, a payment order) is what the executed article declares.

For the voorschot on a zorgtoeslag, the stage VOORSCHOT of the procedure `tegemoetkoming` asks, among others, the BSN (Zorgtoeslagwet 2), the berekeningsjaar (Awir 16, role `TIJDVAK`), the moment of receipt of the application (Awir 16), the expected income (Awir 16) and the dagtekening of the voorschot (the stage's `requires`). Nothing in the cell configuration lists them.

### 2. Some values the cell fills itself, because the law says which

The law marks the parameters a recording runtime fills from its own record, and the cell fills only those:

| The law says | The cell fills it with |
|---|---|
| exactly one date among the stage's `requires`, or `produces.moment.parameter` | the day the decision is taken: the date it bears (`dated_by`) |
| `origin` with `rol: TIJDVAK` | the period the decision concerns ([RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050) §4) |
| `origin` with `rol: GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT` | the decision the application asks for |
| `origin` with `rol: BESLUIT` (§5) | the id of the decision the act is about |
| a date parameter of the application whose origin rests on the provision of `produces.moment` | the moment of receipt of the application |

Anything else a stage requires and nobody gives is refused, not guessed.

### 3. How a cell arrives at the values is its own

A cell may answer the interface in any way. In order of preference, RegelRecht offers three.

1. **What the law already describes needs no reduction.** A decision on an application reads what it asks of that application from the application itself, as the law declares it: the fields of the submission ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046) §3) and its moment. The cell reads them from the latest gram of that submission in the case, at the moment of reading. A field the gram lacks is left out, as a fact nobody has; it is not a `null` the applicant stated.
2. **What policy says is an article of the holder's policy** ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). What was paid on the voorschot for a berekeningsjaar, the latest inkomensgegeven over that year, or the expected income for a following year are outputs of `fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen`. A stream names which policy, or which article of it, an event reads (`reads`).
3. **Cell configuration is the last resort**, and a configuration that reduces states why the law and policy could not carry it.

A cell need not use any of them. A cell in front of a legacy system or an API may answer the same interface with its own query. What it may not do is answer a different interface: a value with another name or type than the article declares is not an answer to that article.

### 4. A lexostatus may be a record

In this implementation a lexostatus is a record: several named, typed values, each with the provisions it rests on, the article that declares it, its provenance, and the grams it was read from. Whether a lexostatus is one value or a record is an implementation choice. The paper leaves the form of a lexostatus open, and RFC-022's own example (`openstaande_vorderingen`) is a list of records. What the record contains is not a choice of the cell: its fields are what the events that read it ask of it, or every value it can give if none does.

The cell describes each lexostatus it can give (`Cell::lexostatuses`): per submission it records, what the application says; per article of a policy that reads a register of the cell, that article. `Cell::read_lexostatus` reads either by name.

### 5. Origin role `BESLUIT`

A parameter with `rol: BESLUIT` receives the id of the decision an act acts on, such as the toekenning a nabetaling executes. It is a fact of the course of the case, so the role is allowed only with `waarde: DOSSIER`. More than one `BESLUIT` parameter in one article is an error when a runtime acts on it, because the runtime could not tell which decision is meant.

The role says in the law what a process configuration said before: which parameter takes the decision. In this repository it is declared in schema v0.8.0 and read by the law model, and no article uses it yet: the payments of the fictitious policies refer to their decision with an explicit `refers_to` ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049) §5), because the law format has no word yet for a fact that executes a decision.

## Why

### Benefits

- What a cell answers is traceable to an article and a provision. A jurist who reads the decision article reads the interface; nothing about it hides in the case system.
- One change in the law changes the interface. If Awir 16 asks one more datum, every cell that supplies the voorschot is asked for it, and one that cannot supply it fails on that parameter.
- The cell stays free in how it reduces, which is what the paper asks of it, and the law-format route of RFC-045 is available without being imposed.

### Tradeoffs

- This is a deliberate deviation from the paper's autonomy of the cell: the paper lets a cell decide what it exposes, and here the law decides. RegelRecht executes law, and for that it is useful that the law fixes what must be answered.
- Reading the application from the latest gram of the submission assumes the latest one is the one that counts. A supplement (Awb 4:5) recorded as a gram of its own would need its own rule.
- A value the law does not declare cannot be asked of a cell at all, even when a process needs it. It has to be declared in the law or in policy first.

### Alternatives Considered

**A lexostatus language in cell configuration** (RFC-022 §4.1's provisional shape, and the compact cell's `lexostatuses.yaml`). Rejected as the default, kept as the last resort: names, types and legal bases of the values were written a second time, outside the law.

**A process configuration that names, per act, which parameter comes from where** (the proof of concept's `process.yaml`). Rejected: it repeats what the law and the actor's policy already say, and the copy in configuration is the one nobody reviews as law.

**The cell decides its interface, as in the paper.** Rejected for RegelRecht: a law execution system then cannot tell from the law what a cell owes an execution.

**The law prescribes the reduction** (`reads` in the reading article, 28 September in the proof of concept). Rejected in RFC-045: no law says how a register works.

## Implementation in this pull request

PR #1689 moves the zorgtoeslag cell to this design: the application is read from the law's description of it (`Cell::lexostatuses`, `Cell::read_lexostatus`), `lexostatuses.yaml` is removed from the demo cell and the test fixtures, and what was paid on a voorschot is an article of the fictitious policy. The values of §2 that the cell fills are built: `dated_by`, the period, the decision requested and the moment of receipt. Role `BESLUIT` (§5) is in schema v0.8.0 and the law model only.

## Relation to the position paper

The paper makes the cell autonomous over its facts and its reduction, and leaves the form of a lexostatus open. This RFC keeps the second and narrows the first: how the cell reduces stays the cell's, what it must be able to answer comes from the law. That narrowing is our own choice, made for a system whose purpose is executing law.

## Open Questions

1. **Several submissions in one case.** A supplement or a change notice (Awir 17) as its own gram: does a decision read the latest gram, the latest per field, or a policy article that combines them?
2. **Declaring the last resort.** When a cell does reduce in its configuration, where is the reason recorded so a reviewer finds it?
3. **`executes`.** The proof of concept's link from a policy article to the article of law it works out would show in a trace which policy carries out which article. Whether it comes back, and under which name, is open.
4. **Is a policy article a grondslag?** A value is given a grondslag only where the text of a law says it in so many words. A policy rule (beleidsregel, Awb 4:81) confers no power; it says how an organ uses a power it already has, and the organ may depart from it (Awb 4:84). Yet the fictitious policies in this pull request cite their own articles as `legal_basis`, and the demo shows that under the heading "Grondslag". The options: keep one field and call it something weaker than grondslag (for example "Basis"), which covers both law and policy; or split it into a grondslag (law only) and a source (policy). Not yet decided.
5. **Derived grondslagen.** Some links hold only through a chain of definitions rather than one article. The application for zorgtoeslag rests on Awir 15, and the Zorgtoeslagwet is reached only through Awir 1 and 2 and Zorgtoeslagwet 1 lid 1 onder e. Such a chain is not listed as a grondslag. Whether it is shown at all, separately as "derived through", and whether the law format needs a key to state the link explicitly, is open.
