## Context

[RFC-044](/rfcs/rfc-044) §8 gave every process in the chronolex runtime a `process.yaml`: the actor, the authority it acts for, mandates, channels and roles, the portal with its check and offer, the synthesis, the work queue and the acts. An analysis of the NAPP case on 2 October 2026 went through that file block by block. Most of it repeated what the law and the actor's policy already say:

| Block in `process.yaml` | Where it already stood |
|---|---|
| `actor`, `on_behalf_of` | the `recording_actor` of the cell, the `competent_authority` of the policy |
| `channels`, `roles` | the services policy (DVB) and the implementing policy (UB 4, UB 6) name the portal, the login and the counter |
| `portal.event` | the stream: the event that Wpp 102 establishes |
| `portal.assessment` | Wpp 102 and the lexostatus that reads the application for it (UB 16) |
| `portal.offer` | DVB 2, 2a and 2b |
| `handling.worklist` | DVB 3 lid 1: the applications "waarop nog niet is besloten" |
| `handling.actions` | the streams, the stages of the procedure (RFC-008) and the articles that read each event |
| `synthesis`, `rows` | the consumer's regulation, once RFC-045 is migrated |
| `examples` | nowhere: demo data |

Two copies of the same rule drift, and the copy in configuration is the one nobody reviews as law. The NAPP portal, for instance, checked the application against Wpp 102 because `process.yaml` said so, while the services policy said nothing about checking before submission.

A second gap: a policy article had no way to say which article of law it works out. UB 4b, which says what the portal records in an application, works out Wpp 102. The corpus could not express that, so the engine could not show it in a trace, and the runtime could not use it.

## Decision

The process of an actor follows from the law and the actor's policy. `process.yaml` is gone. The deployment keeps the technique of a channel, the synthesis until RFC-045, and the demo examples.

### 1. `executes`: a policy article names the article it works out

A `machine_readable` section gets an optional list `executes`:

```yaml
# UB 4b: what the portal records in the application
machine_readable:
  executes:
    - {article: 'wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102'}
```

`article` is `<regulation>#<article>`, without a paragraph. `as` is optional and names the subject of a beleidsregel the policy works out. Awb 1:3 lid 4 names three, and `as` takes only those:

| `as` | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `fact_finding` | the policy fixes how facts are established | Awb 1:3 lid 4, "de vaststelling van feiten" |
| `interpretation` | the policy interprets a statutory provision | Awb 1:3 lid 4, "de uitleg van wettelijke voorschriften" |
| `weighing` | the policy weighs interests | Awb 1:3 lid 4, "de afweging van belangen" |

Without `as`, the policy executes the article without being a beleidsregel in the sense of Awb 1:3 lid 4. UB 4b above is such a case: it describes the portal, a way of working, and fixes no fact, reading or weighing. A fourth kind, `procedure`, was considered for that case and dropped on 3 October 2026, because it does not come from the law.

**Only the specific article.** UB 4b executes Wpp 102, not Awb 4:2. Awb 4:2 and 4:13 already apply to the application that Wpp 102 establishes, through the hook of RFC-046, and the engine knows that. An `executes` towards the Awb would say the same link twice, and a reader could no longer tell which of the two the policy chose. The explanation and the map show the hook as a step of its own.

**Metadata, not law.** An entry of `executes` is declared metadata in the sense of RFC-048: the law model keeps an entry it cannot read as written (`Declared::Invalid`) instead of refusing the law. Loading a regulation never fails on an entry of `executes`. The engine skips an invalid entry; the runtime reports it and does not start.

**In the engine.** The resolver keeps an index in both directions, after the pattern of `decides_on` (RFC-046): `executed_by(law, article)` gives the policy articles that execute an article of law, and `executes_of(law, article)` gives what a policy article executes. The index holds the newest version of each policy: a new version replaces the entries of the old one. `executes_of_on` answers for the version in force on a date. When the engine runs a policy article with `executes`, the trace node says so: "art. 4b voert uit: wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102", followed by the Awb term and its source when `as` is given, as in "(vaststelling van feiten, Awb 1:3 lid 4)". The key→term mapping is in one place, `ExecutesKind::awb_term` in `packages/law-model`. `executes` does not change any outcome.

**The authority check.** A policy rule may only concern a competence of the body that makes it (Awb 4:81). The runtime checks every `executes` of every loaded version at start-up. The target must be a loaded article. The policy must name a `competent_authority` (call it A). Then the target fits when:

- the target article, or else its regulation itself, names A as its competent authority, or
- neither names one, and the articles of the regulation name none, or A among others.

A general law without an authority (the Awb, the Burgerlijk Wetboek) can therefore be executed by anyone's policy. The rule is wider than "the same authority as the target": the Wpp names the Autoriteit only in article 107, so a literal reading would reject DVB 1a executing Wpp 102, the example the design started from. An error names both articles and both authorities, and stops the runtime.

**Where it shows.** The explanation of a form field ("Waarom?") gets a step of kind `executes`, with the policy article as source and "voert <article> uit" as reason, with the same Awb term as the trace when `as` is given. The construction map gets an edge `executes` from the policy article to the article of law.

### 2. Channels in the policy

What a channel is belongs to the policy of the actor. An article of a regulation of layer `UITVOERINGSBELEID` with a `competent_authority` may declare, under `produces.extensions.chronolex`:

- `channels`: per channel name its `kind` (`portal`, `handling` or `counter`, which decides the route group of its role), optionally `role` (default: the channel name), `identifies` (what the login shows, as names or as names with the legal basis that knows the item), `owner` (the field of the submission that decides who follows a case), `legal_basis` (the first entry is the basis of the role), and on the portal `submits` (the article whose submission it records), `assesses` (the output of that article the portal checks before submitting; required on the channel with `submits`), `offers` and `form` (the actor's working document);
- `supplies`: per channel, which parameter of the submission takes which item the channel knows (`kvk_nummer: kvk_nummer`, `dagtekening: $submitted_on`), with the declaring article as its legal basis;
- `mandates`: authorities the actor acts for under mandate (Awb 10:1), each with `authority` and `legal_basis`.

The NAPP services policy, article 1a, declares three channels: the public portal `eherkenning`, the handling portal `medewerker` and the counter `papier`. UB 4b says what `eherkenning` supplies to the application.

Channels, supplies or mandates outside implementing policy, or in a policy without a competent authority, are start-up errors. So is a channel declared twice, or `supplies` for a channel the actor's policy does not declare in the version in force. Two channels with the same role are an error, and so are mandates or supplies in a policy that declares no channel at all. Exactly one channel of an actor names `submits`. Other portal channels of the same actor use the same portal.

### 3. The deployment

Three files remain, each pointed to by an environment variable and each grouped per actor under the id of its cell. The paths below are examples; the variable decides where a file is. NAPP keeps its examples at `chronicles/voorbeelden/examples.yaml`.

- **`deployment/channels.yaml`** (`CELL_CHANNELS`): per channel the adapter (only `simulated` in the proof of concept), the label, an explanation, the role label and the login fields with label, pattern, check and message. A channel in the policy without an adapter is an error, and so is an adapter without a channel. Every name in `identifies` must be a field here.
- **`deployment/synthesis.yaml`** (`CELL_SYNTHESIS`): the synthesis sources and the per-row synthesis of the assessment and of each act, in the shape `process.yaml` had. It is temporary and goes with the migration of RFC-045.
- **`deployment/examples.yaml`** (`CELL_EXAMPLES`): example logins, an application and act forms for the demo.

Without `CELL_CHANNELS` the runtime runs only its cells. `CELL_SYNTHESIS` and `CELL_EXAMPLES` without `CELL_CHANNELS` are an error.

The grouping per cell is needed because one runtime can host several actors that use the same channel names. The test fixtures run three actors, each with an `eherkenning` or a `medewerker` channel.

### 4. What the runtime derives

**One process per actor.** The process of an actor is the set of channels in the policies of the same `competent_authority`. Its `actor` is the `recording_actor` of the stream of the portal event, `on_behalf_of` is the authority, and its id is the id of the cell of that stream. Two authorities recording in one cell is an error.

**The portal event.** The event, in any cell, whose establishing article (the first of `establishes`, otherwise the article of the first legal basis) is the `submits` article, and which is a submission (`type: submission` or stage `AANVRAAG`). There must be exactly one.

**The assessment.** The lexostatus of that cell, not a list, that reads the portal event and derives a parameter of the `submits` article. For NAPP that is the lexostatus of UB 16. There must be exactly one, and `assesses.output` must be an output of the `submits` article.

**The worklist.** Every cell with a submission gets a built-in list lexostatus `worklist`, so no cell defines one itself; the name is reserved. It holds the cases of the submission on which not every requested decision has been taken yet: DVB 3 lid 1 speaks of applications "waarop nog niet is besloten", so a case leaves the list once decided. The requested decisions are the articles that name the establishing article in `decides_on` (RFC-046), otherwise the articles in the cell's chronicle that establish a decision (stage `BESLUIT`). The columns are derived, never configured: the moment of receipt and of recording, the field the portal channel names as `owner`, and the field whose parameter has origin role `TIJDVAK` (the period of the requested decision, Awb 4:2 lid 1).

**All cases.** Next to the worklist every such cell gets a second built-in list lexostatus, `cases`, also reserved. It holds every case of the same submission, decided or not, with the columns of the worklist and `besloten`: the date of the latest gram of stage `BESLUIT` in the case, null without one. A cell whose chronicle has no decision event has no `besloten` column. The handling offers it at `GET /api/cases`, next to `GET /api/worklist`, for the same role. In a list, a derivation over a collection with its own filter reads every gram of the case, not only the grams through the filter of the list; that is how `besloten` sees the decision while the list selects the submission.

**The acts.** Every event in the actor's cell whose intake names a channel of kind `handling` becomes an act, in the order of the streams. Its name is the event name, its label the readable event name with the establishing article, as in "Aanvulling gevraagd (Algemene wet bestuursrecht, artikel 4:5)".

- A *decision* (stage `BESLUIT`) executes its establishing article. Its outputs are the `$external` or supplied keys of the event that are outputs of that article.
- A *follow-up* (a later stage of the procedure of a decision article) acts on that decision. An event that can follow more than one decision becomes one act per decision, named `<event>_<decision-event>` and labelled with " bij <decision>". Its outputs are what the event records of the decision, or all outputs of the decision when it records none.
- A *fact* (no stage) executes, in this order: (a) when it follows a decision, the policy article of the actor with a parameter of origin role `BESLUIT` that executes an article in the event's legal basis; otherwise (b) the articles that read the lexostatuses reading this event: for a lexostatus from the law the articles its reading article executes, for one of the cell every article with a parameter the lexostatus derives from this event, apart from a `BESCHIKKING`, in a regulation of the event or in a regulation that names no authority other than the actor's. A candidate counts when an output depends on those parameters. Its outputs are those dependent outputs, minus the assessments of the article. With more than one, the one in the regulation of a decision of the actor wins; with more than one left the runtime does not start. With none there is no act, and a warning.
- The decision a fact or an amendment acts on is the target of its reference to a decision: the act of that event or article, or of the stage when the actor has exactly one decision act.

The intake of an act's event is now a channel name (`medewerker`). The portal intake stays `portaal`, so the recorded field `kanaal` of an application is unchanged; the runtime finds the portal event through `submits`, not through the intake.

Everything after the derivation is unchanged: the runtime builds the same internal process definition it used to read from `process.yaml`, and the origin check, the forms and the routes run on it. The origin check is always strict; the setting `origin_check` is gone.

### 5. Origin role `BESLUIT`

RFC-048 gets a third role next to `TIJDVAK` and `GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT`. A parameter with `rol: BESLUIT` receives the id of the decision an act acts on, such as the subsidy determination a payment executes (UB 15). It is a fact of the course of the case, so the role is allowed only with `waarde: DOSSIER`; anything else is a load-time error. More than one `BESLUIT` parameter in an article is an error when an act uses the article.

The role replaces the `decision_parameter` field of an act in `process.yaml`: the law now says which parameter it is. Law schema v0.8.0 declares `origin` with all three roles (RFC-048).

### 6. Schema

`executes` goes into `schema/v0.8.0/schema.json`, the draft of RFC-046, on `machineReadableSection`. `schema/latest` points to the released v0.7.1, and a released version is not changed. The conformance suite has a valid and an invalid case for it. The items of `decides_on` in v0.8.0 now `$ref` `articleReference`, which forbids a space, so `decides_on` is stricter in v0.8.0 than before.

### 7. Relation to RFC-044

This RFC replaces RFC-044 §8 where it describes `process.yaml`. The configuration of a cell (`cell.yaml`) and the rest of RFC-044 stay as they are. RFC-044 and RFC-048 get a note pointing here; their text is not changed.

## Why

### Benefits

- A rule of the actor stands once, in the policy, where a lawyer reviews it. The portal checks before submitting because DVB 1a lid 2 says so.
- A process for a new case is written as policy plus a short deployment file. The runtime finds portal, assessment, worklist and acts in what the corpus already says.
- `executes` gives the engine real links from policy to law. A reader of a trace or of the map sees which article a policy works out and how.
- The authority check makes Awb 4:81 something the runtime enforces: a policy cannot quietly work out another body's competence.
- Channel technique and channel meaning are apart, as registers already are (`CELL_REGISTERS`). Swapping the simulated login for real eHerkenning touches the deployment only.

### Tradeoffs

- **`executes` steers behavior.** The outputs of a fact act follow from the article the reading policy article executes. The classification of `executes` is therefore not only explanation: a different classification gives a different act. In NAPP the act `fusieakte_ontvangen` now computes Wpp 110 instead of Wpp 102, because UB 18 executes Wpp 110.
- **The label names the establishing article, not the executed one.** "Aanvulling gevraagd (Algemene wet bestuursrecht, artikel 4:5)" computes Awb 4:15. The label says where the act comes from, not what it computes.
- **Acts follow the order of the streams.** The decision is no longer first in the list.
- **A decided case leaves the worklist, so the handler needs a second list.** The announcement and the payment follow the decision, when the case is no longer in the worklist. The runtime therefore offers `cases` next to it, and the handling screen has a tab per list. The alternative, keeping decided cases in the worklist until the last stage, would contradict DVB 3 lid 1.
- **The worklist with more than one decision.** A case should leave the list only when each requested decision has a `BESLUIT` gram. The reduction DSL cannot say "each of these articles": `without` is one filter, and any gram through it takes the case off. A cell with more than one decision article, such as the allowance fixture (an advance and a determination), therefore keeps every case in its worklist. This is a known limitation, documented in `packages/cel/src/reduction/worklist.rs`, until the DSL can require a gram per article.
- **Renamed routes and answers.** The process id is the cell id and an act is named after its event (`besluit` became `besluit_genomen`). The worklist lost the configured columns `aanvrager` and `maand`; in NAPP it also lost `besloten`, `bekendgemaakt` and `betaald`. A decided case is still reachable through the list `cases`. No backwards compatibility is kept; this is a proof of concept.
- **Corpora on an older schema.** The NAPP corpus validates against v0.5.9, which does not know `executes`. The same holds for `origins` (RFC-048) and the submission hook (RFC-046).

### Alternatives Considered

**`implements` (RFC-003).** It fills an open term the law leaves to delegated legislation or policy. Wpp 102 leaves nothing open about how an application is submitted, and most policy articles work out an article without filling a named term. Rejected.

**`overrides`.** It replaces an output, a lex specialis. A policy rule does not replace the law it works out. Rejected.

**`legal_basis` on the regulation.** It says which law authorizes the whole regulation, not which article works out which article. Too coarse for a trace or a map. Rejected.

**`executes` towards the general law as well** (UB 4b executes Awb 4:2). It repeats the hook of RFC-046 and lets the policy and the engine disagree about which Awb articles apply. Rejected by Tim de Jager on 2 October 2026.

**A flat `channels.yaml`** (channel name at the top level, as the first design sketch had it). It cannot hold two actors with the same channel name in one runtime, which the fixtures need. The files are grouped per cell id instead.

**The authority check as literally specified** (the same authority as the target's regulation, or named at the target). It rejects DVB 1a executing Wpp 102, the case it was designed for. Replaced by the rule in §1.

### Implementation Notes

Built on branch `poc/chronolex`: the schema field (v0.8.0), `Executes` and `OriginRole::Besluit` in `packages/law-model`, the index, `executed_by`, `executes_of(_on)` and the trace note in `packages/engine`, and in `packages/cel` the policy reader and the authority check (`policy.rs`), the deployment files (`deployment.rs`), the derivation (`derive.rs`, `derive/handling.rs`), the built-in worklist (`reduction/worklist.rs`), the `executes` step in the explanation and the edge on the map. `process.yaml` and `PROCESSES_PATH` are removed; the test fixtures and the NAPP corpus run on the derived route.

`implementation` is *Partially implemented* because the synthesis still sits in `deployment/synthesis.yaml` until the migration of RFC-045 moves it into the consumer's regulation. Nothing else of this RFC is open.

## Open Questions

1. **The classification of `executes`.** The first classification of the NAPP implementing and services policy is our own choice and is recorded as a finding of the case, not as a decision point. Some rows do not follow the rule of §1 strictly: UB 19 to 22 read facts for Awb 3:40, 4:52, 2:8 and 4:15. Doubtful rows: DVB 3 towards Wpp 107, UB 3, UB 8, UB 10 and UB 23. Because `executes` steers the outputs of acts (see Tradeoffs), a different classification gives different acts; the generic rule in §4 does not depend on this case.
2. **The name `executes`.** It may still change.
3. **The worklist columns.** The owner field and the `TIJDVAK` field are derived; `aanvrager` and `maand` are gone. Whether a worklist needs more, and whether such a column should come from policy, is open.
4. **The portal intake.** Only act intakes became channel names. The portal intake stays `portaal` to keep the recorded field `kanaal` of an application. Naming it after its channel would make the stream say which channel recorded it, at the cost of changing every recorded application.
5. **The worklist with more than one decision.** The reduction DSL needs a way to require one gram per article (see Tradeoffs), or the worklist leaves the DSL.
