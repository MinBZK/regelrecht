## Context

An article asks for parameters. Who supplies each one was written only as prose in its `description`, for instance "Herkomst: de aanvrager zelf, in de aanvraag". The engine does not need that: it takes whatever the caller passes. A runtime that carries out a procedure does need it, and because it cannot read prose, the same knowledge ends up written a second time in configuration:

- which parameters belong to the application, and so to the form the applicant fills in;
- which period an application asks for, and so which version of the law a decision applies;
- which parameters a register supplies;
- which judgements the official gives when deciding.

The law already divides the work. The applicant supplies the information needed for a decision that he can reasonably obtain (Awb 4:2 lid 2). The administrative body gathers the knowledge it needs (Awb 3:2). A special law may oblige a register keeper to supply facts on request. Some facts arise from the procedure itself (a request to complete the application, Awb 4:5; a suspension, Awb 4:15; publication, Awb 3:41), and some parameters are judgements the body gives when it decides (Awb 3:2, 3:4, 3:46). Classifying a parameter is one act of interpretation, and whoever writes the prose already performs it.

## Decision

### 1. `origin` on a parameter

A parameter can carry an `origin` with a fixed value and a `grondslag`: the provision that says who supplies it.

```yaml
parameters:
  - name: naam_aanvrager
    type: string
    origin:
      waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE
      grondslag: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2 lid 1
  - name: inkomensgegeven
    type: amount
    origin:
      waarde: REGISTER
      register: algemene_wet_inzake_rijksbelastingen
      grondslag: algemene_wet_inzake_rijksbelastingen#21e lid 1
```

The `grondslag` is given only when the text of the provision says it. Awb 4:2 lid 1 says the application contains "de naam en het adres van de aanvrager"; AWR 21e lid 1 says the inspecteur supplies an inkomensgegeven to an afnemer on request. A basis that follows only from reading several provisions together is not a `grondslag`. The origin then has no `grondslag`, and the reasoning goes in a comment next to it, marked as an assumption or an own choice. The BSN on an Awir application is such a case: no article says that the applicant gives it.

| `waarde` | Meaning | Typical `grondslag` |
|---|---|---|
| `BELANGHEBBENDE` | what the applicant supplies or chooses: the content of the application, the decision requested, the period | Awb 4:2 lid 1 and 2; "de aanvraag bevat …" in the special law |
| `DOSSIER` | a fact from the course of the case at the administrative body | Awb 4:5, 4:15, 3:41; the article of the act itself |
| `OORDEEL` | a judgement the administrative body gives when it decides | Awb 3:2, 3:4, 3:46 |
| `REGISTER` (with `register: <regulation>`) | a fact from a register kept by or under that regulation | the provision that lets the consumer ask |
| `KANAAL` | what the intake channel says: who logs in and on whose behalf | Awb chapter 2, section 2.3; the implementing policy for the channel |

Values are Dutch and upper case, like `legal_character`. The `grondslag` is `<regulation>#<article>`, optionally followed by ` lid <n>`. A parameter without `origin` stays valid. A parameter whose value a calling law passes in, which is most of the Awb, needs none.

`REGISTER` means that the value comes from a register through a `source` to the implementing policy of the register's keeper ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) §1). `register` names the law that sets up the register, and `grondslag` the provision that lets the consumer ask. It is kept as a value of its own, rather than left to the `source`, because a runtime needs to know that a fact is known before the application, and a `source` alone does not say that. `register` belongs to `REGISTER` and only to it: a `REGISTER` origin without one says nothing checkable about who keeps the fact, so it is an error, and so is a `register` on any other value.

### 2. Roles

An origin may carry a `rol` when a runtime needs to know what the parameter is within the decision, beyond who supplies it.

| `rol` | With | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `TIJDVAK` | `BELANGHEBBENDE` | the period the decision covers, which the applicant chooses as part of the decision requested (Awb 4:2 lid 1) |
| `GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT` | `BELANGHEBBENDE` | the decision requested (Awb 4:2 lid 1 onder c): fixed by the decision the application asks for, not typed in |
| `BESLUIT` | `DOSSIER` | the decision already taken that the parameter is about; the runtime gives the id of that decision ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)) |

There is no separate origin value for the period. The applicant chooses it as part of the decision requested, so it is `BELANGHEBBENDE`. An earlier draft let the `grondslag` Awb 4:2 lid 1 mark the period. That gave one provision two meanings: the period, and any other part of the request that rests on the same paragraph. The period is now marked on its own:

```yaml
- name: aangevraagd_berekeningsjaar
  type: number
  origin:
    waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE
    grondslag: algemene_wet_inkomensafhankelijke_regelingen#15 lid 1
    rol: TIJDVAK
```

A decision on a period applies the version of the law of that period, not of the day it is taken ([RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050) §4).

### 3. Implementing policy can override

The law gives the default. An article of a regulation with layer `UITVOERINGSBELEID` may override it in `machine_readable`, again with a `grondslag`:

```yaml
machine_readable:
  origins:        # the body retrieves this itself, rather than asking for it
    - regulation: een_bijzondere_wet
      parameter: verdeeltabel
      origin: {waarde: REGISTER, register: een_registerwet, grondslag: een_beleid#4 lid 1}
```

For a process, the applicable origin is the one in `origins` of a loaded implementing policy whose `competent_authority` is the authority the process acts for; otherwise the one in the law. A process acting under a mandate (Awb 10:1) does not take over the overrides of the mandating authority's policy. Two policies of the same actor that give the same parameter different origins are a load-time error. An override classifies; it does not supply. When it sets `REGISTER`, its `grondslag` names the article of the same policy that carries the `source` to the keeper's policy, so classification and supplier sit in one place.

### 4. What a process runtime does with it

The engine does not read `origin` or `origins`. A process runtime does:

- **The fields of an application.** A parameter of the application with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL` is something the applicant or the channel supplies, and so a field of the recorded application ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046) §3, [RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049)).
- **The period.** The parameter with `rol: TIJDVAK` gives the period a decision on the application concerns.
- **The decision requested.** The runtime fills the parameter with `rol: GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT` with the decision the application asks for; the applicant does not type it.
- **A wrong source is always an error.** A parameter supplied by something that does not fit its origin stops the runtime, also when it is `required: false`, and also when another supplier does fit. `required: false` decides what the engine does with a missing value; it does not make a value from the wrong party acceptable.
- **Completeness at start-up.** For every outcome the process executes, every parameter the caller must supply has a supplier that fits its applicable origin. A required parameter without one is a start-up error naming the parameter, the origin and the `grondslag`.
- **The offer.** The condition behind an offer in a portal may only read parameters with `KANAAL` or `REGISTER`, or `BELANGHEBBENDE` with `rol: TIJDVAK`. Anything else is not known before the application is filled in.
- **The decision form.** The `OORDEEL` parameters of a decision, grouped per article of their `grondslag`, are what the official fills in.
- **`required` stays engine semantics.** The runtime does not derive `required` from `origin`. A fact that legitimately does not apply is `nullable` and passed as `null`.

## Why

### Benefits

- Knowledge that was repeated in configuration follows from the law, with a provision a legal expert can check (RFC-027).
- A process that forgets a supplier fails at start-up with the provision that says who should supply the value, instead of failing on the first case with "missing X".
- The form of an application follows from the law: change who supplies a parameter, and the form changes with it.

### Tradeoffs

- The classification is interpretation and can be wrong. The `grondslag` makes it checkable, not correct.
- `origins` in policy is a second place to look. It is limited to the actor's own implementing policy, and conflicts are errors.
- Existing corpora carry the prose. Converting it is a mapping per phrasing, and phrasings that do not map cleanly stay without `origin`.
- The role `TIJDVAK` is allowed only with `BELANGHEBBENDE`, so a decision nobody applies for (an aanslag) cannot name its period this way. [RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050) §5 records the stopgap and the open question.

### Alternatives Considered

**Keep the prose and parse it.** Rejected: the prose varies per law and per author, and a parser for it would be a second, hidden vocabulary.

**Name the supplying cell in the law.** Couples a law to one deployment. `origin` names the regulation under which a register is kept; which cell or system keeps it is said where the register is bound (RFC-045 §1).

**Derive `required` from `origin`.** Rejected: `required` decides what the engine does with a missing value (RFC-036), and a law may have reasons to require an applicant fact.

**A separate value for the period (`KADER`).** Rejected: the period is part of what the applicant asks for.

## Implementation in this pull request

- `packages/law-model` reads `origin` and `origins`. An entry that cannot be read is kept as written (`Declared::Invalid`), so a wrong `origin` never stops the engine from loading a law; a runtime that reads it reports it with the file and the parameter. Serde ignores unknown fields, so older engines are unaffected.
- Law schema v0.8.0 declares `origin` on a parameter: `waarde`, an optional `grondslag`, an optional `register` (required with `REGISTER`, forbidden otherwise) and an optional `rol` (`TIJDVAK` and `GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT` only with `BELANGHEBBENDE`, `BESLUIT` only with `DOSSIER`). It does not declare `origins`, so a law with `origins` does not validate against v0.8.0; that waits for a later schema version.
- The corpus carries `origin` on Awir 15, the Awb 4:2 hook, the decisions of the Awir procedure and the fictitious policies. `packages/cel` uses it for the fields of an application, the period and the decision requested (§4, first three points).
- Not built in this repository: the completeness check, the offer rule and the decision form. The proof of concept on branch `poc/chronolex` built them; they come with a runtime that has forms and a portal.
