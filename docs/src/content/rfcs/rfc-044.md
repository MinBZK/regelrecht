## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) introduces the vocabulary of the Chronolexografie position paper: chronolexograms of three types, the cell that records them in its chronicles, reduction to a lexostatus within a cell, and synthesis across cells. It leaves the configuration of a cell to a later RFC and says nothing about who acts on the facts a cell holds.

The aanvraag-cel proof of concept (`packages/cel`, `frontend-cel`, described in [Cel](/components/cel)) built a runtime for it and ran a real subsidy procedure through it, from the application in a portal to the decision, its notification and the payment. A review against the position paper found that the code follows the paper where RFC-022 does not: RFC-022 §4.2 had the law name a cell, while the paper puts synthesis with the consumer. It also found concepts the runtime needed that no RFC describes: the process as the party that acts, a case identifier on grams, two times per gram, the invariant of one stage per decision in a case, a record of the inputs of a decision, and the whole format of the cell and process configuration.

This RFC records those choices. RFC-022 is amended on the points where it contradicted them and points here for the rest.

## Decision

### 1. The cell records, the process acts

The paper gives a cell three functions: recording, keeping one or more chronicles, and reduction, which "vindt altijd plaats ín de cel waar de betreffende chronolexogrammen zijn vastgelegd, op verzoek van een businessproces". Informing, concluding and having a cell record belong to that business process. The runtime keeps the two apart.

A **cell** builds a gram from its stream definition and what a process sends it, checks its form, appends it to a chronicle and reduces its chronicles to lexostatuses. It does nothing across cells and never runs the engine on a regulation.

A **process** acts. It combines lexostatuses from cells (synthesis, §2), runs the engine on a regulation, and sends a cell what it concluded, with the intake (who submitted and through which channel). It never builds, filters or reduces grams itself; the paper calls filtering and deriving reduction, and puts reduction in the cell. That includes what a process needs to know about a case before it acts in it: which stages are in the case, what the decision recorded, how many grams the case has, the latest `op_moment`, and whether an applicant knows the case. The runtime offers this as a lexostatus of every cell with cases, `zaakstand` (§4). A process may still ask a cell for the grams of one case, to show a case handler the file, but it does not interpret them.

The paper leaves open how agreements on which facts can be recorded come about. It asks for mechanisms that make facts understandable "door eisen aan de vorm, zonder de inhoud te beperken", as a question for further research. This runtime's own answer: the cell enforces the form and never the content. It refuses a gram that does not validate against its stream and `gram.json`, whose actor is not the stream's `recording_actor`, that follows a case the cell does not know, whose stage is already in its decision, or that opens a second decision of the same kind in a case (§4), whose `op_moment` lies after the recording or before the case (§3), or whose case changed since the process read it. It checks the last three under the same lock as the write. Whether an act is right is a conclusion of the process, drawn before it acts: when the law says a payment exceeds the decided amount, the process does not pay of itself. When the case handler reports that the payment happened anyway, the cell records it, because an executogram records a delivery that took place, and the lexostatuses then show what follows (an amount paid without being owed).

RFC-022 §2 keeps three axes apart: the cell, the `competent_authority` and the security context. A process adds the **acting actor**: who acts, in which role, through which channel, and on whose behalf. A process names the authority it acts for (`namens`), literally as the law names it, and may act for another authority under a mandate (`mandaten`, Awb 10:1) with a legal ground. Before a decision is recorded, the competent authority the regulation names is compared with `namens`: equal records, a mandate records in the mandating authority's name, anything else is refused. Each gram carries the acting actor as `handelende_actor`.

### 2. Synthesis at the consumer

The law does not name the cell that supplies a fact. A regulation declares a parameter, and may say which regulation the register holding it is kept under (`origin: REGISTER`, [RFC-043](/rfcs/rfc-043)). The process configuration names, per source, the cell, the lexostatus, how each input is filled and which parameters it supplies. The source speaks the language of its own regulation; when the consumer's article names the same fact differently, the process maps the name at the source to its own parameter. A fact that is not a parameter of any article, but is needed as input for a later source, travels as an extra field, so sources can be chained.

This replaces the first version of RFC-022 §4.2, where `source.regulation` named a cell. The paper puts synthesis with the consumer ("Synthese gebeurt bij de afnemer, niet bij de bron"), and a cell name in a law ties that law to one deployment. The runtime chooses the transport per source, as RFC-022 §4.3 says: internal when the cell runs in the same runtime, HTTP when the source has a URL.

An article can ask for a table the applicant fills in only in part, with the rest established per row from registers. The process configuration then names the table field that supplies the rows and the sources asked per row (`rijen`). An input on the way to a source that follows from a rule of the consuming law, such as the reference date of a figure, is computed by the engine from that law rather than converted in configuration.

### 3. Chronicles and grams

A **chronicle** is what the paper calls a *kroniek*: a time-ordered, append-only collection of a cell's chronolexograms, with its own time axis. A cell may keep several. The word has no other meaning in this RFC or in RFC-022.

A stream definition declares, per event, what a gram records. Compared with the sketch in RFC-022 §1.3:

- `grondslag` is a list. An application rests on several provisions at once. An entry may name a paragraph: `<regulation>#<article> lid <n>`.
- `type` is a value from the open vocabulary of RFC-022 §1. Next to `decretogram` and `executogram`, the runtime uses `indiening` for what an applicant submits (an application, a supplement, a document) and `handeling` for factual acts of an authority that are neither a decision nor a delivery (a request to supplement, a notification, a statement of a statistic). The paper names three types as a first enumeration and asks whether there are others; these two are the answer this runtime gives, not a closed list. `soort` narrows the type (`aanvraag`, `aanvulling`, `mededeling`).
- `stage` names the RFC-008 stage a gram brings about: `AANVRAAG` for an application, `BESLUIT` for a decision, `BEKENDMAKING` for its notification. Only a decretogram, an `indiening` or a `handeling` on an event with a case carries one.
- `zaak` says whether a gram opens a case (`opent`, the cell assigns a new `zaakkenmerk`), follows one (`volgt`, the process passes the `zaakkenmerk`) or has none (`geen`, the default). A register entry or an election result is not a case.
- `besluit` says, for an event that follows a case, which decision in the case its grams concern: `opent` (the gram is a decision; the cell assigns a `besluitkenmerk`), `volgt` (the gram follows a decision, such as its notification or a payment that executes it; the process passes the `besluitkenmerk`) or `wijzigt` (the gram is a decision that changes another decision in the case; it gets its own `besluitkenmerk` and carries the other one as `wijzigt`). Without it the gram belongs to the case itself, as the application does. See §4.
- Two times. `op_moment` is when the fact legally holds or happened; `vastgelegd_op` is when the cell recorded it, always from the cell's own clock, stamped under the write lock so the order of a chronicle file is the order of `vastgelegd_op`. By default `op_moment` is the moment of recording. An event may bind it to a submitted value, always with a legal ground that the gram carries as `op_moment_grondslag`. The stream decides per event who may state the legal moment. Bound to `$external`, the acting actor states it as part of the act: the date of a decision, the day of notification under Awb 3:41, the day of a payment or of a statement. Bound to `$intake`, it comes with the intake and the submitter cannot choose it (the date stamp on a paper application). Either way it has bounds: `op_moment` may not lie after the recording, because what has yet to happen is not a fact, and a gram that follows a case may not lie on a day before the latest `op_moment` in that case, because a case moves forward in time. A decision that takes effect later (a removal from a register as of next year) is recorded on the day it was taken, with its date of effect as a field.
- Fields bind to `$external` (the content as submitted), to `$intake` (who submitted, through which channel) or to a constant. An application separates the core Awb 4:2 paragraph 1 asks of every application (`kern`) from the content the special law asks for (`inhoud`). An empty field is recorded as `null`: completeness is a judgment, and a gram holds none.
- A gram carries the hash of its stream, and `herkomst: startstand` when it was placed at start-up rather than recorded by a process.

The schemas for cell and process configuration, stream, lexostatus and gram live in `schema/chronolex/v0.1.0/`, apart from the law schema, so the two version independently. That version has not been released; only the proof of concept reads it.

### 4. The case identifier and RFC-008

RFC-008 makes the besluit the state container and rejects a separate "case" or "zaak" abstraction. The `zaakkenmerk` of this runtime does not bring one back. It is a grouping in the registration: the application opens it, and the grams that follow (the course of the case, the decision, its notification, payments) carry it, so a cell can filter one case out of a chronicle and check its invariants. It has no state, no stages and no lifecycle of its own. The lifecycle stays per besluit: the decision gram carries the inputs and outcomes of the decision, and a follow-up stage runs through the engine's `execute_stage` on that recorded decision.

**Decisions in a case.** A case can hold more than one decision. A monthly allowance has an advance, a final determination after the month and a recovery of what was paid in advance: three besluiten in one case, each with its own article, its own notification and its own objection period. RFC-022 §1.2 has the stage grams of one besluit share a `zaakkenmerk`; that is not enough once a case holds several. The cell therefore gives each decision a `besluitkenmerk`, `<zaakkenmerk>/<n>` for the n-th decision it recorded in the case, under the same lock as the write. A gram that follows a decision carries its `besluitkenmerk`: the notification, and a payment that executes the decision (RFC-022 §3.3 `references_decision`). The stream says per event which of the three an event's grams are (§3).

*Amended 25-09-2026.* The first version of this section had one stage per case, so a case could hold one decision, and left changing a decision out of scope.

**One stage per decision.** A cell refuses a second gram with the same stage in the same decision, under the same lock as the write; a stage that belongs to no decision, such as the application, is in the case once. A gram that follows or changes a decision names one that is in the case. A second decision of the same event in the same case is refused: that is another decision on the same application, and the law gives such a decision its own ground. Changing or withdrawing a decision (Awb 4:48 and 4:49 for a subsidy determination; RFC-008 Open Question 5 makes a withdrawal a nested decision with its own notification and objection) is a decision of its own, recorded in an event with `besluit: wijzigt` and with that ground in its `grondslag`. It carries the decision it changes as `wijzigt`, which is RFC-022 §3.1's `is_wijziging_van` backlink on the gram. That the event is the unit (rather than the article, which the cell does not know) is our own choice. A process names, per act, the act of the decision it changes or executes (`besluit` in `proces.yaml`), and acts on the latest decision of that act in the case, a change of it included; a follow-up stage finds its decision through its article. When the law leaves an outcome of a change empty, as it does without a ground for changing, the process does not take it.

**The state of a case is a lexostatus.** Every cell with an event that opens or follows a case offers the lexostatus `zaakstand`, with `zaakkenmerk` as its input. It answers, as extra fields that never go to the engine, the number of grams in the case, how many grams each event has there, the latest `op_moment`, per stage that belongs to no decision the gram that brought it about (its event, its two times, its regulation, its fields and the values of its inputs), and the decisions in the case, each with its `besluitkenmerk`, the event that recorded it, the decision it changes, its stages and how many grams of each event follow it. With `eigenaar_pad` and `eigenaar` it also answers whether a gram in the case binds that intake path to that value, which is how a portal checks that an applicant follows up only on a case of their own. The process reads this lexostatus to decide which acts are available and on which decision, to find the recorded decision a follow-up stage continues on, and to derive the legal protection route of each decision; it sends the number of grams back when it records, so the cell can refuse when the case changed. The runtime offers `zaakstand` rather than each cell declaring it, because the case, its identifier, the decisions in it and the stage invariant are the runtime's concepts and not a case corpus's, and a cell cannot define a lexostatus of that name.

**The procedure in the Awb.** The RFC-008 procedure of a `BESCHIKKING` is declared in the Awb regulation of the case corpus, with stages `AANVRAAG`, `BESLUIT`, `BEKENDMAKING` and `BEZWAAR`, each with what it `requires`. The hook of Awb 6:8 fires on stage `BEKENDMAKING` and computes the start and end of the objection period, which the notification gram records. The case view derives the running stage after notification from the procedure, per decision: an objection against the recovery has its own period next to that of the advance.

**A later stage has not happened yet.** An article may read facts that only arise after the decision, such as whether and how it was notified. The engine evaluates the whole article in every stage and gates a stage only on its `requires`, so at `BESLUIT` those facts need a value. The runtime supplies it from the procedure: every parameter a later stage requires that no lexostatus of the case derives is `false` (a boolean) or `null` at the moment of deciding, with provenance `stand_bij_besluit` and the stage. Which facts belong to which stage is the Awb's; the rule that a later stage has not happened is the runtime's. This is the situation RFC-008 calls "a workaround, not a design", made explicit rather than solved: an engine that evaluates only what a stage needs would remove it (Open Question 3).

### 5. Reading at a moment

Facts are never removed, so every earlier state can be recovered, the "time travel" of the paper. A reduction takes two optional parameters. `peilmoment` gives the state as it legally held at a moment, with everything the cell knows now: a gram counts when its `op_moment` is on or before it. `bekend_op` gives the state as the cell knew it at a moment: a gram counts when its `vastgelegd_op` is on or before it. Together they answer the bitemporal question. Choosing the latest gram orders on `op_moment`, then on `vastgelegd_op`, so a fact recorded late about an earlier moment is not the latest state.

A process passes a peil wherever the moment matters. An act reads every cell at the date on which the engine reads the regulation, so a fact that only takes effect later does not count, and repeating the trial later gives the same state.

### 6. What a recorded act carries

Every act the engine computed carries, beyond the stream shape, the acting actor, `inputs` and a `receipt`. A decretogram also carries `legal_character` and `decision_type` from the article's `produces`, `regulation` and `regulation_valid_from`, and `competent_authority`. `inputs` holds every parameter with its value and provenance: the lexostatus of the case, a named source cell, the per-row synthesis, the case handler's form, a choice in the portal, or the state at decision. It is the [RFC-013](/rfcs/rfc-013) `accepted_values` idea applied to every parameter. The `receipt` lists the loaded regulations and the cell's streams with one SHA-256 over both, the generalisation of `loaded_regulations` to all loaded artefacts that RFC-022 §1.3 announced. The receipt does not yet include what a source cell answered with that cell's own receipt (Open Question 5).

### 7. Who may write and read

Recording a gram and a trial reduction are reserved for the processes of the same runtime: at each start the runtime makes a random token, the internal transport sends it, and a cell refuses a write without it. Reading a chronicle, a case or a lexostatus needs that token too, or a read token (`CEL_LEES_TOKEN`) that runtimes which may read each other share (an HTTP transport sends it only to the runtimes listed in `CEL_LEES_TOKEN_BRONNEN`), because grams carry the identity and the intake of whoever submitted them. A case handler reads the cells of their process through the process (`/api/inzage`), which asks the cell with the runtime token; an applicant sees only their own submission. Only the stream definitions stay open.

This is not a security context in the sense of RFC-022 §2. It does not authorise one organisation to another, and anyone who can reach a process can still act through its simulated logins. Signing, FSC, Blauwe Knop and certified login (eHerkenning, DigiD) are out of scope for the proof of concept.

### 8. Configuration of cells and processes

Cells and processes are configuration; nothing in the runtime names a case. The runtime loads every directory with a `cel.yaml` and every directory with a `proces.yaml`.

`cel.yaml` holds what a cell does and nothing else: `id`, `recording_actor`, `stromen` (stream definitions, §3), `lexostatussen` and an optional `startstand`. A lexostatus definition filters one chronicle, optionally picks the latest gram, and derives each parameter (`afleidingen`) from the chosen gram or over the grams that pass its own filter; with `groepeer: zaakkenmerk` it answers one row per case, which never goes to the engine. A derivation may carry a `grondslag`. A derivation can give the period a date falls in (`periode_van`: a year, a quarter or a month, as its first day, so the engine reads a period as a date); when it does not name the period, the runtime takes the `temporal.period_type` of the parameter from the regulation (RFC-001), so whether a period is a month or a year is the law's. Every field an event records is read by a derivation or listed as not reduced, with a reason.

`proces.yaml` holds who acts and how:

| Block | Holds |
|---|---|
| `actor`, `namens`, `mandaten` | the recording actor, the authority the process acts for, and mandates with their legal ground (§1) |
| `herkomst` | `streng` makes a parameter without `origin` a start-up error (RFC-043) |
| `kanalen` | per channel its label and identification fields, with a pattern and an optional check, the field that makes someone the owner of a case, and the intake prefix; every channel is simulated |
| `rollen` | per role its channel, its route groups (`portaal`, `behandeling`, `loket`) and optionally its legal ground |
| `portaal` | the event a submission becomes, the lexostatus and outcome of the check before submitting, the form file, and `aanbod` |
| `portaal.aanbod` | the outcome of the actor's own policy regulation that decides what the portal offers, with optional outcomes for the deadline, the periods on offer (`tijdvakken`), the first day of a period (`begin`) and its opening (`openstelling`) |
| `synthese` | the sources (§2); a source with `zaak: true` is a lexostatus of the case in the process's own cell |
| `behandeling` | the work queue (a list lexostatus) and the acts (`handelingen`): per act the article's outcomes, the event that records it, optionally a role and the per-row synthesis (`rijen`) |
| `voorbeelden` | example logins, applications and act forms for a trial setup |

What an act needs is not configured. It follows from the stage of its event and the `origin` of the parameters of its article: a decision's form is its `OORDEEL` parameters, a follow-up stage's form is what the stage requires, and a fact without a stage has the fields of its event. Periods, deadlines and the opening of a period are outcomes of the actor's policy regulation, not values in the configuration: what the law or the policy says belongs in a regulation, and only what makes the proof of concept playable belongs in `proces.yaml`.

Both files are checked at start-up, each cell and process on its own; a failing check stops the runtime and names the cell or process. The checks and the full format are in [Cel](/components/cel).

## Why

### Benefits

- The division of the paper holds in code: a cell cannot be asked to do cross-cell work, and a process cannot write or reduce grams.
- A law stays deployment-independent. Which cell supplies a register fact is the consumer's configuration, and the law says only under which regulation the register is kept.
- The form of what can be recorded is enforced in the cell, under its lock, so two simultaneous decisions or payments in one case cannot both be recorded. The content is judged by the process before it acts, and a fact that happened is never refused for what the process thinks of it.
- Two times per gram make a late recording visible and let a consumer ask what held at a moment, as known at another.
- A recorded decision says what it was computed from, per parameter, and against which regulations and streams.

### Tradeoffs

- The `zaakkenmerk` is a concept neither the law nor the paper introduces. It is kept narrow (a filter key and the scope of two invariants) so it does not grow into a case model next to RFC-008.
- The state at decision is a rule of the runtime, not of the engine, and it makes the decision depend on the procedure being declared in the loaded Awb.
- The configuration is a second language next to the law format: reduction, synthesis and per-row synthesis each have their own vocabulary (Open Question 1).
- The runtime token protects nothing beyond "this came from a process of the same runtime", and the read token nothing beyond "this runtime was given the shared secret".
- A fact reported as having happened is recorded against the process's conclusion. The gram does not say so; the warning in the answer does, and the lexostatuses show the consequence.

### Alternatives Considered

**The law names the cell (`source.regulation: <cell>`).** The first version of RFC-022 §4.2. Rejected: see §2 and RFC-022 Alternative 10.

**The process checks vastlegbaarheid.** An earlier version of the runtime read the whole chronicle in the process, checked whether a decision already existed, and then asked the cell to record. Two simultaneous requests both passed, and the process filtered grams itself, which is reduction outside the cell. Rejected for the cell-side checks of §1 and §4.

**A case as state container.** Rejected: RFC-008 already makes the besluit the state container, and a second one would have to be kept consistent with it.

**One decision per case.** The first version of §4. A case that needs an advance, a final determination and a recovery would need three applications, or would lose the notification and the objection period of every decision after the first. Rejected for a `besluitkenmerk` per decision.

**A new case per decision.** Each decision could open a case of its own. Rejected: the decisions answer one application, and a recovery reads what was paid on the advance; splitting them would make the process join cases, which is reduction outside the cell.

**The process reads the grams of a case.** Until 25 September 2026 the process counted the grams per stage, looked up the decision gram and checked ownership over the fields of a case's grams. That is filtering and deriving, which the paper calls reduction and puts in the cell. Rejected for `zaakstand` (§4).

**Content checks in the cell.** An earlier version refused to record a payment above the decided amount and a notification that did not meet Awb 3:41, on the ground of the event. That refuses a fact because of a conclusion about it, while an executogram records a delivery that took place. Rejected: the process concludes before it acts, and the cell records what happened.

**One timestamp per gram.** RFC-022 §1.3 binds one submitted timestamp. Rejected: the paper separates the moment of recording from the moment a fact concerns ("Op 3 april heeft de gemeente-ambtenaar vastgesteld dat … per 2 april"), and one field cannot hold both.

**Normative content in the process configuration.** An earlier `proces.yaml` held which years a portal offered, the reference date of a figure and the state at decision. Rejected: those are rules of a law or a policy, and they now live in a regulation with a legal ground, where they are checked and traced.

## Open Questions

1. **One rule language.** Reduction, synthesis and per-row synthesis each have their own vocabulary next to the law format, and the start-up checks tie them together. An experiment (Experiment A, 25 September 2026) ran the reduction and the synthesis of the proof of concept as ordinary engine runs, with the same outcomes and the same amount. It works, but only with engine changes that need an RFC of their own: a lexical scope in which a nested `FOREACH` sees the outer element (which RFC-016 rejected), record construction, an operation to pick the latest element in time or a timestamp type, and a way past the limit on collection size, since a real chronicle exceeds it. Reduction as an engine run was also 100 to 200 times slower. Whether to write that engine RFC is open; until then the runtime keeps its own vocabulary.
2. **The lexogram.** The paper defines a lexogram as the recording of a (possible, future) change in legislation, in a cell. RFC-022 §1.1 and this runtime map it to the consolidated regulation YAML in the corpus, which records a version and belongs to no cell. A lexogram in the paper's sense, the change itself recorded by whoever enacts it, is not modelled.
3. **The state at decision.** An engine that evaluates only what an outcome or a stage depends on would let the decision run through `execute_stage` like its follow-ups, and remove the runtime rule of §4.
4. **The trial reduction.** Before submitting, a process asks the cell to reduce a draft as if it were recorded. That is a reduction of something that is not a fact, which the paper does not describe. A cleaner reading is that the check is concluding in the process, over a draft the process holds; that needs the reduction to be available outside the cell without the process filtering grams.
5. **Receipts across cells.** A decision records what a source cell answered, with its provenance, but not that cell's own receipt. A cell that signs its lexostatus answers would close the chain RFC-013 §4 sketches.
6. **Schema release.** `schema/chronolex/v0.1.0/` is unreleased and changes in place. Whether it merges with the law schema or stays apart is open, as is the `origin` and `origins` change to the law schema that RFC-043 prepares.
