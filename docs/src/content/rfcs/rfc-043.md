## Context

An article asks for parameters. Who supplies each one is written today only as prose in its `description`, for instance "Herkomst: de aanvrager zelf, in de aanvraag". The engine does not need that: it takes whatever the caller passes. A runtime that carries out a procedure does need it, and because it cannot read prose, the same knowledge is written a second time by hand in the configuration of each process:

- which parameters a register cell supplies;
- which judgements the official gives when deciding, as a hand-written decision form with labels;
- which parameters are `required: false` because nobody knows them before the application is filled in.

A process that offers a service in a portal has a further rule to enforce: it may only test conditions that are known in advance, such as who logs in and what the registers say. Without machine-readable origin it has to guess this from a trace.

The law already divides the work. The applicant supplies the information needed for a decision that he can reasonably obtain (Awb 4:2, second paragraph). The administrative body gathers the knowledge it needs (Awb 3:2). A special law may oblige a register keeper to supply facts on request. Some facts arise from the procedure itself (a request to complete the application, Awb 4:5; a suspension, Awb 4:15; publication, Awb 3:41), and some parameters are judgements the body gives when it decides (Awb 3:2, 3:4, 3:46). Classifying a parameter is one act of interpretation, and the build process that writes the prose already performs it.

## Decision

### 1. `origin` on a parameter

A parameter can carry an `origin` with a fixed value and, always, a `grondslag`: the provision that says who supplies it.

```yaml
parameters:
  - name: bevat_naam
    type: boolean
    required: false
    origin:
      waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE
      grondslag: een_bijzondere_wet#12 lid 2
  - name: is_ingeschreven
    type: boolean
    origin:
      waarde: REGISTER
      register: een_registerwet
      grondslag: een_bijzondere_wet#30
  - name: relevante_feiten_vergaard
    type: boolean
    origin:
      waarde: OORDEEL
      grondslag: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#3:2
```

| `waarde` | Meaning | Typical `grondslag` | Supplier in a process runtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BELANGHEBBENDE` | what the applicant supplies or chooses: the content of the application, the decision requested, the period | Awb 4:2 first and second paragraph; "de aanvraag bevat …" in the special law | the reduction of the application gram in the process's own cell; the period from the choice made in the portal |
| `DOSSIER` | a fact from the course of the case at the administrative body | Awb 4:5, 4:15, 3:41; the article of the act itself | the reduction of the own chronicle; facts that only arise after the decision, with their state at the decision |
| `OORDEEL` | a judgement the administrative body gives when it decides | Awb 3:2, 3:4, 3:46 | the decision form |
| `REGISTER` (with `register: <regulation>`) | a fact from a register kept by or under that regulation | a provision obliging the keeper to supply it, or the article of the register | a synthesis source of the process that supplies it (the consumer maps the source's name to its parameter), from a cell whose chronicle has a `grondslag` in that regulation |
| `KANAAL` | what the intake channel says: who logs in and on whose behalf | Awb chapter 2, section 2.3; the implementing policy for the channel | the session |

Values are Dutch and upper case, like `legal_character`. The `grondslag` uses the format of the event streams: `<regulation>#<article>`, optionally followed by ` lid <n>`. A parameter without `origin` stays valid. A parameter whose value a calling law passes in, which is most of the Awb, needs none.

`register` belongs to `REGISTER` and only to it: a `REGISTER` origin without one says nothing checkable about who keeps the fact, so it is an error, and so is a `register` on any other value.

There is no separate value for the period a decision covers. The applicant chooses it as part of the decision requested (Awb 4:2, first paragraph), so it is `BELANGHEBBENDE`. An earlier draft let the `grondslag` Awb 4:2 first paragraph mark the period as well. That gave one provision two meanings: the period, and any other part of the request that happens to rest on the same paragraph. The period is now marked on its own:

```yaml
  - name: subsidiejaar
    type: number
    origin:
      waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE
      grondslag: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2 lid 1
      rol: TIJDVAK
```

`rol: TIJDVAK` is allowed only with `BELANGHEBBENDE`. It is what the offer rule and the choice of periods in a portal look for; the `grondslag` no longer does that work.

### 2. Implementing policy can override

The law gives the default. An article of a regulation with layer `UITVOERINGSBELEID` may override it in `machine_readable`, again with a `grondslag`:

```yaml
machine_readable:
  origins:        # the body retrieves this itself, rather than asking for it
    - regulation: een_bijzondere_wet
      parameter: verdeeltabel
      origin: {waarde: REGISTER, register: een_registerwet, grondslag: een_beleid#4 lid 1}
```

For a process, the applicable origin is the one in `origins` of a loaded implementing policy whose `competent_authority` is the actor of the process; otherwise the one in the law. Two policies of the same actor that give the same parameter different origins are a load-time error.

### 3. What a process runtime does with it

- **Completeness check at start-up.** For every outcome the process executes (the check before submitting, the offer in the portal, and each outcome of the decision), every parameter the caller must supply has a supplier that fits its applicable origin (the table in §1). A required parameter without one is a start-up error that names the parameter, the origin and the `grondslag`. A parameter declared `required: false` without a supplier is a warning: the engine does not receive it and computes with an unknown value (RFC-036).
- **A wrong source is always an error.** A parameter that is supplied by something that does not fit its origin stops the runtime, also when it is `required: false` and also when another supplier does fit. `required: false` decides what the engine does with a missing value; it does not make a value from the wrong party acceptable.
- **The applicant or the case.** A process runtime can tell `BELANGHEBBENDE` from `DOSSIER` by what a derivation reads. A fact taken from grams the applicant submits (type `indiening`: the application, a supplement, documents) is the applicant's; a fact taken from other grams the body itself records (the course of the case) is the dossier's. A derivation that reads only the intake channel is `KANAAL`. An `OORDEEL` that any lexostatus supplies has a wrong source.
- **What cannot be checked is said.** A source cell reached over a URL, or one that does not run in the same runtime, counts as a supplier, with a warning per source that says what could not be verified.
- **Strict mode.** A process may declare `herkomst: streng`. A parameter without `origin` is then an error instead of a warning.
- **Form at load time.** An `origin` or `origins` entry that cannot be read, a `REGISTER` without `register`, `rol: TIJDVAK` outside `BELANGHEBBENDE`, a `grondslag` that cannot be parsed, and `origins` outside implementing policy are start-up errors that name the file, the article and the parameter. A `register` that is not a loaded regulation is an error; a `grondslag` in a regulation that is not loaded is a warning.
- **The offer.** The condition behind an offer in a portal may only read parameters with `KANAAL` or `REGISTER`, or `BELANGHEBBENDE` with `rol: TIJDVAK`. Anything else is not known before the application is filled in.
- **The decision form.** The `OORDEEL` parameters of the decision, labelled with their description (or the part after "Naam:"), grouped per article of their `grondslag`. The process configuration no longer lists them.
- **`required` stays engine semantics.** The runtime does not derive `required` from `origin`. It warns when a `BELANGHEBBENDE` parameter other than the period lacks `required: false`, because RFC-036 lets a law declare application-form fields that way. A register fact is normally required: a register always answers, if only with "not registered". A fact that legitimately does not apply (a paper-only field when the application came through a portal) is `nullable` and passed as `null`, not `required: false`.

The engine does not read `origin` or `origins`.

## Why

### Benefits

- The knowledge that is now repeated in configuration follows from the law, with a provision a legal expert can check (RFC-027). The build process that writes the prose writes the field.
- A process that forgets a supplier fails at start-up with the provision that says who should supply the value, instead of failing on the first case with "missing X".
- The offer rule becomes a check on declared origin, not an inference from which parameters a trace touched.

### Tradeoffs

- The classification is interpretation and can be wrong. The `grondslag` makes it checkable, not correct.
- `origins` in policy is a second place to look. It is limited to the actor's own implementing policy, and conflicts are errors.
- Existing corpora carry the prose. Converting it is a one-time mapping per phrasing, and phrasings that do not map cleanly stay without `origin`.

### Alternatives Considered

**Keep the prose and parse it.** Rejected: the prose varies per law and per build, and a parser for it would be a second, hidden vocabulary.

**Name the supplying cell in the law.** RFC-022 §4.2 lets `source.regulation` name a cell. Doing that for register facts couples a law to one deployment. `origin` names the regulation under which the register is kept; which cell keeps it is the runtime's business, named in the consumer's synthesis configuration.

**Derive `required` from `origin`.** Rejected: `required` decides what the engine does with a missing value (RFC-036), and a law may have reasons to require an applicant fact. A warning shows the mismatch without changing execution.

**A separate value for the period (`KADER`).** Rejected: the period is part of what the applicant asks for.

### Implementation Notes

- `packages/law-model` reads `origin` and `origins`. Serde ignores unknown fields, so older engines are unaffected. An entry that cannot be read is kept as written (`Declared::Invalid`), so a wrong `origin` never stops the engine from loading a law; the process runtime reports it with the file and the parameter.
- The law schema does not yet declare either field, and no released schema version is changed for this RFC before it is discussed. `parameterField` is open, so `origin` validates against the current versions without being checked; `machineReadableSection` is closed, so a law with `origins` fails schema validation until a new version declares it (RFC-013: `$schema` URLs are tag-based). The change that is ready for the next version, after discussion:
  - on `parameterField`: `origin`, an object with `waarde` (enum `BELANGHEBBENDE`, `DOSSIER`, `OORDEEL`, `REGISTER`, `KANAAL`), `grondslag` (string, pattern `^[a-z0-9_]+#[^#]+$`), optional `register` (a regulation `$id`) and optional `rol` (enum `TIJDVAK`), `additionalProperties: false`, with `if waarde = REGISTER then required register`, and `register` forbidden otherwise;
  - on `machineReadableSection`: `origins`, an array of `{regulation, parameter, origin}`, all required, `additionalProperties: false`;
  - a schema rule that `origins` appears only in a regulation with `regulatory_layer: UITVOERINGSBELEID` is not expressible per article and stays a runtime check.
- The completeness check, the offer rule and the derived decision form live in the process runtime (`packages/cel`).
- An `OORDEEL` value enters the decision with the official as its provenance. Recording it as an accepted value in the receipt (RFC-013 `accepted_values`) is not built yet.
