## Context

[RFC-001](/rfcs/rfc-001) removed the POC field `discoverable` with the reason "all outputs are public endpoints". That reason conflates two things.

Every output is **callable**: another law may reference it. True, and the basis of cross-law execution ([RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)).

Whether an output is **presentable**, something a portal should offer a citizen, is a separate question. RFC-001 did not answer it, and because nobody wrote down that it was a separate question, the demo corpus reintroduced `discoverable` and nobody could say what was wrong with it.

This RFC answers it.

## Decision

**An entry point is an article that produces a *beschikking*.**

The Awb draws the line. Article 1:3 defines a *besluit* as a written decision by an administrative body constituting a public-law legal act (lid 1), a *beschikking* as a besluit not of general scope (lid 2), and an *aanvraag* as a request by an interested party that a besluit be taken (lid 3).

Article 2 Wet op de zorgtoeslag gives the insured an *aanspraak*: a right claimed from an administrative body and settled by a beschikking. The Penitentiaire beginselenwet establishes when someone is detained, a fact another law consumes. Nobody applies for it; no beschikking follows. That is why the first belongs on a portal and the second does not, although the engine runs both for nearly everyone.

The schema has recorded this since v0.2.0, in `produces.legal_character`. No new field is needed. What follows is what that implies.

### 1. Tag `produces` from the statute

An article gets `legal_character: BESCHIKKING` when a beschikking results, one a *belanghebbende* can contest. An article that establishes a fact gets `INFORMATIEF` with `GEEN_BESLUIT`, the pattern Burgerlijk Wetboek Boek 5 already uses, or no `produces` at all.

The demo corpus does neither. It carries 77 `BESCHIKKING` tags against the main corpus's 8, because the POC migration stamped the value on nearly everything, including the Wet BRP and the Penitentiaire beginselenwet, which have no `produces` in the main corpus. Three laws are tagged `BESCHIKKING` and `discoverable: HIDDEN` at once: the law file says a decision comes out, the portal says hide it.

### 2. Derive the portal from `produces`

A law is an entry point when one of its articles produces a beschikking. A derivation over an index the engine already builds.

`hidden_laws` in `demo-config.yaml` stays, as a deliberate narrowing on top of what the law says rather than a second truth beside it. What a deployment shows is its own choice ([RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022), design principle 6).

### 3. Then remove `discoverable`, and judge what is left

Two things it does are not audience at all, and they need their own answer rather than a shared field:

- `findCase` uses it to decide whether a case is keyed on BSN or KvK number.
- `DELEGATION_PROVIDER` asserts that a law satisfies a fixed output contract, closer to what the Belastingdienst calls a *surface* in RegelSpraak than to anything else here.

The Kieswet is the known case that fits nothing: citizen-facing, no beschikking, because voting rights are a status. Whether that needs a construct or belongs in deployment curation is a decision for after the retagging, with the remaining cases as evidence. [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) rejected a `bezwaarbaar` field because RFC-008 already derived it; the same test applies here, and it cannot be applied to data that is wrong.

## Why

### Benefits

The distinction is the Awb's, not ours, and it removes a contradiction rather than adding a layer. `discoverable` exists because `produces` stopped discriminating in one corpus.

Most of the work is already right: huurtoeslag, kinderopvangtoeslag, AOW, WW and inkomstenbelasting all carry `BESCHIKKING` correctly today.

### Tradeoffs

Retagging 43 files is a legal reading, not a migration script, and a confident wrong call is worse than the present mess. Nothing keeps it correct afterwards either: the schema cannot tell a misapplied `BESCHIKKING` from a right one, so this rests on review, like the rest of the modelling.

### Alternatives Considered

**Alternative 1: Add `discoverable` to the schema.** Rejected. It answers three questions at once, and RFC-001 removed it for a reason that still holds once the reason is stated correctly.

**Alternative 2: Derive entry points from the dependency graph.** Rejected after testing. `wet_inkomstenbelasting` is referenced 36 times and is still something a citizen wants to see; the Penitentiaire beginselenwet is referenced 5 times and should stay hidden.

**Alternative 3: Detect it from the statutory wording.** Rejected. Article 42 Penitentiaire beginselenwet gives a detainee "recht op verzorging door een aan de inrichting verbonden arts", a right of a natural person. What separates the cases is whether the right is claimed from an administrative body and settled by a besluit, which no keyword captures.

**Alternative 4: Bind `produces` to the Uniforme Productnamenlijst.** Rejected, though the UPL corroborates this RFC: it lists zorgtoeslag with `Burger`, `Aanvraag` and a basis of article 2 Wet op de zorgtoeslag, the article named above, and has no entry for the Penitentiaire beginselenwet. But it is an administrative catalogue revised quarterly, and it points at the law. Having the law point back makes the norm depend on its own packaging. Useful as a cross-check, not as a source.

### Implementation Notes

The main corpus needs no change. The work is 43 files in `corpus/demo/regulation` that carry `BESCHIKKING` while being hidden or delegation-only, starting with `wet_brp`, `penitentiaire_beginselenwet` and the three tagged `BESCHIKKING` and `HIDDEN`.

Four places read `discoverable`: `portalLaws` and `findCase` in `frontend-demo/src/store/demoStore.js`, `delegationProviders` in `frontend-demo/src/data/delegation.js`, and a label in `ZaaksysteemView.vue`.

`service`, the other POC field RFC-001 replaced, is a separate cleanup with an existing answer in [RFC-002](/rfcs/rfc-002). All 81 demo laws still carry it; none carries `competent_authority`.
