## Context

A portal has to decide which laws to show a citizen. Zorgtoeslag belongs on the page; the Penitentiaire beginselenwet does not, although the engine executes it for nearly everyone because detention suspends the insurance zorgtoeslag requires.

The Awb already draws this line. Article 1:3 defines a *besluit* as a written decision by an administrative body constituting a public-law legal act (lid 1), a *beschikking* as a besluit that is not of general scope (lid 2), and an *aanvraag* as a request by an interested party that a besluit be taken (lid 3).

Article 2 Wet op de zorgtoeslag gives the insured an *aanspraak*: a right claimed from an administrative body and settled by a beschikking. The Penitentiaire beginselenwet establishes when someone is detained, a fact another law consumes. Nobody applies for it and no beschikking follows.

So: an entry point is an article that produces a beschikking. The schema has recorded exactly that since v0.2.0, in `produces.legal_character` ([RFC-001](/rfcs/rfc-001), [RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)).

The demo corpus does not use it. It carries `discoverable`, a POC v0.1.6 field that RFC-001 removed, which survives only because the schema's root has no `additionalProperties: false`. The field answers three questions at once (who the law is about, whether to display it, whether it fills a delegation contract), and it contradicts the law files beside it: `wet_bibob`, `alcoholwet/vergunning` and `alcoholwet/register_sociale_hygiene` are each tagged `BESCHIKKING` and `discoverable: HIDDEN`. The law says a decision comes out; the portal says hide it.

The cause is visible in the counts:

| `legal_character` | corpus/regulation | corpus/demo |
|---|---|---|
| BESCHIKKING | 8 | 77 |
| not a decision | 11 | 10 |

The main corpus is disciplined. The Penitentiaire beginselenwet has no `produces` there, and neither does the Wet BRP: a register records facts. The POC migration stamped `BESCHIKKING` on nearly everything, `produces` stopped discriminating, and `discoverable` was added to compensate.

## Decision

**1. Tag `produces` from the statute.** An article gets `legal_character: BESCHIKKING` when a beschikking results, one a *belanghebbende* can contest. An article that establishes a fact gets `INFORMATIEF` with `GEEN_BESLUIT`, the pattern Burgerlijk Wetboek Boek 5 already uses, or no `produces` at all.

**2. Derive the portal from `produces`.** A law is an entry point when one of its articles produces a beschikking. No new field: the engine already indexes this.

`hidden_laws` in `demo-config.yaml` stays, as a deliberate narrowing on top of what the law says rather than a second truth beside it. What a deployment shows is its own choice ([RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022), design principle 6).

**3. Judge afterwards whether a field is still needed,** citing the cases that remain. [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) rejected a `bezwaarbaar` field because RFC-008 already derived it; the same test applies here, and it cannot be applied to data that is wrong.

## Why

### Benefits

The distinction is the Awb's, not ours. `produces` records how the modeller read the statute, which is what the rest of the corpus already does.

It removes a contradiction rather than adding a layer. `discoverable` exists because `produces` stopped discriminating; repairing the cause is cheaper than maintaining a field that disagrees with the law file it sits in.

The five demo laws that matter most (huurtoeslag, kinderopvangtoeslag, AOW, WW, inkomstenbelasting) already carry `BESCHIKKING` correctly.

### Tradeoffs

Retagging is slow and needs a legal reading per article. A confident wrong call is worse than the present mess.

Two cases do not fit and stay open. The Kieswet is citizen-facing with no `produces` in either corpus, because voting rights are a status rather than a decision. And `DELEGATION_PROVIDER` is not an audience but a claim that a law satisfies a fixed output contract, nearer to what RegelSpraak calls a *surface*.

### Alternatives Considered

**Alternative 1: Add `discoverable` to the schema.** Rejected. It conflates three questions, and RFC-001 removed it for a reason that still holds.

**Alternative 2: Derive entry points from the dependency graph.** Rejected after testing. `wet_inkomstenbelasting` is referenced 36 times and is still something a citizen wants to see; the Penitentiaire beginselenwet is referenced 5 times and should stay hidden.

**Alternative 3: Detect it from the statutory wording.** Rejected. Article 42 Penitentiaire beginselenwet gives a detainee "recht op verzorging door een aan de inrichting verbonden arts", a right of a natural person. What separates the cases is whether the right is claimed from an administrative body and settled by a besluit, which no keyword captures.

**Alternative 4: Bind `produces` to the Uniforme Productnamenlijst.** Rejected, though the UPL corroborates this RFC. It lists zorgtoeslag with `Burger`, `Aanvraag` and a basis of article 2 Wet op de zorgtoeslag, the same article named here, and it has no entry for the Penitentiaire beginselenwet. But it is an administrative catalogue, revised quarterly, and a specification that draws meaning from it is no longer derived from the statute. The direction is also backwards: the UPL points at the law. It stays useful as a cross-check, not as a source.

### Implementation Notes

The main corpus needs no change. The work is in `corpus/demo/regulation`, starting with `wet_brp`, `penitentiaire_beginselenwet`, and the three laws tagged both `BESCHIKKING` and `HIDDEN`.

`frontend-demo/src/store/demoStore.js` reads `discoverable` in `portalLaws`, `isLawEnabled` and `findCase`, where it also decides whether a case is keyed on BSN or KvK number.

`service`, the other POC field, has an answer already: RFC-001 replaced it with `competent_authority`, designed in [RFC-002](/rfcs/rfc-002). All 81 demo laws still carry `service`; none carries `competent_authority`. Whether that cleanup rides along here is a scheduling question.
