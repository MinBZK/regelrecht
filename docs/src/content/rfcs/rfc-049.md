## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) maps the chronolexogram types onto RegelRecht: a decretogram is an engine output with `legal_character: BESCHIKKING` (§1.2), an executogram is declared in a chronicle-stream file (§1.3), and integration configuration that must touch a regulation is namespaced under `produces.extensions` (§3.2).

The chronolex proof of concept (branch `poc/chronolex`, not for merging) took §3.2 further. On 28 September 2026 the form of a gram moved into the article that establishes the fact, as a block `produces.extensions.chronolex`: the event name, the gram type (`type: decretogram`), the stage, the reference to another gram (`refers_to.on_application`), the moment that counts (`effective_at`), the fields, and aliases between parameters of two laws. The creator of Chronolexografie agreed that the form of a gram belongs with the article that establishes it. The compact cell of PR #1679 read the same block.

On 7 October 2026 the owner of this work asked what that block was doing in the law. Chronolexography is an implementation detail of RegelRecht: RegelRecht describes how a law works and what follows from it, and uses chronolexography to execute the law and record what follows. A reader of a rulework should not need to know chronolexography. What Awir 15 says is that an application arises; that we record it as a gram is our choice.

An inventory of the block showed that almost nothing in it could only stand there. Most of it follows from what the law already says in its own words (`produces.submission`, `decides_on`, the hooks, `origin`). The event name belongs to the stream. Two things were law content written in our words: the moment that counts, and that a parameter of a special law is the specific form of a parameter of a general one. Those needed a word of the law format.

## Decision

A rulework contains no chronolexography vocabulary: no event names, no gram types, no `produces.extensions.chronolex` block. The law says in its own words what arises, and the cell derives the record from it. Only a fictitious policy that records an execution still carries an explicit block, because the law format has no word for that yet (§5).

### 1. The test for a key

Can you explain the key to a jurist without the words gram, chronicle (*kroniek*), cell (*cel*) or chronolex? If you can, it is law and may stand in the rulework. If you cannot, it is implementation and belongs in the stream, in the policy of the holder, or in the code.

`produces.submission` passes: "here an application arises". `decides_on` passes: "this decision is taken on that application". An event name such as `aanvraag_ontvangen` does not, and neither does `type: decretogram`.

### 2. What the cell derives

| The law says | The cell derives |
|---|---|
| `produces.submission: {kind: AANVRAAG}` ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)) | a gram of type `submission`, subtype `aanvraag`, stage `AANVRAAG`. Its fields are the parameters with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL` ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)) of the article and of the hooks on it |
| a hook with `applies_to.submission` | the hook extends that submission: what it asks of the applicant becomes a field of the same gram |
| `produces.legal_character: BESCHIKKING` with `decides_on` | one decretogram per stage of its procedure that is a decision (the stage `BESLUIT`, or a stage that `is: BESLUIT`), referring to the submission as `on_application`. Its fields are the outputs of the article and of the hooks at that stage |
| `produces.legal_character: BESCHIKKING` without `decides_on` | the same decretogram, referring to nothing: a decision taken ex officio, such as the aanslag of AWR 11 |
| `produces.moment` (§3) | the moment that counts (`effective_at` of the gram) and its legal basis |
| exactly one date among the `requires` of a decision stage, or `produces.moment.parameter` | the parameter that is the date the decision bears (`dated_by`), which the cell fills with the day of the decision |
| `origin` with `rol: TIJDVAK` | the period the fact concerns |

A decision on more than one submission (`decides_on` with two entries) is refused: the cell could not tell which application the decision answers.

An article that decides at two stages of its procedure gives two decretograms, one per stage. Zorgtoeslagwet 2 decides at VOORSCHOT and at TOEKENNING of the procedure `tegemoetkoming` of the Awir. The stream then says, per event, which stage the event records (`stage: VOORSCHOT`). That is the only place a stage is named outside the procedure.

The name `on_application` and the gram types are vocabulary of the cell, not of the law, and this table is our own derivation. A cell built by someone else may derive differently from the same law.

### 3. `produces.moment`: the moment that counts

The law says when a fact counts. An application counts from its receipt (Awb 4:13 lid 1: the decision period runs from receipt). A decision counts from the day it is taken.

```yaml
# Awb 4:13, a hook on every application for a beschikking
produces:
  legal_character: TOETS
  decision_type: GEEN_BESLUIT
  moment:
    legal_basis:
      - algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13 lid 1
```

`moment` has an optional `parameter`, the parameter of the article or the requirement of the stage that is the moment, and `legal_basis`, the provisions that say why it counts. Without a parameter the administrative body establishes the moment itself: for an application, the moment it records the receipt. Reading Awb 4:13 lid 1 as the basis for "receipt counts" is an interpretation, and the rulework says so in a comment.

### 4. `specifies`: one datum, two legal bases

A special law often asks for something the general law already asks, in its own words. Awb 4:2 lid 1 onder a asks "de naam van de aanvrager"; the Wet op de politieke partijen, article 102 lid 3 onder a, asks for "de statutaire naam van de politieke partij". The applicant is the party (Awb 1:3 lid 3), so it is one datum with two legal bases: the special law says what the general term means here.

```yaml
- name: statutaire_naam
  type: string
  origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: 'wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102 lid 3'}
  specifies: {regulation: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht, article: '4:2', parameter: naam_aanvrager}
```

`specifies` on a parameter names the parameter of the general law it is the specific form of. A cell records one field for both. This replaces the aliases of the proof of concept's block.

[RFC-003](/rfcs/rfc-003)'s `open_terms` and `implements` do not fit: they fill a value the law leaves open, from a lower layer, while here the Awb leaves nothing open and the special law is not a lower layer.

### 5. An explicit block where the law has no word

A payment, a payment order or a bank's answer is neither an application nor a decision. The law format has no word for "an execution arises here", so the fictitious policies that record executions carry an explicit `produces.extensions.chronolex` block. It says what the derivation cannot: `type: executogram`, `refers_to` (by the stage or the article of the gram it belongs to), `fields`, `period`, and the keys of [RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050) for time and messages (`executed_on`, `record_when`, `until`, `identified_by`).

An explicit block always wins over the derivation. A block the cell cannot read is an error that names the article, so a typo cannot make a field silently disappear from a gram. In this repository only fictitious policies carry one.

### 6. What the stream says

A stream registers an event: its name, the article it `establishes`, and, for an article that decides at two stages, the `stage`. It also says what concerns this holder's way of working and not the law: which policy articles an event `reads` ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)), on which day the holder takes a decision (`decided_on`), and for a decision ex officio its period and subject ([RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050)).

### 7. Relation to RFC-022

This keeps RFC-022 §1.2 (a decretogram is an engine output with `legal_character: BESCHIKKING`) and its stage-splitting: each decision stage is its own gram. It narrows §3.2: `chronolex` is no longer a namespace on articles of law, and the `extensions` mechanism stays for integration configuration. It changes where an executogram is declared (§1.3): as an explicit block on the policy article that establishes it, with the event in a stream of the cell (`cells/<cell>/streams/`), where §1.3 had a separate `chronicles/` directory. RFC-022 gets a note pointing here.

## Why

### Benefits

- A rulework reads as law. A jurist reviewing Awir 15 or Zorgtoeslagwet 2 meets no recording vocabulary.
- Each fact the cell records rests on a key the law states, so a change in the law changes the record: a new hook on the application adds a field to every application gram.
- A different runtime can record the same law differently, because the law names no record format.

### Tradeoffs

- The derivation is code in the cell, not a declaration a reader sees next to the article. The demo shows the derived shape, and the [Zorgtoeslag as Chronolexography](/components/zorgtoeslag-chronolexography) page lists the rules, but the rules themselves are ours.
- Executions still need an explicit block, so the rule "no chronolex in the law" holds for law and not yet for every policy.
- `moment` and `specifies` are read by the law model and accepted by schema v0.8.0 (which leaves `produces` open), but not described in it.

### Alternatives Considered

**The explicit block on every establishing article** (the proof of concept, 28 September). It worked, and the creator of Chronolexografie agreed with the form of a gram living next to its article. Rejected on 7 October: it puts our recording vocabulary into the law, and almost all of it can be derived.

**`effective_at` and aliases as they were** (in the block). The content is law, the words were ours. Replaced by `moment` and `specifies`.

**A stage per article** (`stage: BESLUIT` in the block). The procedure already says which stages are decisions, since `is` on a stage ([RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008), Later additions). A stage per article could not express an article that decides twice.

**`legal_character: ANDERE_HANDELING` with `moment` for an execution.** A candidate for the missing word of §5; not chosen yet (Open Question 1).

## Implementation in this pull request

Built (PR #1689, on top of PR #1679): the derivation in `packages/cel` (`extension::derive`, `shape`), `moment` and `specifies` in `packages/law-model`, and the removal of every `extensions.chronolex` block from the law in `corpus/regulation` and `corpus/demo`. The fictitious policies keep their explicit blocks. No article in this repository uses `specifies`; the derivation does not read it yet.

## Open Questions

1. **A word for an execution.** What does the law format say for a fact that is neither an application nor a decision (a payment, Awb 4:87; a notice, Awb 3:41), so that executions need no explicit block?
2. **The schema.** A proposal that describes `produces.moment` and `specifies` in the schema is still to be written.
3. **A decision on more than one submission**, if a law ever takes one decision on two applications.
