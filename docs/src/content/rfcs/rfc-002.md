## Context

When representing laws and regulations in machine-readable format, we need to capture who has the authority (*bevoegdheid*) to make binding decisions.

### Observed Patterns

**In Wet op de Zorgtoeslag (BWBR0018451):**
- Article 1(1)(a) defines "Onze Minister" (responsible minister)
- Article 5 defines "Dienst Toeslagen" as executing authority

**In Regeling Standaardpremie (BWBR0050536):**
- The preamble names the Minister as issuer and executor, but the authority to set the standard premium (*standaardpremie*) comes from article 4 of the Wet op de zorgtoeslag. A ministerial regulation carries out a delegation granted elsewhere; the preamble records that delegation, it does not create it. This is the pattern described in [RFC-003](/rfcs/rfc-003).
- The delegation is narrow. Article 4 Wzt covers the standard premium only; the administrative-law premium (*bestuursrechtelijke premie*) of articles 18d and 18e of the Zorgverzekeringswet rests on its own delegation, in a different act, and is owed to the CAK.

**In Wet langdurige zorg (BWBR0035917):**
- Article 2.1.3: Sociale verzekeringsbank, insurance determination (*vaststelling verzekering*)
- Article 3.2.3: CIZ, indication decision (*indicatiebesluit*)
- Article 3.3.3: Zorgkantoor, personal care budget (*persoonsgebonden budget*)
- Article 4.2.1: Wlz-uitvoerder, duty of care (*zorgplicht*). An obligation rather than an authority. It produces factual acts (*feitelijke handelingen*), and nothing in the article grants the power to take a decision.
- Article 5.1.1: Zorginstituut Nederland. Chapter 5 is titled "Het Zorginstituut" and its first paragraph "Taken". The article charges the Zorginstituut with promoting lawful and efficient execution by the Wlz-uitvoerders and the CAK, and with the uniform interpretation of the insured package (*verzekerd pakket*). It is not supervision (*toezicht*) in the sense of chapter 5 Awb. That supervision sits with the IGJ, article 10.4.1, over the chapter 8 obligations of care providers.
- Article 6.1.1: CAK. Chapter 6 is titled "Het CAK" and its first paragraph "Instelling en taak". The article establishes the CAK as a legal person and settles its seat, the composition of its board, representation in and out of court, and the non-application of article 15 of the Kaderwet zelfstandige bestuursorganen. The tasks, and with them the authority, follow in article 6.1.2.
- Article 7.1.1: CIZ. Mirrors 6.1.1 lid for lid; the authority to take indication decisions is in article 7.1.2(1)(a).

### What Must Be Captured

One law can have **multiple** competent authorities for different actions. That is ordinary: different actions, different bodies. The model has to record which authority belongs to which article, because the law grants authority article by article. For example, a law might grant authority to:
- A minister for the delegated rulemaking the law provides for (see [RFC-003](/rfcs/rfc-003))
- An implementing body for individual decisions (*beschikkingen*), where the law attributes that power to it directly

Objection and appeal (*bezwaar en beroep*) are a separate question, handled in [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008); the courts are not administrative bodies and fall outside `competent_authority` altogether.

Additionally, some authorities are **categorical** rather than specific. For example, the College of Mayor and Aldermen (*college van burgemeester en wethouders*) applies to all 340+ municipalities, not a single entity.

### Attribution, Mandate, Delegation

Three routes lead to a body exercising authority, and they leave different traces in the law text.

**Attribution** (*attributie*) creates the authority and assigns it in one move. The law names the holder, so the article itself tells you who is competent. Article 5(1) of the Wet op de zorgtoeslag charges the Dienst Toeslagen with executing the act; the same holds for the *inspecteur* in the tax acts, and for the SVB, UWV and CAK.

**Mandate** (*mandaat*) leaves the authority where it is and allows another body to exercise it in the holder's name (art. 10:1 Awb). Legally the minister takes the decision. Mandate is arranged in a mandate decree (*mandaatbesluit*) rather than in the law, so it is normally invisible to a reader of the law text, and this is the common arrangement for implementing bodies: the IND decides on residence permits, while article 14 of the Vreemdelingenwet 2000 grants the power to Onze Minister. A law can also fence mandate in: article 7.1.2(4) Wlz allows mandate for indication decisions only to someone working under the CIZ's responsibility.

**Delegation** (*delegatie*) transfers the authority itself, and the receiving body exercises it under its own responsibility (art. 10:13 Awb). Delegation requires a statutory basis, so it is visible in the law text.

Where the law attributes, `competent_authority` names the implementing body. Where the law grants the power to a minister and an implementing body acts in mandate, it names the minister.

## Decision

### 1. Article Level

Define `competent_authority` at the **article level** (in `machine_readable`), not at document top-level.

A document-level field would have to be read off the preamble, which is the wrong place to look. The preamble of the Regeling Standaardpremie names the Minister without granting anything; the grant is article 4 of the Wet op de zorgtoeslag. Article level keeps the field where the law puts the authority.

### 2. Object Structure

`competent_authority` is an object with:
- `name`: name of the authority (required)
- `type`: enum `INSTANCE` or `CATEGORY` (optional, defaults to `INSTANCE`)

```yaml
# Specific authority (instance) - type can be omitted
competent_authority:
  name: "Dienst Toeslagen"

# Categorical authority - type must be explicit
competent_authority:
  name: "college van burgemeester en wethouders"
  type: CATEGORY
```

The first example names an implementing body because the Wet op de zorgtoeslag attributes execution to it. For a law that grants the power to Onze Minister, the name is the minister, whoever signs the decision in practice.

### 3. Type Definitions

- **`INSTANCE`**: A specific organization (Dienst Toeslagen, CIZ, CAK, Sociale verzekeringsbank)
- **`CATEGORY`**: A category that must be resolved per context (College of Mayor and Aldermen (*college van B&W*), municipal council (*gemeenteraad*), provincial executive (*gedeputeerde staten*))

## Why

**Article-level authority makes sense because:**
- One law can have 0..n competent authorities
- Different articles may have different authorities
- The law text itself defines where authority is granted

**Object structure with type because:**
- Distinguishes between specific instances and categories
- Explicit about what needs runtime resolution

**Skip identifiers (OIN, TOOI) for now because:**
- Multiple identifier systems exist
- Name is sufficient for MVP
- Can add identifiers later when requirements are clearer

## Open Questions

- How much of mandate and delegation (*mandaat/delegatie*) does the model need? Mandate is arranged outside the law and delegation inside it, which suggests only delegation has to be modeled. Two things complicate that. The Awb uses "delegatie" more widely than the Aanwijzingen voor de regelgeving do: article 10:13 Awb defines it as transferring "de bevoegdheid tot het nemen van besluiten", and afdeling 10.1.2 Awb governs the transfer of administrative authority generally, while the Aanwijzingen use the word for delegated rulemaking. A reader who knows one convention will misread the other. And a law can restrict mandate for a specific decision (art. 7.1.2(4) Wlz), which is a statement about the article that the model may want to record.
- How to handle `#` references for `competent_authority`? When `competent_authority: '#bevoegd_gezag'` references an output, the `type` (INSTANCE/CATEGORY) must be specified separately. This is awkward because the type isn't part of the law being modeled; it's metadata we're adding. Some laws designate the "bevoegd gezag" themselves. Paragraph 5.1.2 of the Omgevingswet, titled "Reikwijdte aanvraag omgevingsvergunning en aanwijzing bevoegd gezag", does exactly that for the environmental permit (*omgevingsvergunning*): article 5.8 makes the college of mayor and aldermen competent for a single-activity application, and articles 5.9 and following assign other bodies. Where a law does that, the reference points at a term the law already fixes, and the awkwardness shrinks to the laws that leave it implicit.
