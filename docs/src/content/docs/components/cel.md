title: "Cel"
description: "A proof-of-concept runtime for cells that record facts as chronolexograms and reduce them to lexostatuses, and for processes that combine those lexostatuses, run the engine and have a cell record the outcome."
---

The cell runtime is a small proof of concept for [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022). It keeps two things apart that the position paper keeps apart as well. A **cell** records facts as chronolexograms in its own append-only chronicle, and a reduction turns that chronicle into a lexostatus. That is all a cell does. A **process** acts: it informs itself by combining lexostatuses from cells (synthesis), draws conclusions by running the engine, and asks a cell to record what it decided.

This page describes the runtime as built. [RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) (draft) changes the design, and part of that change is built. Grams carry an id and refer to each other by id (RFC-045 §3), and a register can be queried through an article in its keeper's implementing policy that the deployment binds to a cell (`CELL_REGISTERS`, RFC-045 §1). The reduction language and the synthesis blocks below still run until the rest of the migration.

A process can have a portal: a fictitious applicant logs in through a simulated channel that the actor's policy declares, sees what the actor offers it, fills in an application form, checks it and submits it. The check asks the cell to reduce the draft on trial, then evaluates one outcome of a regulation (a `TOETS`) against that lexostatus, combined with what other cells know. A process can also have a counter, where an employee enters an application that arrived by another route, and a case handler: a fictitious employee who logs in, sees the cases, opens one and performs acts (`actions`) in it, each first on trial and then recorded by the cell: the decision (a stage decretogram), the later stages of that decision such as its notification (with the objection period the law then computes), a payment, and the facts of the course of the case, such as a request to supplement.

Cells are configuration and processes follow from the law, not from code. The runtime loads every directory under `CELLS_PATH` that has a `cell.yaml`, and derives one process per actor from that actor's implementing policy and three deployment files ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)). The same binary runs the generic test fixtures in this repository and the cells and processes of a private case corpus. Nothing in the code names a case.

## Overview

- **Language**: Rust (axum) for the runtime, Vue 3 with Vite and `@nldd/design-system` for the frontend
- **Location**: `packages/cel/` and `frontend-cel/`
- **Schemas**: `schema/chronolex/v0.3.0/` (`cell.json`, `stream.json`, `lexostatus.json`, `gram.json`, `synthesis.json`). `process.json` describes the earlier configuration; since RFC-047 no document has that shape, and `synthesis.json` reuses its definitions of a synthesis source and of the synthesis per row.
- **Run locally**: `just cel` starts the runtime on port 7170 with the fixture cells and processes, and the frontend on port 7171
- **Frontend checks**: `npm test -w cel` (vitest) and `npm run build -w cel` run in CI's frontend job; `frontend-cel/src/nldd-components.js` imports only the design-system components in use and is kept in sync by `script/check-nldd-imports.mjs`

## One runtime, cells as configuration, processes from policy

```
cells/
  intake/
    cell.yaml           # id, recording_actor, streams, lexostatuses
    lexostatuses.yaml
  register/
    cell.yaml
    lexostatuses.yaml
    initial_state.jsonl # optional: grams for an empty chronicle
deployment/
  channels.yaml         # per cell: per channel its adapter, labels and identification fields
  synthesis.yaml        # per cell: synthesis, assessment_rows, action_rows (until RFC-045)
  examples.yaml         # per cell: logins, application and a form per act, for a trial setup
```

The policy that declares the channels is an ordinary regulation under `REGULATION_PATH` (see "The process from policy"). Paths in `cell.yaml` are relative to its directory, paths in `examples.yaml` to that file. Each cell gets its own chronicle under `DATA_DIR/<id>/` and its own routes under `/cells/<id>/api/`; each process gets its routes under `/processes/<id>/api/`. `GET /api/cells` lists the cells with their chronicles and lexostatuses (inputs, parameters, extra fields). `GET /api/processes` lists the processes with their actor, their cell, their roles, whether they have a portal or case handling, and their synthesis sources. The frontend reads both lists. With one process that has a portal, it opens that process directly. A case handler can open the chronicle and the lexostatuses of each cell their process reads; the frontend reads them through the process, because a cell's own read routes need a token (see below).

A cell has no login, and its routes are not open. Recording a gram and a trial reduction are reserved for the processes of the runtime itself: at every start the runtime makes a random token, the internal transport sends it in the `x-cell-runtime-token` header, and the cell answers 401 without it and 403 with another one (deviation 37). Reading a chronicle, a case or a lexostatus needs the same token, or the read token of `CELL_READ_TOKEN` in the `x-cell-read-token` header, which runtimes that may read each other share: the grams carry the identity and the intake of whoever submitted them. Only the stream definitions are open. A case handler reads the cells of their process through the process (`GET .../api/inspection/<cell>/...`), and an applicant sees the gram of their own submission. `CELL_CHANNELS` is optional: without it, the runtime serves only cells.

The boundary between cell and process is real. A cell only sees its own grams. A process never reads a whole chronicle and never filters or interprets grams itself: filtering and deriving are reduction, and reduction happens in the cell. What a process needs to know about a case, such as which stages are in it or what the decision recorded, comes from the lexostatus `case_state` that the runtime offers for every cell with cases (see "The state of a case"). The process asks the cell for the grams of one case (`GET cases/<root>`) only to show a case handler the file, and passes them on as they are. It reaches every cell through a `Transport`, including the cell it records in, which is to the process a source like any other. The transport is internal when the cell runs in the same runtime (the same call as the HTTP route, through the router, without a network) and HTTP when a synthesis source has a `url`. This follows RFC-022 §4.3, where the runtime chooses the transport. In this step a process only records in a cell of its own runtime; reading may go either way.

## Four layers, and the process above them

The design keeps four things apart. The regulation does not know that a chronicle exists, and the chronicle does not know which article reads it. The reduction is the only place where the two meet. The first layer belongs to no one; a cell holds layers 2 to 4. The process stands above them, reading lexostatuses and asking the cell to record.

1. **Lexogram.** The regulations under `REGULATION_PATH`, unchanged and shared by all cells. An article declares the parameters it needs, such as `bevat_naam` or `aanvraagdatum`.
2. **Stream definition.** Which facts the cell records, by which actor, in which chronicle and on which legal ground (`legal_basis`). Each field binds to `$intake.*` (who submitted it and through which channel), to `$external.*` (the content as submitted) or to a constant of the stream. A table field declares its columns: `{table: $external.<path>, columns: [...]}`. An event may refer to other grams. `refers_to` names each reference after the law text (`on_application`, `application`, `decision`, `amends`, `concerns`), says what the referenced gram must be (`to`: an article that establishes it, an event name, or `{stage: <STAGE>}`) and whether the process must supply it (`required`). An event that names the article establishing it (`establishes`) takes its references, type, stage, legal ground and fields from that article (`produces.extensions.chronolex` in the law), and the stream then says only which cell records it and through which intake. What an event is to a case is derived, not configured. An event whose grams other events can refer to, and that refers to nothing itself, opens a case (the application). An event with a reference follows one, and any other event stands alone. An event with stage `BESLUIT` is a decision, and with an `amends` reference a decision that changes another (see "The state of a case"); an event whose reference can only point to a decision follows that decision, as a notification or a payment does. An event may bind the moment at which its fact legally holds to a submitted value, with a legal ground: `effective_at: {source: $intake.<path> | $external.<path>, legal_basis: [...]}` (see "Time"). This follows the sketch in RFC-022 §1.3.
3. **Reduction to lexostatus.** A `lexostatus_definitions` entry narrows the chronicle with `filter` (on a field of the gram itself, such as `name`, `stage`, `id`, `root` or a reference `refers_to.<name>`, so a lexostatus can select what follows one decision in a case, or on a field path under `fields`) and, when it needs one gram, picks it with `pick: latest`. Each parameter gets a derivation (`derivation`), of one of two kinds. On the chosen gram: `field`, `filled`, `equals`, `table` with `each_row` or `one_row` (optionally `only_where`), and `moment` (`effective_at` or `recorded_at`). On the chosen gram there is also `year_of`, the year of a date, and `period_of`, the period a date falls in, written as the first day of that period (`2025-03-17` in a month is `2025-03-01`), so the engine reads a period as a date. `period` says which: `year`, `quarter` or `month`. Without it the runtime takes, at start-up, the `temporal.period_type` (RFC-001: `year`, `month`) of the parameter the derivation supplies, from the articles of its legal ground: the law says whether a period is a month or a year, not the cell. Over the grams that pass the derivation's own `filter`: `exists: true` (optionally with `filled: <field>`, so only a gram with that field filled counts), `sum: <field>`, `collect` (a list with one row per gram, see deviation 31), and `pick: latest` with `field`, `year_of`, `period_of`, `moment` or `contains: {field, value}`. `pick: latest` with `field`, `year_of`, `period_of` or `moment` may add `no_gram: <value>`, the value when no gram passes the filter, such as `null` for the date of something that did not happen. Field paths use dots, as in `content.organen`.

   With `group_by: root` the lexostatus is a **list**: one row per case with at least one gram through `filter`, and, with `without: {filter}`, no gram through that filter. `pick` and the derivations then work per case, and the derivations are columns rather than parameters. Without `pick`, a case is ordered by its first gram, so a list without `filter` can show every case with how far it has got (`exists` on a stage, `sum` of the payments). A list is meant for the consumer, such as a case handler with a work queue, and never goes to the engine.
4. **The gram.** What the cell writes, one JSON line per gram in `DATA_DIR/<cell>/<chronicle>.jsonl`. A gram is appended and never changed; a correction is a new gram. It carries `kind`, `type`, `subtype`, `stage` (for a stage decretogram, an application or an act that brings about a stage, such as the notification), `name`, `chronicle`, `recording_actor`, `legal_basis`, `effective_at` (when the fact legally holds or happened), `recorded_at` (when the cell recorded it), `stream {id, sha256}` and `fields`, and `provenance: initial_state` when it was placed at start-up. When the event bound `effective_at` to a submitted value, the gram also carries `effective_at_legal_basis`. A gram of an act the engine computed carries `inputs` and `receipt`, and a decision also `legal_character`, `decision_type`, `regulation`, `regulation_valid_from` and `competent_authority` when the regulation names one (see "Performing an act"). Every gram carries an `id`, a UUID v7 the cell gives it under the write lock, so ids rise in time. When its event refers to other grams, the gram carries `refers_to` with the id of each. The root of a gram is the gram without a reference that its references lead to, such as the application. It is not part of the gram: the chronicle keeps an index from id to root, and a case is named after its root. `effective_at` and `recorded_at` must be moments with a time zone. A chronicle from before chronolex v0.2.0, with `zaakkenmerk` and `besluitkenmerk` and without `id`, is not converted: the runtime refuses to load it and asks for an empty data directory. The file is the source; the runtime also keeps the grams in memory with an index per id and per root, so a reduction or a case query does not read the file again, and nobody but the runtime writes to it. A line counts only once it ends with a newline: an incomplete last line (the runtime stopped while writing) is cut off at start-up with a warning, and an unreadable line before it stops the runtime from starting (nothing is cut off then). Every write starts at the length the cell knows, so the remains of an earlier failed write are overwritten. Readers do not wait for the disk; only writing waits for fsync.

The process holds everything about acting: who logs in, what the portal offers and checks, which sources it combines, and the decision. It never builds or reduces a gram itself. It sends the cell the event, the intake (who submitted, through which channel) and the external content, and the cell builds the gram from its stream, validates it, checks that the process's `actor` is the stream's `recording_actor`, checks that the gram fits its case, and records it or reduces it on trial. The cell enforces the form of a gram and never its content: whether an act is right is a conclusion of the process, drawn before it acts. The position paper leaves the agreements on which facts can be recorded open, as a question for research ("eisen aan de vorm, zonder de inhoud te beperken"); this split is our own choice (RFC-044 §1, deviation 43).

A submitted application is recorded with `type: submission` and `subtype: aanvraag`, and its event opens a new case (`case: opens`). For that subtype, `gram.json` fixes `fields.core` to the elements Awb 4:2 paragraph 1 asks of every application: the applicant's name and address, the date, the decision requested and the signature (`signed_via`). The content a specific regulation asks for goes under `fields.content`, which each stream defines. A field left empty is recorded as `null`, so an incomplete application is recorded as well. Completeness is a judgment, and the gram holds none.

A decision of a register keeper (an entry, a removal, an established result) is recorded as `type: decretogram`. It belongs to no case: an election result or a register entry is not a case, and its event refers to no other gram. The grams are grouped by chronicle instead. A cell can keep several chronicles, one per stream, and each lexostatus reduces one of them.

## The process from policy

There is no process file. Since [RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047) the runtime derives one process per actor from three things: the implementing policy (`UITVOERINGSBELEID`) of the actor's competent authority, the cells, and the deployment files.

The policy declares the channels in `produces.extensions.chronolex` of an article, together with what a channel supplies to an application (`supplies`) and the authorities the actor acts for under mandate (`mandates`):

```yaml
extensions:
  chronolex:
    channels:
      <channel>:
        kind: portal | handling | counter    # decides the routes of its role
        role: <role>                         # optional; default the channel id
        identifies: {<field>: [<legal basis>, ...]}   # or a list of field names
        owner: <field of the submission>     # who follows a case
        submits: <regulation>#<article>      # exactly one channel per actor: the portal
        assesses: {output: <output of that article>}
        offers: {regulation, output, deadline?, windows?, start?, opening?}
        form: {document: <path from the corpus root>, screen: <screen>}
        legal_basis: [<regulation>#<article>, ...]
    supplies:
      <channel>: {<field of the gram>: <field of the channel> | $channel | $submitted_on}
    mandates:
      - {authority: <name>, legal_basis: <regulation>#<article>}
```

The process id is the id of the cell that records the portal's submission. Its actor is the `recording_actor` of that stream, and it acts on behalf of the policy's competent authority. The portal event is the one submission event, over all cells, that the `submits` article establishes. The check before submitting reduces the one lexostatus of that cell that reads the portal event and derives a parameter of the `submits` article. The work queue is the built-in list `worklist` (see "Acts in a case"). Anything the derivation cannot settle uniquely (no portal event or two, two assessment lexostatuses, two channels with `submits`) stops the runtime with a message that names the policy article.

A policy article names the article of law it works out with `executes` (RFC-047), optionally with `as`: `fact_finding`, `interpretation` or `weighing`, the three subjects of a beleidsregel in Awb 1:3 lid 4. The explanation of a form field ("Waarom?") shows it as a step with the term of the Awb and its source, as in "voert wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102 uit (vaststelling van feiten, Awb 1:3 lid 4)". Without `as` the step reads "voert wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102 uit".

The deployment keeps what is technique rather than law. Each file is grouped under the cell id, because one runtime can run several actors with the same channel names:

- `CELL_CHANNELS` (`channels.yaml`): per channel its adapter (only `simulated` exists), the label and explanation of the login screen, an optional `intake` prefix, the `role_label`, and the identification fields with their label, `pattern`, `check`, `message` and `numeric`. The name of a field is its key, and its legal ground comes from `identifies` in the policy. A channel of the policy without an adapter, or an adapter without a channel, is an error.
- `CELL_SYNTHESIS` (`synthesis.yaml`): the synthesis sources, the synthesis per row of the check (`assessment_rows`) and of each act (`action_rows`, under the act's name), in the shape described under "Synthesis". At start-up the file is validated against `synthesis.json`. It goes with the migration of RFC-045.
- `CELL_EXAMPLES` (`examples.yaml`): the examples of a trial setup (see "Examples").

## Channels and roles

A channel is a simulated login: a label, an optional explanation for the login screen, and its identification fields. Every channel is simulated. A field is valid when its trimmed value matches `pattern` as a whole (without one: not empty) and, with `check: elfproef`, passes the check digit test of a citizen service number (nine digits weighted 9 down to 2 and -1, the sum divisible by 11). Nothing is checked against a register and no certified login takes place; the login screen says so. Whether a person may act for an organisation is for the policy regulation to decide, through synthesis with a register cell. The `legal_basis` of a channel in the policy is the rule that says with which means and on whose behalf someone logs in, which also grounds the owner field; the ground `identifies` gives a field is the rule that knows the value and its form, such as the number a register assigns. Each names a loaded article, checked at start-up like the ground of a role.

A channel delivers its fields to the cell under `$intake.<prefix>.<field>`, with `$intake.channel` naming the route of intake. When a portal has more than one channel, the intake carries the paths of every portal channel, with `null` for those of the channels the user did not log in through, so each channel supplies every path the event binds. The start-up check refuses an event of the portal that binds an `$intake` path no portal channel supplies. `owner` in the policy names the field of the submission that makes someone the owner of a case; the runtime finds the field of the login that carries it, through the `$intake` path the submission binds or through what the channel supplies. An applicant who follows up on a case must have a gram in it whose field bound to that path carries their value. A portal whose event follows a case (`case: follows`) needs an owner field on every portal channel.

Every channel has one role, named by `role` in the policy (default: the channel id), with the route group its `kind` gives: `portal` (form, check, offer, submission), `handling` (work queue, case, and the acts in it) or `counter` (see "The counter"). Which routes belong to a group is the runtime's; which role may use them is the policy's. A route answers 401 without a session and 403 to a role without its group. A portal needs a role with `portal` and the other way around, and the same holds for `handling`. The legal ground of a role is the first `legal_basis` of its channel, such as a mandate or a service policy; a decision carries it with the acting actor. A process has one session cookie, so logging in as another role replaces the session. A session expires after eight hours without use, and a process keeps at most ten thousand; a login beyond that pushes out the one unused the longest. When more than one role logs in through a channel, the login names the role (`role`). A process without roles has no login at all.

## The counter

An application that arrives by another route (on paper, at a desk) is still an application (Awb 4:1), and its receipt counts in law (Awb 4:13). A role with routes `counter` enters it: `POST .../api/counter/application` with `{applicant, received_at, external}`. `aanvrager` identifies the applicant with the fields of a portal channel (with `channel` when the portal has several); nobody logs in with them. The cell records the gram in the event of the portal, with `$intake.channel: counter`, the applicant's fields under the channel's intake paths, `entered_by` with the role and identity of the employee, and the day of receipt at the `$intake` path the event binds its `effective_at` to. That binding is required: a process with a counter whose portal event binds `effective_at` to nothing under `$intake` does not start. The receipt may not lie after today (the cell refuses that too), and when the offer's policy names `offer.opening` (an outcome of the same regulation with the period as its only parameter), it may not lie before the opening of the period the application asks for; the period comes from the application field the check reads it from.

## Acting on whose behalf

RFC-022 §2 keeps three axes apart, and so does the runtime:

- `recording_actor`: who records. The `actor` of the process, which must be the `recording_actor` of every stream it records in. An identifier.
- `competent_authority`: who the law makes competent, from the article or else the regulation. A name.
- the acting actor: who acts in the process, in a role, through a channel, on behalf of an authority.

The process acts on behalf of the competent authority of the policy that declares its channels. At start-up the name must occur in a loaded regulation. The runtime compares names literally; an identifier is never read as a name. The same authority makes the overrides of implementing policy count (see "Who supplies a parameter").

A process may act for another authority under a mandate (Awb 10:1): `mandates: [{authority, legal_basis}]` in the policy, where `authority` occurs in the law and `legal_basis` names a loaded article. A mandate from the process's own authority is an error.

## Acts in a case

The acts a case handler can perform in a case follow from the streams and the law. Every event in the actor's cell whose intake names a channel of kind `handling` is an act, in the order of the streams, and the act is named after its event (the route is `cases/<root>/actions/<name>`). A follow-up event that belongs to more than one decision, such as a notification, gives one act per decision, named `<event>_<decision event>`. The label is the readable event name with the regulation and article, such as "Besluit genomen (Testregeling afnemer, artikel 3)". The article of an act:

- for a decision (stage `BESLUIT`), the article that establishes the event; its outcomes are the `$external` keys of the event that are outcomes of that article;
- for a follow-up stage, the article of its decision;
- for a fact, the article of an implementing policy that executes an article of the event's legal ground with a parameter of origin role `BESLUIT` (see "Who supplies a parameter"), or else the article whose outcomes depend on the lexostatuses that read the event. With more than one candidate, the one in the regulation of a decision of the actor wins; when more than one is left, the runtime does not start. Without a candidate the event is no act, with a warning.

`decision` of a fact or a change is the act of the decision its reference points at. The work queue is the built-in list lexostatus `worklist`, offered for every cell with a submission: the cases of applications on which not every requested decision has been taken. With one deciding article (`decides_on`, or else the one article that establishes a decision in the chronicle) a gram of stage `BESLUIT` takes the case off the list. With more than one the list keeps every case, because the reduction language cannot yet require a decision gram per article. The columns are derived, not configured: `received_at`, `recorded_at`, the owner field of the portal channel, and the field of the submission whose parameter has origin role `TIJDVAK`. Next to it the runtime offers the list `cases`: every case of the same submission, decided or not, with the same columns and `decided_at`, the date of the latest gram of stage `BESLUIT` (null without one; a chronicle without a decision event has no such column). The handler reaches a decided case through it, for the announcement or the payment that follows. Both names are reserved. In a list, a derivation over a collection reads every gram of the case, not only the grams through the filter of the list. The lexostatuses of the case are the sources with `case: true` in the synthesis, plus every lexostatus the law reads in the cell.

What an act needs and from whom is not configured. It follows from the stage of its event (RFC-008) and from the `origin` of the parameters of its article (RFC-048), and the event decides the kind of act:

- **The decision.** The event has a stage that the procedure of the article's legal character knows (`BESLUIT`), and it is the earliest such act on that article. Its form is every parameter with origin `OORDEEL`. A case can hold several decisions, each of its own article: an advance, a final determination, a recovery. Each is an act whose event has stage `BESLUIT`. A decision that changes another one (Awb 4:48 and 4:49 for a subsidy determination) is a decision of its own, with its own article and legal ground; its event has an `amends` reference to the article of the decision it changes. The process then acts on the latest decision of that article in the case, a change of it included, unless the case handler names another one of those decisions with `decision`, the id of its gram. When the law leaves an outcome of a change empty (`null`, a change without a ground for changing), the process does not take it.
- **A follow-up stage** of the same decision, such as the notification (`BEKENDMAKING`). Its event refers to the decision (`decision`, to `{stage: BESLUIT}` or to the article of the decision), and it continues on the latest decision in the case that the decision act of its article recorded. The engine runs that stage on the inputs and outcomes of the recorded decision (RFC-008 `execute_stage`: the decision is the state container, the process supplies what the stage asks). Its form is what the stage asks (`requires`), with the type the procedure gives. The hooks the law fires on that stage (RFC-007) add their outcomes; see "Notification and legal protection".
- **A fact** from the course of the case (a request to supplement, a receipt, a suspension, a payment): the event has no stage. A fact that executes a decision, such as a payment or a repayment, has an event whose `decision` reference points at the article of that decision (RFC-022 §3.3 `references_decision`); it waits until that decision is in the case. Its form is the `$external` fields of the event that are not outcomes. On trial, the cell reduces the lexostatuses of the case as if the fact were already recorded (the trial reduction of deviation 32), so the outcomes show what the fact does. The type of a field comes from the parameter a derivation of the cell makes of it, or, for a field that only binds `effective_at`, a date.

The portal, the work queue, the acts and the sources with `case: true` name one cell. A source with `case: true` is a lexostatus of the case itself, with `root` as its only input: an act asks the cell for it with the id of the case's root and takes all its parameters and extra fields. Such a source names no inputs or parameters. The other synthesis sources an act asks are the ones that supply a parameter of its article, and those that pass an input on to them or to its synthesis per row; a payment does not ask the registers an application needs.

The process finds its decision in the law: the derivation sets the article and the regulation of every act (see above), and no configuration names them.

Every parameter of an act comes from exactly one source: a lexostatus of the case (provenance `own`), another synthesis source, the synthesis per row, the form (provenance `handler`), or what has not happened yet. That last one covers facts that can only arise after the decision, such as the notification. It is not configured: the procedure of the decision's legal character (RFC-008, `procedure` with stages and `requires`, in the Awb) names in a later stage what only arises then. Every parameter of the decision that a later stage requires and that no lexostatus of the case derives, has not happened at the moment of deciding: a boolean is `false`, anything else `null`, with provenance `state_at_decision` and the stage. A parameter a lexostatus of the case derives, such as the day of notification read from the notification gram, needs no such rule: no gram means it did not happen (deviation 9). A recording event whose stage the procedure does not know is a start-up error.

An act on trial (`POST .../cases/<root>/actions/<name>/trial`) runs the article on the peildatum (see "Time"). The answer has every outcome that has a value, and `takeable` when all of them have one, every test holds and every fact of the form is filled in; otherwise `reason` says why ("niet te nemen: mist <parameter>", "vul in: <field>", or which test says no). `reportable` says the reason is about content, not form: a test says no, or a hook gives no value. An outcome the law cannot compute completely (a value or a source is missing) is a matter of form: then nothing is recorded, not even when reported, because the inputs and the receipt would be incomplete. The process then does not act of itself, but a case handler who reports that the fact happened (`happened: true`) has it recorded (see "Performing an act"). A decision is never reported; the process takes it. A date bound to `effective_at` after today or before a gram the act refers to (see "Time") is a matter of form. It names the provenance of each parameter and, under `not_delivered`, every parameter a caller of the article has to supply that no source supplied. Nothing is recorded and nothing is filled in. `GET .../cases/<root>` gives the grams of the case (for the file, uninterpreted), the procedure with the stages that are in the case, the legal protection that follows, and per act whether it can be performed, its form and a trial without a form. Everything but the grams comes from the `case_state` of the cell and the law. A stage act can be performed while its stage is not in the case, a follow-up only once the decision is; a fact always, and its trial says what it would do. So the case view shows what is still to be paid: the trial of a payment without an amount. It shows each decision in the case (`decisions`) with the procedure of its article, the stages it went through, its legal protection and the acts that now act on it; per act, `decision` names the decision the act would act on.

## The state of a case

Every cell with an event that opens or follows a case offers one lexostatus that no `lexostatuses.yaml` declares: `case_state`, with input `root`, the id of the root gram. The case is the group around that root: the application and every gram that refers to it, directly or through another gram. The cell takes the grams under the root and derives, as extra fields that never go to the engine:

| Field | Holds |
|---|---|
| `grams` | the number of grams in the case; a process sends it back as `root_grams` when it records |
| `events` | per `<stream>/<event>` the number of grams |
| `latest_effective_at` | the latest `effective_at` in the case, for information; no bound is taken from it (see "Time") |
| `stages` | for the stages that belong to no decision (such as `AANVRAAG`), per RFC-008 stage the gram that brought it about: `stream`, `event`, `effective_at`, `recorded_at`, `regulation`, its fields (`fields`) and the values of its inputs (`input`) |
| `decisions` | the decisions in the case, in the order the cell recorded them: per decision its `id` (that of the decision gram), the `stream` and `event` that recorded it, `amends` for a change (the id of the decision it changes), its `stages` (as above; the stage of the decision itself carries its outcomes and inputs) and `events`, the number of grams per event that follow it |
| `owner` | only with the inputs `owner_path` (an `$intake` path without `$intake.`) and `owner`: whether a gram in the case binds that path to that value |

A process reads it for everything it needs to know about a case: which acts are available, the recorded decision a follow-up stage continues on (the RFC-008 state container, from the stages of that decision), the legal protection route of each decision (the hook outcomes its last stage recorded), and in the portal whether the applicant follows up on a case of their own (the `owner` of their channel). The runtime offers it rather than each cell declaring it, because the root, the references, the decisions in a case and one stage per decision are the runtime's concepts, not a corpus's; a process would otherwise depend on each cell repeating the same derivations under the same name (deviation 46). An unknown case is a 404; `as_of` and `known_at` work as for any lexostatus.

The cell checks the references of a gram under the write lock. Every gram a reference names must be in the cell's chronicles and fit what the reference may point to, and all of them must share one root; otherwise the cell refuses with 400. Each decision goes through each stage once: a second gram with the same stage that refers to the same gram is refused (409). A second decision of the same event that refers to the same gram is refused too (409): that would be a second decision on the same application, and changing a decision takes a decision of its own with its own legal ground, an event with an `amends` reference. Which decisions there are, and on which ground, is the law's and the stream's; the cell enforces only this form (deviation 47).

## Who supplies a parameter

A parameter can say who supplies it, per the law, with `origin` and always a `grondslag` (RFC-048): `BELANGHEBBENDE` (what the applicant supplies or chooses), `DOSSIER` (a fact from the course of the case), `OORDEEL` (a judgment given when deciding), `REGISTER` with `register: <regulation>` (a fact from a register kept under that regulation) and `KANAAL` (what the login says). The period of the decision requested carries `rol: TIJDVAK`, with `BELANGHEBBENDE`. The parameter that receives the decision an act works on carries `rol: BESLUIT`, with `DOSSIER` (RFC-047): the runtime fills it with the id of that decision, so a policy that reads per decision, such as a payment administration, can be called by an act. An article of an implementing policy (`UITVOERINGSBELEID`) can override the origin of a parameter of another regulation with `origins` in `machine_readable`. For a process, the override counts when the competent authority of that policy is the authority the process acts for; otherwise the law's origin applies. Two overrides that disagree are a start-up error.

At start-up the process checks every parameter a caller must supply for the check, the offer and every outcome of the decision. Its applicable origin needs a supplier of the fitting kind. Which kind a derivation of the own cell is follows from the events its filters can let through: grams of type `submission` are what the applicant submits, other grams of the process's actor are the course of the case.

| Origin | Supplier |
|---|---|
| `BELANGHEBBENDE` | a derivation of the check lexostatus or a lexostatus of the case that reads only submissions (`type: submission`), or synthesis per row from such a table; for the offer also the period the portal offers, when the parameter has `rol: TIJDVAK` |
| `DOSSIER` | a derivation of such a lexostatus that reads only other grams of the actor, or the state at decision (for the decision only) |
| `OORDEEL` | the form of an act (a decision or a fact, not a follow-up stage); nothing else may supply it |
| `REGISTER` | a synthesis source that supplies the parameter (under the name the process's synthesis gives it), and whose chronicle has a stream with a `legal_basis` in the regulation of `register`; or synthesis per row from a table such a source passes on |
| `KANAAL` | a derivation of the check lexostatus that reads only `$intake` fields; such a derivation may also supply a `BELANGHEBBENDE` parameter, since the applicant submits the intake as well |

A supplier that does not fit the origin is always a start-up error ("verkeerde bron"), even when the parameter is `required: false` and even when another supplier does fit. A required parameter without any supplier stops the runtime too. Both messages name the parameter, the origin and the `grondslag`, and where the value comes from now. A parameter declared `required: false` without a supplier is a warning: the engine does not receive it and computes with an unknown value (RFC-036), so an outcome that reads it becomes unknown. A `BELANGHEBBENDE` parameter that is not `required: false` is a warning as well, except the period (RFC-036 lets an application-form field be declared that way; the runtime does not derive `required`). A parameter without `origin` is an error, since who supplies it cannot be traced (RFC-047 made this strict for every process).

What the runtime cannot inspect counts as a supplier, with one warning per source that says what could not be verified: a source with a url, and a source without one whose cell does not run in this runtime. A `register` that is not a loaded regulation is an error; a `grondslag` in a regulation that is not loaded is a warning.

Loading the corpus checks the form of every `origin` and `origins` entry, in every regulation, before any process: a value the law model cannot read (an unknown `waarde`, a misspelled key), a `REGISTER` without `register`, a `register` with another value, `rol: TIJDVAK` outside `BELANGHEBBENDE`, a `grondslag` that cannot be parsed, and `origins` outside implementing policy each stop the runtime with the file, the article and the parameter. The law model keeps such an entry as written, so the engine itself still loads the law; it does not read `origin`. The runtime logs the warnings after start-up.

## Synthesis per row

An article can ask for a table as a parameter of which the applicant fills in only part; the authority establishes the rest itself, row by row, from registers other cells keep. The process does not assemble that table in code. `rows` says which table field (of a lexostatus of the case, or passed on by a source) supplies the rows, which column travels under which name, and which source is queried per row with which input.

Per row the process walks the sources in order, so a source can use a column an earlier one supplied. An input comes from the row (`column`), from a lexostatus of the case (`lexostatus` and `field`), from the merged parameters (`parameter`), from the law (`regulation` and `output`), or is a fixed value (`value`). An input from the law is an outcome of a regulation that the process computes once, before the rows, with the merged parameters and on the date of the check or the decision: the law derives the input, such as the reference date of a figure (the first of January of the year of the election), and the configuration converts nothing. When the regulation cannot compute it, the source is not asked and the reason says what the law was missing. The result lists the values under `from_law`. A source supplies a column from its `parameters` or from its `extra_fields`, so a lexostatus that only supplies columns of someone else's table parameter supplies no article parameter at all.

When an input is missing, a source is unreachable, or it does not supply the value, that column stays out of the row and `missing` names it. Nothing is filled in, and the engine then reports the parameter it could not compute. A source must answer with a lexostatus (at least `name` and `parameters`); anything else is an error of the source, not an empty lexostatus. A table field that is not a list of objects yields no table at all (with `error` in the result) rather than a row that silently drops out. The rows are queried concurrently (at most sixteen at a time), each walking its sources in order, and the table keeps the order of the table field.

The check before submission takes the same block under `assessment_rows` in `synthesis.yaml`. There the table comes from the trial reduction of the draft (the check lexostatus) or from a source that passes it on, and the check builds the rows before the engine runs, as the decision does. A `rows` block supplies only the evaluation it belongs to: in the provenance check, the rows of the check count for the check and those of the decision for the decision.

## Performing an act

`POST /processes/<id>/api/cases/<root>/actions/<name>` computes the same thing and has the cell record it, in the event of the act, with its references: a reference that can only point to a decision gets the id of the decision the act acts on, and every other reference gets the root of the case. Each `$external` key of the event is an outcome or a field of the form. The decision records all its outcomes; a follow-up records what its stage asks and what the hooks computed. Every act the engine computed carries `inputs` with every parameter's value and provenance ([RFC-013](/rfcs/rfc-013) `accepted_values`) and a `receipt` holding the loaded regulations and the cell's streams with a SHA-256 over both. A decretogram also carries what makes a decision a decision: `legal_character` and `decision_type` from the article's `produces`, `regulation` and `regulation_valid_from`, and `competent_authority`. The process assembles these, because the process runs the engine, and sends them with the event to `POST /cells/<cell>/api/grams`; the cell builds the gram from its stream and records it.

For a decision and a follow-up, the competent authority comes from the regulation (the article, otherwise the regulation itself) and is tested against the authority the process acts for. Equal means record. A different authority means refuse, unless `mandates` names it; then the process records under that mandate. When the regulation names none, the process records with a warning and without `competent_authority`. Nothing makes one up. Every act's gram carries `acting_actor`: the role, the channel and the identity the user entered, the role's `legal_basis`, and for a decision `on_behalf_of` (the authority in whose name it is taken) and, under a mandate, `mandate` with its legal ground.

An executogram records a delivery that took place (position paper: "de vastlegging van daadwerkelijke levering"). The process concludes before it delivers: when the article of the act is in the `legal_basis` of the event and produces a `TOETS`, every boolean outcome whose `legal_basis` is that provision (the article, and the paragraph when the ground names one) must hold for the process to act of itself. The runtime asks these outcomes in the same run; `assessments` in the answer shows them. A judgment or a finding is not a delivery, so this does not apply to other types: what it works out on its ground is exactly what it records.

A conclusion is not a refusal of the fact. When the trial says no on content (`reportable`), the case handler may report that the fact happened anyway: `POST .../actions/<name>` with `{form, happened: true}`. The cell records the gram, the answer carries the reason as a warning, and the lexostatuses of the case show the consequence: a payment above the decided amount counts in the amount paid, and the law then says what was paid without being owed; a notification that does not meet Awb 3:41 is recorded without an objection period. A decision is not reported (400), and a report does not pass what is a matter of form.

These lead to a refusal with 409 and no gram: the act is not to be taken and was not reported as having happened (or cannot be, because the form is missing something), the cell refuses because a gram with the same stage is already in that decision (or, for a stage without a decision, in the case), because a decision of the same event already refers to the same gram (changing it takes an event with an `amends` reference), its `effective_at` lies on a day before a gram it refers to, the case changed since the process read it, or the law names a different authority without a mandate. The process sends the number of grams the case had when it read it (`root_grams`), and the cell records only while that still holds, under the same lock as the write: what the process computed, such as what is left to pay, held for the case as it was then, so two simultaneous payments cannot both pass. The gram is validated against `gram.json` before it is recorded.

## Notification and legal protection

The notification is a follow-up stage of the decision. Its event has stage `BEKENDMAKING` and type `act` (the sending is not a decision and not a delivery), and the facts of that stage really happen when it is recorded: the date and the manner of notification, from the form. The engine runs the decision's article in stage `BEKENDMAKING` on the recorded decision's inputs, with those facts, and fires the hooks the law declares on that stage. The law derives the legal protection route (RFC-022 §3.3), not the configuration: in the case corpus, Awb 6:8 paragraph 1 is a hook on stage `BEKENDMAKING` of a `BESCHIKKING` that computes the start (the day after notification) and the end of the objection period of Awb 6:7, and leaves both empty when the notification was not made in the prescribed manner (Awb 3:41). An act whose hook gives no value is not taken of itself; reported as having happened, it is recorded with the empty period, so no objection period runs. The notification gram records the route; a lexostatus of the case can reduce it (the route per chronolexogram of RFC-022 §3.3).

`GET .../cases/<root>` shows it per decision, as `legal_protection` in `decisions`: after the last stage of that decision, the next stage of the procedure when no act records it (such as `BEZWAAR`, which runs of itself after the notification), with the hooks of the last stage as `legal_basis` and their outcomes from its gram. An objection against the final determination or the recovery therefore has its own period, next to that of the advance. Filing an objection and deciding on it are not acts yet.

## Payment

A payment is a fact of the course of the case, recorded as an executogram. The amount is a field of the form; the article that judges it is the one that says how a decided amount is paid (in the case corpus Awb 4:52 paragraph 1: "overeenkomstig de subsidievaststelling"). A lexostatus of the case derives the decided amount from the decision gram and the amount paid as the sum over the payment grams; on trial the new payment counts. The law then says what is left to pay, and whether the payments together are in accordance with the decision. A payment above the decided amount is not, and neither is a payment on a decision that is not yet in force (Awb 3:40, read by Awb 4:52 in the case corpus). That test is on the ground of the event, not in the configuration, and the process does not make such a payment of itself. When one was made anyway and is reported, the cell records it, and the law then says how much was paid without being owed (in the fictitious fixtures `onverschuldigd_betaald`; in the case corpus the matching outcome of Awb 4:52).

## Start state

A cell may name a `initial_state`: a JSONL file of grams to put into an empty chronicle. Each line gives `stream`, `name`, `effective_at` (set by hand: when the decision or finding legally holds), `provenance: initial_state` and `fields`. A line has no `recorded_at`: that is the load time, the moment the runtime puts the start state into the empty chronicle, so a start state never claims the cell knew something earlier than it did. A line whose `effective_at` lies after the load time stops the start, as for any gram: what has yet to happen is not a fact. A decision that takes effect later is placed on the day it was taken, with its date of effect as a field. A line may give an `id` and `refers_to`, to an earlier gram of the start state under the names of its event. Without an `id`, the gram gets a fixed id derived from the line, so every load gives the same one. Type, subtype, legal ground, chronicle, actor and the hash of the stream come from the stream, so a start state does not go stale when the stream changes. The fields must be exactly those of the event. The runtime loads the start state only when every chronicle of the cell is empty, and writes each file in one go (a temporary file, then renamed), so an interrupted start leaves no half file; the next start removes a leftover temporary file. For a start state over several chronicles this holds per file: a start that stops between two renames leaves the other chronicles empty, and the runtime does not fill them in afterwards.

## Time

A gram has two times (position paper, "Het chronolexogram": "Op 3 april heeft de gemeente-ambtenaar vastgesteld dat ... per 2 april"):

- `effective_at`: when the fact legally holds or happened. By default the moment of recording. An event can bind it to a submitted value, always with a legal ground, and the gram then carries that ground as `effective_at_legal_basis`. The binding says per event who states the legal moment:
  - `$external`: the acting actor, as part of the act. The date of a decision is the case handler's judgment (the decision gram's `effective_at` is that date, not the moment of recording), as are the day of notification under Awb 3:41, the day of a payment and the day of a statement under Awb 4:15. The case handler can therefore record such an act later than it happened.
  - `$intake`: the channel, when whoever submits the fact may not choose the moment. An application that arrives by another route (on paper) is the example: the counter gives the day of the date stamp (see "The counter"), the receipt counts in law (Awb 4:1, 4:13: the decision period runs from receipt), and the portal never supplies the value, so an applicant cannot backdate.

  The value is a date (the start of that day) or a moment with a time zone, within two bounds the cell enforces: never later than the recording, because what still has to happen is not a fact, and, for a gram that refers to another, never on a day before the `effective_at` of a gram it refers to, because what follows moves forward in time (a notification before its decision, or a decision before its application, is refused with 409). The trial of an act applies the same rule before it acts: a date before a gram the act refers to (the root of the case, or the decision it acts on) is held back, and a later fact of the case the act does not refer to does not bind it. The comparison is per day, since a bound date is the start of its day and the fact before it may have been recorded later that day. The trial of an act reports both bounds before anything is recorded.
- `recorded_at`: when the cell recorded the gram, always the cell's own clock; for a start state the load time. The cell stamps it under the write lock of its chronicle, and never earlier than the line before, so the order of a chronicle file is the order of `recorded_at`. A gram without a bound `effective_at` gets the same moment as its `effective_at`.

`pick: latest` picks in time: the latest `effective_at`, on a tie the latest `recorded_at`, and then the gram added last. A fact recorded late with an earlier `effective_at` is therefore not the latest.

A reduction can be read at an earlier moment, the "time travel" of the position paper ("Historische integriteit en betrouwbaarheid"): facts are never removed, so every earlier state can be recovered. `GET .../lexostatus/<name>` takes two optional query parameters, each a date (`JJJJ-MM-DD`, the whole day counts) or a moment with a time zone:

- `as_of` (valid time): the state as it legally held at T, with everything the cell knows now. A gram counts when its `effective_at` is on or before T.
- `known_at` (transaction time): the state as the cell knew it at T. A gram counts when its `recorded_at` is on or before T.

Both together give the bitemporal question ("what held on 1 March, as known on 5 March"). Without either, every gram counts, including a fact that only takes effect later. The answer echoes the peil. These names are reserved: the schema refuses a lexostatus input called `as_of` or `known_at`. The trial route takes them in `inputs`; the draft counts as recorded now.

The process passes a peil wherever the moment matters. An act reads every cell at the peildatum on which the engine reads the regulation, so a fact that only takes effect after the act does not count, and the same trial gives the same state later. The peildatum of an act comes from its event: when the event binds `effective_at` to a field of the form, with a legal ground, the act is peiled on that day (the date of the decision, of the notification, of the payment); otherwise on today. The date of the decision is a judgment of the case handler (`OORDEEL`), and the decision gram's `effective_at` is that date, not the moment of recording. A date after today or before a gram the act refers to is held back, as above. The check before submitting reads the sources as of today. The offer for a period that has yet to start reads them as of its first day, so a strike-off effective before that period counts, instead of the registers of today standing in for a year that is still to come. Which day that is, the policy says: `offer.start` names an outcome of the offer's regulation, computed with only the chosen period as parameter (for the case: 1 January of the subsidy year, the peildatum of Wpp art. 1). Without `start` the offer reads the sources as of today. The synthesis per row passes the same peil to every source it asks.

## Examples

For a trial setup the deployment may name standard data per action in `examples.yaml` (`CELL_EXAMPLES`), under the cell id of the process: `logins` (a list of JSON files, each with the fields of a channel, and optionally `channel` (default: the only channel of the portal) and `role`; the label is the file name without extension), `application` (a JSON file with `{external: {...}}`, as the portal receives it) and `actions` (per act a JSON file with `{form: {...}}`, as the act receives it). A form value `"$today"` becomes today's date when the examples are served, since a decision or payment dated in the future is not a fact and a fixed date grows stale. Paths are relative to `examples.yaml`, and an act is named after its event. `GET /processes/<id>/api/examples` returns `{logins: [{label, channel, role?, fields}], application, actions}`, with `null` for an application without an example, and needs no login, since the login examples are there to log in with. The frontend offers each example to fill in the form first or to perform the action with it directly. An example is ordinary input: it is checked like any other and nothing about it is recorded.

## Synthesis

The check of a portal first asks its cell to reduce the draft on trial, to the assessment lexostatus the derivation found (see "The process from policy") (`POST /cells/<cell>/api/lexostatus/<name>/trial`). The cell builds the gram in memory and reduces its chronicle with that gram added, so the answer is what the lexostatus would be if the draft were recorded; nothing is recorded. Then the process asks each other source in `synthesis` for a lexostatus, with inputs taken from that one:

```yaml
synthesis:
  - cell: <source cell id>
    # no url: the source runs in this runtime, so the transport is internal
    lexostatus: <name at the source>
    input:
      <source input>: {lexostatus: <check lexostatus>, field: <parameter or extra field>}
      <other input>: {value: <fixed value>}
    parameters: [<name>, ...]   # explicit, no wildcard; the same name at source and consumer
    # or: parameters: {<name at the source>: <name of the consumer's parameter>}
    legal_basis: [<regulation>#<article>, ...]   # what the translation rests on
```

A source speaks the language of its own regulation. A register cell names a fact as the register regulation names it, per article (`is_ingeschreven_in_register`); the consumer asks for it under the name of its own article (`is_ingeschreven_gemeenteraad`). The translation belongs to the consumer: `parameters` as a map from the name at the source to the consumer's parameter. The source may supply that name as a parameter or as an extra field: whether a fact is a parameter is up to the consumer's law, not the source. A fixed input (`value`) lets one lexostatus serve several questions, such as the register of each body. A translation is a reading of the law, so a source can say what it rests on with `legal_basis`: the consumer's article that asks for the fact, and the source's article that supplies it or that the fixed input names (the register of the House of Representatives, say). A source per row carries it the same way. Each ground names a loaded article with the paragraph it gives; every source that translates (a name at the consumer that differs from the name at the source, or a fixed input) must carry one, and a source without is a start-up error that lists what it translates.

A value that is needed as input but is not a parameter of any article, such as the name the applicant registered under, is an `extra_fields` entry of the check lexostatus. It is marked apart and never goes to the engine.

A source can also pass a value on to a later source. It lists the field under `extra_fields` (and may then have no parameters at all), and the later source names that source's lexostatus as the origin of its input:

```yaml
synthesis:
  - cell: <register>
    lexostatus: <by organisation number>
    input: {nummer: {lexostatus: <own>, field: nummer}}
    parameters: []
    extra_fields: [aanduiding]         # not a parameter; input for the next source
  - cell: <register>
    lexostatus: <by name>
    input: {aanduiding: {lexostatus: <by organisation number>, field: aanduiding}}
    parameters: [<name>, ...]
```

The synthesis then runs in rounds: first the sources that need only the check lexostatus (or, for a decision, a lexostatus of the case), then each source whose inputs an earlier round passed on, and so on; a chain such as organisation number, then name, then designation, then result is three rounds. When the earlier source passes nothing, the later one is not asked (`not_queried`), and nothing is filled in.

Each source gets three seconds. The check takes only the listed parameters from the answer and passes the merged set to the engine. Its answer shows, per parameter, where the value came from: the lexostatus of the draft or the case (`own`), or cell and lexostatus with the transport. Per source it shows the status: `queried`, `unreachable`, `error`, or `not_queried` when an input was missing from the draft. Nothing from the synthesis is recorded; it informs the check and is not a fact of anyone. When a source is unreachable and the outcome therefore cannot be judged, the check says "niet te beoordelen: bron <id> onbereikbaar". It does not fill anything in.

## What an organisation can apply for

Nobody lists which applications an organisation can make, not in the law and not in the configuration. The actor decides what each portal offers, and on what grounds, in a policy regulation of its own. `offers` on the portal channel names one outcome of that regulation, and optionally a second outcome of the same article that gives the deadline. When the article asks for a period, the parameter with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` and `rol: TIJDVAK` (part of the decision requested, Awb 4:2 paragraph 1), `offer.windows` names the outcome of the same regulation that lists the periods on offer. The runtime computes it in a run without parameters on today's date: which periods the portal offers is policy, not configuration. `GET /api/possibilities` builds a draft with only that period, adds what the login supplies, runs the synthesis, and evaluates the regulation in one run per period. When the check lexostatus derives the period from a field of the draft, the runtime fills in that field, so the provenance is the same as for a real application; otherwise the chosen value goes in with provenance `choice`.

The offer tests only conditions that are settled beforehand: who logs in, what the registers know, and the period asked for. Whether all the facts an application needs will be there cannot be known before the form is filled in; that is the check after filling it in (completeness) and then the decision. So the verdict is simple. True (or a positive value) means an application is possible (`possible`). A definite zero or false means the policy offers nothing (`excluded`). Everything else is `undeterminable`: an empty outcome, a missing fact, an engine error. The verdict looks only at the offer outcome itself; a fact the deadline needs does not count.

The runtime refuses to start when the offer could lean on something that is not settled beforehand. Every parameter the article of the offer outcome asks of its caller must have origin `KANAAL` or `REGISTER`, or `BELANGHEBBENDE` with `rol: TIJDVAK`. The `grondslag` Awb 4:2 paragraph 1 alone does not make a parameter the period: other parts of the request rest on that paragraph too. Otherwise, or without an origin, the start-up check says "aanbod: voorwaarde leunt op '<parameter>' (<origin>), dat vooraf niet bekend is". The check works per article rather than per outcome, because the engine evaluates the whole article for any outcome of it. A period without `windows`, `windows` without a period, more than one period, a `windows` outcome that does not exist, and one from an article that asks for a parameter are start-up errors too.

The regulation decides who may act. The login states only what its channel asks, such as an organisation number and a person's name; whether that person may act for the organisation comes from a register cell through synthesis, and the policy regulation reads it like any other fact. The deadline is shown next to the verdict and never changes it.

No offer is not a refusal. An application sent another way (Awb 4:1) is still recorded and judged. Nothing of this is recorded.

The frontend shows one button per period to start an application, and fills in the field of the period when the application form opens. It is disabled unless the verdict is possible. A question mark next to it opens the verdict, the reason, the deadline and the trace of the run.

Every outcome the portal shows that comes from an engine run carries the trace of that run: the check before submitting, the trial decision, and the offer. The answer carries it as text (`trace_text`, the same box-drawing rendering the editor shows), and the frontend opens it from a small RegelRecht icon.

## Start-up checks

Each cell and each process is checked on its own. When one fails, the runtime does not start, and every message starts with `cel '<id>':` or `proces '<id>':`.

For a cell:

1. `cell.yaml`, the stream definitions and the lexostatus definitions validate against their schemas. The lexostatus file belongs to this cell, and every stream has the cell's `recording_actor`. Every legal ground names a loaded article, and a paragraph it names exists in the article text.
2. Every derivation points at a parameter of an article in the `legal_basis` of an event its filter selects, or of an article in its own `legal_basis`, and at field paths that exist in that event. A derivation can carry a `legal_basis`: the articles its reading rests on, such as the article that asks for the fact or the register the cell keeps ("no gram is no"). Each of them is a loaded article with the paragraph it names, as for an event. A `table` derivation points at a table field and reads only columns the stream declares for it. A derivation on the chosen gram needs a lexostatus with `pick`.
3. No orphaned field: each field of each event is read by a derivation or a filter, or is listed under `not_reduced` with a reason.
4. No name collision: a parameter gets one derivation.
5. Every reference of an event can point to an event of the cell: its `to` names an article some event establishes, an existing event, or a stage some event has.
6. The start state fits the streams of the cell.
7. The `without` of a list lexostatus (`group_by: root`) selects at least one event, since otherwise it would never leave a case out. Every gram has a root, so `filter` and `without` may select any event. Its columns need not be parameters and do not collide with other lexostatuses.
8. A lexostatus supplies something: at least one derivation or one extra field.
9. No lexostatus is called `case_state`: the runtime offers that one.
10. A `period_of` without `period` finds exactly one `temporal.period_type` (`year` or `month`) for its parameter in the articles of its legal ground or of the events it reads.
11. A stream declares no `case` or `decision` (the stream schema): both follow from the stage and the references of an event.

For a process:

1. The derivation from policy settles one portal channel, one portal event, one assessment lexostatus and an article for every act, and every channel of the policy has an adapter in `channels.yaml` and the other way around (see "The process from policy"). The portal, the acts and the sources of the case name one cell, and that cell runs in this runtime. What a policy `executes` is valid and stays within the competence of its own authority (RFC-047, Awb 4:81).
2. The `actor` is the `recording_actor` of every stream the process records in: the stream of the portal and those of the acts. The authority of the policy is one a loaded regulation names, and every mandate names an authority in the law with a legal ground that is a loaded article.
3. Every channel has unique fields, patterns that compile and an owner that resolves to one field of the submission. The first segment of its intake prefix is not `channel` or `supplied`: the runtime reserves those names under `$intake` for the route of intake and for what the receiving channel supplies; the `legal_basis` of a channel or a field names loaded articles, and so does that of a role. The route groups match the portal and the acts (see "Channels and roles"), and a counter needs a portal event that binds `effective_at` under `$intake`, with a legal basis for a stated moment (`effective_at.legal_basis`). Without that basis the law only explains why the moment of recording counts, so the counter cannot state the day of receipt.
4. The portal names an existing event of that cell that binds only `$intake` paths the portal's channels supply, a lexostatus that reads that event and picks a gram (not a list, with no input other than `root`), and a regulation outcome whose article is in the `legal_basis` of the event. An offer names an outcome that exists, and a deadline outcome from the same article, because the two are evaluated in one run. Every parameter the article of the offer asks for has an origin that is settled beforehand, and a period has `windows`, an outcome of the same regulation from an article without required parameters (see "What an organisation can apply for").
5. Synthesis needs a portal or an act. Each input comes from a field of the check's lexostatus or of a lexostatus of the case, or from an extra field an earlier source passes on; that earlier source takes its own inputs from one of those. A source supplies a parameter or passes an extra field. Each parameter is a parameter of the article of the check, the decision or the offer, or of an article one of them calls, transitively through `source`. A parameter comes from exactly one place. An ordinary source is a cell other than the process's own; the own cell is read through sources with `case: true`. The `legal_basis` of a source (and of a source per row) names loaded articles, and every source that translates has one.
6. A source of the case needs case handling. The work queue is a list. Act names are unique, and an act's `role` is a role with route group `handling`. The outcomes of an act come from one article (for a follow-up also from the hooks of its stage). The lexostatuses of the case have `root` as their only input and are not lists. Each parameter in the form, in the state at decision or in a `rows` block is one a caller of the article has to supply, and every parameter of an act comes from one source only. A stage event's stage is in the procedure of the article.
7. Synthesis per row: the table comes from a lexostatus of the case, or from a source that passes it on; each column name comes from one place only (the table or one source); a `column` input names a column something before it fills; a source is another cell. Where an act is recorded: an existing event that follows a case. A decision's event records every outcome and nothing that is not an outcome or a judgment; a follow-up's event records what its stage asks and the outcomes of the hooks. A decision records in an event with stage `BESLUIT`, a change in one with an `amends` reference; a follow-up in an event whose reference points at its decision; a fact whose event refers to a decision acts on that decision, and another fact on none. An amount in an act's form (a fact or a judgment with `type: amount`) names its unit with `type_spec.unit` in the regulation; without it the frontend cannot tell euros from eurocents.
8. Once synthesis and acts pass: every parameter of the check, the offer and each outcome of every act (other than a follow-up) has a supplier that fits its applicable origin and none that does not, and the overrides in implementing policy do not conflict (see "Who supplies a parameter"). Before that, loading the corpus checks the form of every `origin` and `origins` entry.
9. Each example file exists and is JSON of the right shape: a login has valid fields for its channel (and a role of that channel, when it names one), an application has an `external` object, an act has a `form` object. An example for an action the process does not have (a login without roles, an application without a portal, an act the process does not name) is an error.

Whether a source is reachable and offers the lexostatus with those parameters and inputs is checked after start-up, through `GET /api/cells` at the source. A problem there is a warning, not a refusal: the source may come up later.

## Frontend

`frontend-cel/` is a Vue 3 app on `@nldd/design-system`, with `@regelrecht/frontend-shared` for API calls, amounts and the trace button (`TraceKnop.vue`). An amount is shown and entered in the unit its parameter names (`type_spec.unit`), so a eurocent amount is entered in euros and sent to the law in eurocents (`frontend-cel/src/form.js`). The law names the columns of a table but not their types, so an amount column takes its unit from the form file (`eenheid`, `unit` in the API).

Two things fall outside the design system. The map of a process (`MapView.vue`, `MapNode.vue`) draws its graph with `@vue-flow/core`, since NLDD has no graph component; it imports vue-flow's own stylesheet. And there is custom CSS for it, built from NLDD tokens:

- `MapNode.vue` (`.map-node`): a plain `<button>` as the node, 220px wide, with padding, font size, colors, border and radius from NLDD tokens, `overflow-wrap: anywhere`, `pointer-events: auto` (vue-flow turns pointer events off on nodes), and a `:focus-visible` ring from NLDD tokens.
- `MapView.vue` (`.map`): `height: 75vh`, because vue-flow needs a parent with a height, and the NLDD body font.

No other view or component has a `<style>` block.

## Deviations from RFC-022

These are deliberate. [RFC-044](/rfcs/rfc-044) (draft) now records the design behind most of them: the split between cell and process, synthesis at the consumer, the stream and gram fields, the grouping of grams into a case, the decisions in a case with one stage per decision, two times per gram, what a recorded act carries, the runtime token and the configuration format. RFC-022 is amended where it contradicted them (deviations 6 and 12). The list stays as the record of where the runtime and the first version of RFC-022 differ, and was checked against the code as of 25-09-2026. Deviations 14, 15, 38 and 47 follow the gram ids and references of chronolex v0.2.0 (29-09-2026).

The split between cell and process follows the position paper, which gives a cell three functions (recording, one or more chronicles, and reduction, which "always takes place in the cell where the chronolexograms concerned were recorded, at the request of a business process") and gives informing, concluding and having a cell record to the process ("synthesis happens at the consumer, not at the source"). RFC-022 agrees where it speaks ("the cell never does cross-cell work; it only reduces its own chronicles", "the cell is not the engine"), but it leaves the configuration of a cell to a later RFC and says nothing about how a process is configured. Deviations 7, 32 and 33 fill that gap.

1. **Type `submission` with `subtype: aanvraag`**, instead of an executogram. The position paper leaves the typology open, and RFC-022 §1 calls the set of types "not a closed set".
2. **`legal_basis` is a list, and may name a paragraph.** An application rests on several articles at once; RFC-022 §1.3 has a single value. An article can hold more than one application or act, so a legal ground may name the paragraph: `<regulation>#<article> lid <n>`, such as `een_wet#102 lid 1`. Article numbers can contain a space (`kieswet#G 1`, deviation 13), so the paragraph is read after the last ` lid ` and only when a paragraph number (digits, optionally one letter) follows. A paragraph has no parameters of its own: the checks on derivations and on the portal's outcome stay per article. The start-up check only tests that the paragraph exists, as a line of the article text that starts with `<n>.` or `<n> `.
3. **The format of `lexostatus_definitions`.** RFC-022 §4.1 sketches it and calls it provisional. This runtime fills in `reduction` with `chronicle`, `filter`, `pick`, `derivations` and `extra_fields`, adds derivations over a set of grams, each with its own filter, and adds `year_of` for an article that asks for a year where the chronicle holds a date.
4. **`core` and `content`, constant fields, and `$intake.*`** next to `$external.*`. Who submitted and through which channel belongs to the intake, not to the content.
5. **`not_reduced`** in the stream, for the orphaned-field check.
6. **A separate schema directory**, `schema/chronolex/`, instead of the `chronicle.json` that the first version of RFC-022 §1.3 placed in `schema/v0.6.0/`. Resolved: RFC-022 §1.3 as amended puts the chronicle schemas in `schema/chronolex/`.
7. **Two kinds of configuration: `cell.yaml` and `process.yaml`.** RFC-022 leaves the full cell configuration to a future RFC and has no process configuration at all. Here `cell.yaml` holds only what a cell does (its actor, streams, lexostatuses and start state), and `process.yaml` holds who acts and how: channels, roles, portal, synthesis, case handling with its acts (`handling`, `actions`) and examples, with an `actor` that must be the `recording_actor` of every stream the process records in. The own cell is a source of the process like any other, marked `case: true` where it supplies the lexostatuses of a case. The configuration, not the code, decides which outcome the check evaluates and where its other facts come from. `process.json` is added to the chronolex schemas (then v0.1.0) and `cell.json` shrinks, without a new schema version, because the proof of concept has not been released. Since [RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047) (2 October 2026) there is no process configuration: the process follows from the actor's policy, and the deployment keeps only technique.
8. **Decretograms from a start state, without an engine trace.** RFC-022 has a decretogram come out of the engine, with a trace. A start-state gram is placed, not computed, and says so with `provenance: initial_state`. Having the engine produce these decisions from the articles that ground them is a separate step.
9. **Absence in the own chronicle reads as "no".** The position paper does not say how a reduction reads absence. `exists` and `contains` are false and `sum` is zero when no gram passes the filter: a cell speaks only about its own register, closed within its own chronicle. A value that has no gram at all, such as a date, stays out, unless the derivation names a value for that case with `no_gram` (such as `null` for the date of something that did not happen).
10. **No security context between cells.** A lexostatus is queried over plain HTTP, without signing and without authorization of one organization to another (RFC-022 §2 and §4.3). There is no FSC and no Blauwe Knop in between; a shared read token (`CELL_READ_TOKEN`, deviation 37) only keeps the reading routes off the open network.
11. **One cell per kind of register keeper.** In practice each body can have its own register keeper. The runtime allows any number of cells; a proof of concept can start with one.
12. **The consumer translates** (`parameters` as a map in the synthesis). A regulation that keeps a register names a fact generically per article (`is_ingeschreven_in_register`), while the article that consumes it names the same fact per body (`is_ingeschreven_gemeenteraad`), or asks for a fact no article of the register regulation models at all. Until 25-09-2026 the source spoke the consumer's names through `levert_aan`; that put another authority's law in a register, which deviation 23 rejects for the reference date. Now the source speaks its own regulation's names, a fact without a parameter in that regulation is an extra field, and the consumer maps both to its parameters in its synthesis (the paper puts synthesis with the consumer). The first version of RFC-022 §4.2 put the cell in the law (`source.regulation: <cell>`); here the consumer's configuration names it, and RFC-022 is amended to match (RFC-044 §2).
13. **An article number may contain a space** (`een_wet#A 1`), because the corpus numbers some articles that way.
14. **A case is a group of grams joined by references.** RFC-022 §1.2 uses a case identifier only to group the stage decretograms of one decision, and neither the law nor the position paper introduces a case; the position paper groups facts with chronicles. Until chronolex v0.2.0 the cell gave an application a `zaakkenmerk` that every gram after it carried. Now each gram has an id and refers by id to the gram it belongs to (RFC-045 §3), and a case is a root with everything that leads to it. The paper says nothing about the identity of a gram; the id and the references are our own, and the creator of Chronolexografie agreed to them on 29-09-2026.

15. **A lexostatus may be a list.** RFC-022 §4.1 has a lexostatus answer with parameters. `group_by` and `without` let a lexostatus return one row per case instead. RFC-022 §4.1 sketches `group_by zaakkenmerk` in a comment, which is `group_by: root` here; the list shape and the rule that a list never reaches the engine are filled in here.
16. **Channels and roles in a process, with simulated logins.** RFC-022 leaves authorization to the security context (§2). Here `channels` and `roles` in `process.yaml` decide who may act and through which route group, with simulated logins that check only the form of what is entered (a pattern, optionally the check digit test), and no security context. The route groups are fixed by the runtime; the roles that may use them are configuration. Since RFC-047 the channels and their roles are declared in the actor's policy and only their technique is in `channels.yaml`. A certified login (eHerkenning, DigiD) is out of scope for the proof of concept.
17. **Judgments in the decretogram, the course of the case as grams.** The judgments of the case handler (careful preparation, proportionality, reasoning, the date of the decision) exist only at the moment of deciding and will be accepted input of the decretogram (RFC-013 `accepted_values`). Facts from the course of the case, such as a request to supplement, are grams of their own that follow the case, and the decision reads them through a reduction. When there is no such gram, the reduction reads it as "did not happen" (`no_gram`, deviation 9).
18. **The state at decision comes from the procedure, not from `execute_stage`.** An article can ask for facts about the notification of the decision, which only arise after it. The runtime reads the RFC-008 procedure of the legal character and passes the parameters of later stages as `false` or `null`, with provenance `state_at_decision` and the stage. It does not run the decision through `execute_stage`: the engine evaluates the whole article in each stage and only gates a stage on its `requires`, so the article still needs a value for the notification facts at stage `BESLUIT`. The rule "a later stage has not happened yet" belongs to the runtime; which facts belong to which stage belongs to the Awb.
19. **`stage` on a gram.** A stage decretogram carries its RFC-008 stage (such as `BESLUIT`), taken from the stream. So does a submission that opens a case: the application gram carries `stage: AANVRAAG`, because it is the recorded fact behind that stage (RFC-008: "Belanghebbende dient aanvraag in (Awb 4:1)", which requires `aanvraag_datum`; the reduction takes that date from `effective_at`). The schemas allow a stage only on a decretogram, a submission or an `act` (the notification is an `act` with stage `BEKENDMAKING`), and only on an event with a case. RFC-022 §1.2 names the stages but gives the chronicle stream no field for them. A filter on `stage: BESLUIT`, such as the work queue's `without`, does not match the application.
20. **Types of the grams in the course of a case.** An invitation to supplement, a suspension of the decision period or a notification is recorded as an `act`: a factual act of the authority that is neither a decision nor a delivery. A supplement from the applicant is a `submission` of subtype `aanvulling`, and a payment an executogram. The position paper does not classify procedural acts, and `act` is a type of our own next to `submission` (open question 1). The fictitious fixtures in this repository still record some of these as executograms; the type is open vocabulary, and only `executogram` (the test on the legal ground) and `decretogram` (the fields of a decision) change what the runtime does.
21. **Synthesis per row** (`rows`). RFC-022 §4.1 has synthesis take a lexostatus as one answer. An article that asks for a table the applicant only partly fills needs the source queried once per row, with values from that row as input. The row shape, the column mapping and the order of the sources are configuration here; nothing of it is in the RFC.
22. **A lexostatus that supplies only columns.** `derivations` may be empty when `extra_fields` supplies something. Such a lexostatus answers with no parameter of any article: its values are columns of a table parameter the consumer assembles. RFC-022 §4.1 has a lexostatus answer with the parameters of an article.
23. **An input from the law on the way to a source** (`{regulation, output}` in `rows`). The reference date of a figure is a rule of the consuming article (Wpp art. 106 paragraph 1a), so the process asks that article for it. Until 25-09-2026 the configuration converted a year into a date (`als: eerste_dag_van_het_jaar`); the alternative, having the source cell hold that rule, would put one authority's law in another authority's register. The engine evaluates the whole article for one outcome, so the article's other parameters must be `required: false` for this run to succeed.
24. **What a recorded act carries.** Every act the engine computed adds `inputs`, `receipt` and `acting_actor` to the chronicle-stream shape of RFC-022 §1.3, which has none of them, and a decretogram also `legal_character`, `decision_type`, `regulation`, `regulation_valid_from` and `competent_authority`. `inputs` is the RFC-013 `accepted_values` idea applied to every parameter rather than to cross-organisational ones only, and the `receipt` is a short form of the RFC-013 Execution Receipt: the loaded regulations and the cell's streams with one hash over both, which is the generalisation RFC-022 §1.3 announces as an amendment to RFC-013.
25. **The competent authority is tested before recording.** RFC-002 and RFC-007 model who is competent; nothing says a process has to compare that with the authority it acts for before it has a decision recorded. Here `on_behalf_of` names that authority, literally as the law does, and a start-up check makes sure a loaded regulation names it. Equal records, a mandate (`mandates`, Awb 10:1) records in the name of the mandating authority, anything else refuses, and absent records with a warning. RFC-022 §2 names the three axes (recording actor, competent authority, acting actor) but not how a process links them; `on_behalf_of`, `mandates` and `acting_actor` on the gram are our own choice. Until 25-09-2026 the runtime compared the process's `actor` with the authority after normalizing case and punctuation, which made a mandate impossible to express. Since RFC-047 the authority is that of the policy that declares the channels, and `mandates` is in that policy.
26. **One gram per stage per decision, enforced by the cell.** A second gram with the same stage in the same decision is refused with 409, under the same lock as the write. This follows RFC-022 §1.2 (each stage of one besluit is its own elementary stage decretogram); it is not configuration. Until 25-09-2026 the scope was the case, so a case could hold one decision; deviation 47 describes the decisions in a case.
27. **Deriving what can be applied for.** The position paper and RFC-022 say nothing about which acts an actor may perform, or about applications at all. The process does not list them either: a policy regulation of the actor states what each portal offers and on what grounds, and the portal runs that regulation on an empty draft. This is informing by the actor, in the paper's sense, and records nothing. The offer tests only conditions that are settled beforehand, and an unknown outcome is no offer. An earlier version read "unknown because the application still has to supply facts" as "possible"; it ran the decision article on an empty draft, which kept the verdict at "possible" for an organisation that would almost certainly not qualify (a merger route open to any registered association). Running a law for a draft that does not exist yet is not tenable, so the offer now leans on the login and the registers alone, and the start-up check enforces it.
28. **The portal refuses nothing and offers what the policy allows.** The position paper is silent on refusing a recording. The general administrative law is not: an application is a request for a decision (Awb 1:3 paragraph 3), not treating it is a decision after receipt (Awb 4:5), and refusing an electronic message beforehand is limited to two grounds (Awb 2:15). So the portal refuses nothing it receives. What it offers follows the policy regulation of the actor, which is a choice about service and not a decision on the application.
29. **Passing values between sources.** RFC-022 §4.1 has each source answer from inputs the consumer already holds. Here a source can pass an extra field to a later source, so a register keyed on an organisation number can supply the name under which another register keeps the results.
30. **Traces in the answers.** The check, the trial decision and the possibilities return the engine trace of their run. RFC-013 puts a trace in the Execution Receipt; here it is part of the answer and is not recorded.
31. **A table from a register.** The derivation `collect` turns the grams that pass a filter into a list with one row per gram, and a source can pass that list on as an extra field. Synthesis per row then takes its rows from the source rather than from the application, so a decision can build a table from what a register decided (the rows of an election result) instead of from what the applicant stated. The position paper has reductions produce a lexostatus; a list as an extra field is a shape it does not describe.
32. **A trial reduction as a route of the cell.** The check before submitting needs the lexostatus of a draft that is not a fact. The position paper has reduction happen only in the cell, so the process does not reduce: it asks the cell (`POST .../lexostatus/<name>/trial`), and the cell builds the gram in memory, reduces its chronicle with that gram added, and records nothing. Neither the paper nor RFC-022 has a reduction over something that was not recorded: this reduces a non-fact. A cleaner reading is that the check is concluding in the process; RFC-044 keeps this open (Open Question 4).
33. **The process passes the intake without a security context.** The process tells the cell who submitted and through which channel (`intake`), and which actor asks to record. The cell refuses an actor that is not the stream's `recording_actor`, and otherwise trusts what the process says. Between process and cell there is no signing and no authorization of the actor, as between cells (deviation 10); only the runtime token of deviation 37 says that the request comes from a process of this runtime. Whether an applicant follows up only on a case they own (`owner` of their channel) is decided by the process on what the cell derives: the `owner` of the `case_state` (deviation 46).
34. **Who supplies a parameter (`origin`, `origins`).** The position paper speaks of synthesis at the consumer and does not say how a process knows which source supplies which fact. `origin` on a parameter and `origins` in implementing policy are a RegelRecht addition (RFC-048, draft), not part of the paper. The process configuration still names the parameters each synthesis source supplies; finding sources through their declaration alone is a later step.
35. **A missing supplier is a warning when the parameter is `required: false`; a wrong one never is.** RFC-048 now says the same: a parameter declared `required: false` that nobody supplies reaches the engine as unknown (RFC-036) and gives a warning, while a supplier that does not fit the origin is an error in every case. An earlier version of this runtime also let `required: false` turn a wrong source into a warning, so a register fact could start from the application; that is gone. What stays a deviation is the classification of the own cell's derivations by gram type (`submission` against the actor's other grams), which RFC-048 describes but the position paper does not.
36. **The periods on offer are policy.** Which values the portal offers for the period is an outcome of the actor's policy (`offer.windows`), computed on today's date. The runtime assumes no granularity: a period is whatever value the policy lists, and `offer.start` gives its first day. In the case corpus a period is a year (a number); in the fictitious allowance fixture it is a month, written as its first day (a date, RFC-001 `temporal.period_type: month`), which a cell derives from a date with `period_of`.
37. **Only the runtime's own processes record.** RFC-022 leaves who may record to the security context (§2), which this proof of concept does not have. Without anything, every client that reaches the port (the runtime binds to `0.0.0.0`) could put a gram into a chronicle with any actor and intake. The runtime therefore makes a random token at every start, known only in its own memory; the internal transport sends it, and the cell refuses `POST .../grams` and `POST .../lexostatus/<name>/trial` without it. An HTTP transport carries the token only when it is given one explicitly (the runtime does not do so anywhere yet, because a process records only in a cell of its own runtime), and the runtime never gives it to a transport to another runtime. Reading needs the token too, or the read token of `CELL_READ_TOKEN` that runtimes which may read each other share, because a gram carries the identity and the intake of whoever submitted it; until 25-09-2026 anyone on the port could read them. The frontend reads a chronicle and the lexostatuses through a process, for a role with route group `handling` (`/api/inspection`), and an applicant sees only their own submission. This is not authorization between organizations, and it does not make the processes safer: whoever reaches the port can still submit an application or take a decision through a process's simulated logins, and the process then records it.
38. **Two times per gram, and a peil on the reduction.** The position paper separates the moment of recording from the moment the fact concerns ("Op 3 april heeft de gemeente-ambtenaar vastgesteld dat ... per 2 april") and promises "time travel". RFC-022 §1.3 binds one timestamp from the submission (`received_at: $external.received_at`, "at which moment the fact occurred"), and §4.1 has the reduction order on "the made-it-fact timestamp". Here a gram carries both: `recorded_at`, always the cell's clock, stamped under the write lock, and `effective_at`, the legal moment. The stream binds `effective_at` to a submitted value per event, always with a legal ground: to `$external` where the acting actor states it as part of the act (the date of a decision, of a notification under Awb 3:41, of a payment or a statement), to `$intake` where the submitter may not choose it (the date stamp of the counter). Either way the cell refuses a moment after the recording and, for a gram that refers to another, a day before the gram it refers to. The case handler can therefore record an act later than it happened, within those bounds. The reduction orders on `effective_at` first and on `recorded_at` on a tie, because a late-recorded fact about an earlier moment is not the latest state; that deviates from RFC-022 §4.1. The peil (`as_of` and `known_at`) and its bitemporal semantics are our own choice; neither the paper nor RFC-022 specifies how a consumer asks for an earlier state. The two times were added to chronolex `v0.1.0` without a version bump, since that version was never released. The schema is now `v0.3.0`, and a chronicle from before v0.2.0 is refused rather than read (see "Four layers").
39. **The counter enters an application on behalf of the applicant.** RFC-022 and the position paper do not say how a fact that arrived outside the portal reaches a cell. Here a role with route group `counter` has the same event recorded, with the applicant identified by the fields of a portal channel, the employee under `entered_by`, and the day of receipt as `effective_at`. The applicant never chose that day and never logged in; the gram says so with `$intake.channel: counter`. A receipt before the opening of the period is refused only when the offer's policy names one (`offer.opening`); that is our own choice, not the law's.

40. **Acts in a case instead of one decision.** RFC-022 and the position paper speak of a process that concludes and has a cell record; they do not say which acts a process performs. Here `actions` lists them, and the event decides their kind: the decision, a follow-up stage of it (RFC-008), or a fact of the course of the case. What an act needs follows from the stage and the `origin` of its parameters, the direction of "alternative B" in the review; which acts there are and which outcomes they show stays configuration. Since RFC-047 that too follows from the streams and the law (see "Acts in a case").
41. **A follow-up stage runs through `execute_stage`, the decision itself does not.** Deviation 18 still holds for the decision: it runs the article with what has not happened set to `false` or `null`. A later stage, such as the notification, runs through the engine's `execute_stage` on the recorded decision's inputs and outcomes, and fires the hooks of that stage. The decision gram is the state container of RFC-008; the zaak around it remains deviation 14.
42. **The legal protection route comes from a hook, recorded at notification.** RFC-022 §3.3 has the route derived from the procedure and requires integrations to carry it. Here the hook of Awb 6:8 on stage `BEKENDMAKING` computes its dates, the notification gram records them, and the case view derives the running stage (`BEZWAAR`) from the procedure. The route's field names are those of the law (the outputs of the hook), not a fixed `bezwaar_route`; objection itself is not modelled.
43. **A conclusion before acting, never a refusal of what happened.** The position paper defines an executogram as the recording of a delivery that took place, and asks agreements on recordability to demand form without restricting content (as a research question). Here the cell enforces only form: the schema, the actor, the case, one stage per decision and the bounds of `effective_at`. Content is a conclusion of the process, drawn before it acts. For an executogram, a `TOETS` outcome whose `legal_basis` is the ground of its event must hold for the process to deliver of itself (Awb 4:52: not more than the decided amount, and not before the decision is in force); a hook that gives no value, such as the objection period after a notification that does not meet Awb 3:41, holds back a follow-up stage the same way. When the case handler reports that the fact happened anyway (`happened: true`), the cell records it and the lexostatuses show the consequence: an amount paid without being owed, or no objection period. Until 25-09-2026 the process refused to have such a fact recorded. Limiting the test to executograms, and matching on `legal_basis`, is our own choice: a finding or a judgment on a provision (such as default under a deadline) records what the provision concludes.
44. **The case must not have changed.** The cell refuses a gram when the number of grams in its case differs from what the process read (`root_grams`). This is optimistic concurrency, our own choice; it keeps a conclusion of the process tied to the facts it was drawn from.
45. **The lexogram is the consolidated regulation.** The position paper defines a lexogram as the recording of a (possible, future) change in legislation, made in a cell like any other chronolexogram. Layer 1 above, following RFC-022 §1.1, is the consolidated regulation YAML: a version of the text, in no cell, that belongs to no one. The change itself, recorded by whoever enacts it, is not modelled, and a reduction cannot ask which change made a provision apply (RFC-044, Open Question 2).
46. **The state of a case is a lexostatus the runtime offers.** The paper puts filtering and deriving (reduction) in the cell. Until 25-09-2026 the process counted the grams of a case per event and stage, took the recorded decision from its gram, read the hook outcomes from the gram of the last stage and checked ownership over the fields of a case's grams. Now the cell derives all of it in the lexostatus `case_state` (see "The state of a case"), and the process reads only lexostatuses; the grams it shows a case handler are the file, not input. That the runtime offers this lexostatus rather than each cell declaring it is our own choice: the case is a concept of the runtime (deviation 14), and the paper does not say how a process learns the state of a case.
47. **A case holds several decisions, each a gram of its own.** RFC-022 §1.2 has the stage grams of one besluit share a `zaakkenmerk`, and RFC-008 makes the besluit the state container. A monthly allowance has an advance, a final determination and a recovery in one case: three besluiten, each with its own stages, notification and objection period. Here each decision is a gram with stage `BESLUIT`, and a gram that follows it refers to it by id: `decision` for a notification or a payment, which is RFC-022 §3.3's `references_decision`, and `amends` for a change, the §3.1 `is_wijziging_van` backlink. Until chronolex v0.2.0 the cell gave each decision a `besluitkenmerk` (`<zaakkenmerk>/<n>`) instead. Whether an event opens, follows or changes a decision follows from its stage and its references; which decision an act acts on follows from the article for a follow-up and from the reference of its event for a fact or a change. That a second decision of the same event on the same gram is refused, and a change needs its own event with its own ground (Awb 4:48 and 4:49; RFC-008 Open Question 5 makes a withdrawal a nested decision), is our own reading: the cell cannot know an article, and the event is where the registration names the kind of decision. A lexostatus of the case can filter on a reference like on any other field of the gram itself, so with a notification event shared by several decisions it can select the notification of one decision (`refers_to.decision: $decision`, with the decision's id as an input). The fictitious fixture `test_toeslag` (its policy under `packages/cel/tests/fixtures/regulation/testbeleid_toeslag/`) runs such a case through the same runtime and frontend as the other process.

## Open questions

1. **Typology.** Are `submission` (what an applicant or third party submits) and `act` (a factual act of an authority, such as a request to supplement or a notification) classes of their own next to lexogram, decretogram and executogram? The position paper leaves this for further research, and so does RFC-022 §1. RFC-044 §3 uses both as open vocabulary without closing the list.
2. **RFC-008.** The submission gram is the recorded fact behind the `AANVRAAG` stage, and carries it (deviation 19). A request to supplement and the supplement (Awb 4:5) are now grams of their own in the case corpus, without a stage. Open: the acknowledgement of receipt (Awb 4:3a) has neither a gram nor a stage.
3. **Correction and supplement.** A supplement is a new gram that refers to the application (`application`, Awb 4:5). Does the reduction then take `latest`, or does it merge field by field?
4. **Whose cell.** Only the receiving authority records the submission now. Does it also belong in a cell of the applicant, and how do the two grams relate?
5. **`legal_basis` as a list.** A submission rests on several articles at once, where RFC-022 §1.3 has one `grondslag`.
6. **The format of the reduction.** RFC-022 §4.1 calls `lexostatus_definitions` provisional. Does the derivation vocabulary (`field`, `filled`, `equals`, `table` and the rest) belong in an RFC?
7. **Value or `bevat_*`.** Regulations model the content of an application as booleans ("the application contains ..."). Should an article ask for the value itself as a parameter, so a decision can use it?
8. **Awb 4:2 paragraph 1.** The fixed core (name, address, date, requested decision, signature) is not machine-readable in the corpus, so `core` is not reduced. Should paragraph 1 get a `TOETS`?
9. **Signature.** Does the login method count as a signature (Awb 2:16)? And how does `signed_via` relate to signing the gram itself (RFC-009)?
10. **Prefilling.** If data is later prefilled from registers, how do you record that the applicant saw and confirmed it, without keeping a shadow copy of the source?
11. **Engine and single outcomes.** The engine evaluates the whole article when one outcome is asked for. Every action runs and every `input` is resolved, including cross-law inputs the requested outcome never reads. A required parameter that only another output uses is therefore needed as well. The engine stops at the first missing value (`Variable not found`); a `required: false` parameter that is not passed becomes an unknown value and is reported as missing. For the check before submitting, this means an outcome can only be judged when every fact the article touches is present, including facts of the authority itself. The cell does not fill anything in. It reports "niet te beoordelen: mist <parameter>", one missing value per run. A test (`engine_requires_the_whole_article_for_an_output` in `packages/cel/src/assessment.rs`) pins this behavior on the fixtures. An engine that resolves only what the requested outcome depends on would let the check answer from the application alone. The same limit pushes corpora towards `required: false` on facts that are in fact always supplied, such as register facts, only so that a caller that needs one outcome of the article does not fail on the rest. The start-up checks no longer let that hide a wrong source. The smaller workaround is for such a caller to pass the required facts on, even those its outcome never reads, and to say so in their description.
12. **Schema version.** `schema/chronolex/v0.3.0/` stands apart from `schema/v0.8.0/`. Merge them, or keep them separate?
13. **Claim in the submission, fact in the decision.** A regulation can require the application to *state* a fact that belongs to another cell, such as the number of seats a list received, while the calculation in the decision uses the fact itself ("per seat that *was allocated*"). The submission gram records the claim. The decision should take the fact from the source cell through synthesis and compare the two. Where is that comparison recorded, and what follows from a mismatch: a request to supplement the application under the general administrative law's completion procedure, or a decision that simply follows the source?
14. **Proposed decisions.** Drafts before the decision (a *voorgenomen besluit*) are out of scope. The position paper asks whether they are decisions already.
15. **A column with no source.** A table parameter can have a column no cell can answer for, because no register holds the fact under that shape. The row then simply lacks the column, which the engine reports as a missing value only when it reads it. Should the configuration be able to name such a column and say why it stays empty, the way `not_reduced` does for an unread field?
16. **The receipt and the source cells.** The receipt covers what the process loaded (its regulations) and the streams of the cell that records the decision. What a source cell answered is in `inputs` with its provenance, but not with that cell's own receipt. RFC-013 §4 sketches the chain; a cell that signs its lexostatus answers would close it.
17. **`origins` and older schema versions.** Law schema v0.8.0 declares `origin` and `origins` (RFC-048, Implementation Notes); no earlier version is changed (RFC-013: `$schema` URLs are tag-based). On those older versions `origin` on a parameter validates without being checked, because a parameter field is open, and `machine_readable` is closed, so an override in implementing policy fails schema validation. A corpus that declares v0.5.9, such as NAPP, therefore fails until it moves to v0.8.0. The runtime checks the form at load time either way and names the file and the parameter.
18. **The date of the decision.** Which origin does the date on which the body decides have? It is given when deciding, like a judgment, but it is a fact rather than an assessment, and a rule that classifies by the article a parameter is passed to can make it `DOSSIER`. The case corpus now declares it `OORDEEL`, as an exception to that rule, so the decision form asks for it; the principle is still open.
